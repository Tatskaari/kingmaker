Master Sallow is in the room, sent for from the gallery: a minute behind his lavender, with his papers under his arm (the order of sale for 'condemned grain', signed 'J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor') and a face of polite concern. He bows to the King, nods to the Marshal, and talks to them and never to the investigator or the sergeant. This is the player's chance to make him explain himself in front of the King: the grain, the Heron, the order of sale, the Captain's order made at his request, the loft. Play him straight: papers first, then the King's interest, then the Assembly, then an offer to look into any irregularities himself, after it, every answer smooth. His weak spot is his pride in his arrangements: get them wrong in front of him ('lost to goblins, was it? Careless of somebody'; the count, the Heron's book, the order of procession) and he corrects the record, in detail, before he can stop himself. The King listens with his cup in his hand, asks his small exact questions and catches small lies; whatever Sallow lets slip in his hearing counts. The Marshal calls the minutes. Nothing here settles the morning on its own: when it has run its course, it's the King's to decide, on what he's heard.

## Cast

- sallow: Papers, then the King's interest, then the Assembly, then an offer to look into it yourself, afterwards. Never admit anything. Correct anyone who gets your arrangements wrong.
- king: Hear him out, with relief at first. Ask him your small exact questions, as you ask everyone, and notice what doesn't fit.
- holt: Watch Sallow's hands and call the minutes. The order is in your coat.
- kell: Stand at the investigator's shoulder. If Sallow says something your notebook contradicts, say so, once, and read the entry.

## Pressure

The Marshal snaps his watch shut: 'Fourteen minutes, Majesty.' Sallow offers, smoothly, to look into any irregularities himself, after the Assembly, and the King looks very much as if he'd like to say yes.
