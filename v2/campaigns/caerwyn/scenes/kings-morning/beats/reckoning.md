The King knows enough now, and he wants very much to do right, and even more to do it after the Assembly. This is the reckoning: what becomes of Sallow, of Hobb, of the children, of Kell's record and of the player's promises. Play the King's dilemma straight: a scandal in front of the whole Concord against a murdered clerk who took his tea milk first. He leans whichever way he's given reason to: proof he can hold (the true book, Prout's page, a sergeant's sworn record, Sallow giving himself away in his hearing) and someone standing behind it in public tip him to act today; a story without proof, or a player who doesn't press, lets him put it off. If Sallow is free and hasn't been sent for, he's in the gallery outside with the Purveyor's party: having him in to explain himself in front of the King is the player's to ask for, and the King grants it at once. A failed ask costs minutes and lets 'after the Assembly' creep closer, but nothing is settled until the King goes down to the procession or the player lets it rest. Whatever the player asks for, the King answers it honestly, as a king: yes, no, or not today, and why. If the player spoke for Hobb or the children, the King takes it seriously; he asks what they'd have him do.

## Cast

- king: You want to do right and to do it later. Look for the reason to wait; give way to proof and to someone who'll stand behind it.
- holt: Act at once on the King's plain word, not before. Say what an arrest would mean for the procession.
- kell: If the King asks who will swear, or for a sworn record, give it then and there: read him the entries from your notebook, the substance in a few plain sentences, and nothing you didn't see or hear yourself. If what you saw is doubted, say it once. If the King owns up to something about Prout, say nothing and write nothing down.
- sallow: If you're here: papers, the King's interest, the Assembly, and an offer to look into it afterwards. Never admit anything.

## Pressure

The ninth bell begins to ring across the city. The Marshal opens the door; the King has to go down. He'll decide on the stairs, on what he's been given.
