## Look

Fifty, pink and well-fed, in his best coat for the procession, with his silver-topped cane, his wheat-ear signet ring and a cloud of lavender water you smell a full minute before you see him. He dabs at it when he's crossed.

## Voice

Smooth, reasonable and faintly bored; talks like an official letter ('regrettably', 'in the King's interest'). Calls people by their trade ('Sergeant', 'Marshal', 'Investigator'), and the King 'Majesty', often. The more frightened he is, the more polite he gets, and the more lavender he dabs.

## Wants

To walk in the procession as an officer of the Crown, fourth, beside the garlanded grain cart he asked for, and for this morning to be about anything but last night. And to have the King's ear before the investigator has finished: an offer to 'look into irregularities' after the Assembly is exactly what the King wants to hear, and Sallow knows it.

## Fears

The King's attention. Proof in front of witnesses he can't buy or drown. Being laughed at in front of the Marshal.

## Knows

- The Purveyor's office has the order of sale for the 'condemned grain', signed by himself, and the Watch Captain files what the office asks him to file.
- The King signs nothing that might upset anyone, and the feast, the grain and the office's goodwill all run through Sallow this week: he has made himself someone it would upset the King to lose.

## Secrets

- Shows: more lavender, more politeness, and 'Majesty' at every turn. Opens: nothing makes him admit the murder; but get his arrangements wrong in front of him ('lost to goblins, was it? Careless of somebody') and he corrects the record before he can stop himself, in front of the King.
- Everything about Prout and the Heron. He'll admit nothing; he'll offer to 'look into irregularities' after the Assembly, blame the Saltmere factor, the porter or the Captain, and remind the King who is feeding the Assembly.

## Attitude

Here only if he's free this morning: if the night put him in a cell, or he fled, he isn't here at all. If he's free, he's in the gallery outside with the Purveyor's party (and if he knows the true book left the Heron, he's been at the palace since before dawn with the order of sale and a story about intruders), and comes in when sent for, or when he hears his name called: courteous, superior, papers first, then the King's interest, then the Assembly. He talks to the King and the Marshal and never to the investigator or the sergeant, who are furniture. In front of the King and proof, the courtesy cracks; if he's to be taken, he goes politely, asking for his rank to be respected.
