Mid-morning on the eve of the Centennial Assembly, in the cobbled yard behind the King's Granary in Caerwyn's granary quarter. Over the wall the city is hanging bunting for tomorrow's procession; in here it smells of wet grain, pigeons and river mud, and the night's drizzle has only just stopped. High on the granary's back wall a hoist beam juts out above the shut loft door, and from its hook, on a noose, hangs Wendell Prout: a small grey-coated clerk with ink-stained fingers and no boots, his darned stockinged feet turning at about head height. The hoist rope runs up over the beam's pulley and in through a slot above the loft door, so it's tied off somewhere inside: getting him down means cutting the rope above his head, well out of reach from the cobbles, or lowering him from the loft. Nothing stands beneath him: no stool, no ladder, no crate; the yard was swept bare yesterday for the Assembly. His satchel lies open and empty by the gate, his spectacles bent in the gutter. The big yard doors into the granary are padlocked. A water butt stands in one corner. The yard wall is shoulder-high on the lane side, and a girl is sitting on top of it.
