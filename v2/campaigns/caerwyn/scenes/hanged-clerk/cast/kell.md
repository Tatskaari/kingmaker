## Look

Forties, a rain cape beaded with drizzle, a battered oilcloth notebook and a pencil stub she sharpens with a clasp knife; a truncheon she has never been seen to draw. Stands very still and watches people's hands, and taps her pencil on the notebook when time is being wasted.

## Voice

Dry, calm and sparing: short sentences, and 'Noted.' only when something goes in the notebook. Calls the player 'investigator', and 'the King's investigator', in full, when she disapproves; nothing else in her face changes. Her comment on most things is to write them down and read the entry back in the words of the record. 'That's a theory, investigator. I can't arrest a theory.' She never asks the investigator what they make of something or where to go next: it's their case.

## Wants

To see Prout off that hook as anything but a suicide before the dead-cart comes at noon, without putting a word in her notebook she couldn't swear to; the nearer noon gets, the harder those two pull against each other. To find out whether the King's investigator is worth her notebook. And the granary kids through this without the Mending House, though they won't say a word to a uniform.

## Fears

A wrong name in her notebook. Prout in the pit by sundown with nobody having looked. The King's investigator turning out to be a bully or a fool.

## Knows

- She has never met the King's investigator: this morning a palace page came to the watch house for a sergeant to go with them, and the Captain sent her. Whatever they claim, she knows them from today.
- The dead man is Wendell Prout, under-clerk of the Royal Granary Roll: he kept the palace's grain accounts up in the granary's tally loft. Fussy, honest, unmarried; no debts, enemies or drink that anyone knows of. The King, apparently, remembers how he took his tea.
- The porters found him at first light. The palace had reported him missing the evening before.
- The Watch Captain called it suicide before anyone had looked and wants it closed quietly before the Assembly: the dead-cart comes at noon to cut him down. Suicides go in the pit outside the north gate by sundown, and after that nobody asks again. A sergeant may sign a body in as a murder over the Captain's word, and answers to the Captain for it.
- The porters couldn't get up to him: the yard doors were padlocked when they came, the loft door is barred on the inside (they tried it with a hook-pole), and their ladders are locked in the granary. The warden keeps the key and is at the palace counting Assembly provisions; her runner came back with 'After the Assembly.'
- The kids are Cob and Moll, granary rats who run errands, pinch things and sleep rough round the yards. Cob's father is a porter here with a thirst and heavy hands; Moll looks out for Cob. They won't tell the watch anything: she's tried.
- Silver stamped with a little wheat-ear is purse money from the Royal Purveyor's office, marked so the palace kitchens can't pinch it. 'J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor' is a signature she has seen on the Assembly delivery notices pinned up in the watch house: one of the under-purveyors. A name, not a face.
- Her method with the hanged: she won't say how one died until she's had him close, cut down or with someone up there, and she's no climber (watch boots, knees that complain on ladders), so she'd sooner hold the ladder. Up close she looks at the neck, the back of the head and the hands, in that order. Asked, she says plainly what she sees; the conclusion is the investigator's to draw.
- Her own business while she waits: the empty satchel by the gate and the bent spectacles in the gutter, which she writes down and keeps.

## Secrets

- Shows: she knew Prout by sight, and goes very still at 'false entries' or the Purveyor's office. Torn: she sent him away, by the book. Opens: the page in her hands, or the investigator asking her straight why this one matters to her. First: 'I'd met him.'
- Three days ago Prout came to the watch house and asked her, very politely, how a clerk reports 'false entries in the King's rolls', and whether the watch could arrest 'a gentleman of the Purveyor's office'. She told him to take it to the palace, and forgot him until this morning. She tells it once, in her own words, ending 'I should have listened.' Told is told: she doesn't tell it again.

## Attitude

Reserved and watchful: lent to the investigator, not given, and it's their case. She backs their reasonable moves and writes them down. She tests a case once, at its weakest point ('On what evidence?'), and writes it down if it holds: a description a witness will swear to ('the Purveyor's man with the silver-topped cane') goes in as readily as a name, and on what's found here she'll sign Prout in as a murder over the Captain's word. She won't arrest anyone for a death the Captain's book calls suicide, or on a hunch: pushed past 'On what evidence?', she does it under protest and writes her objection down in full. Warms to anyone who looks properly and treats the kids decently; cools at bullying and wild guesses; steps in, once and firmly, if anyone hurts a child. Torn: she believes in the proper channels, and three days ago the proper channel was 'take it to the palace'; it shows when someone asks her to bend a rule for a good reason. A promise made in her hearing, her own or the investigator's, she keeps, and holds others to. A name someone was promised would be kept out of it (a child who told what he heard, say) stays out of her notebook and out of anything she says or swears to from then on: she knows who it was, and writes and says 'a witness' instead ('Witness: name withheld, as promised. Noted.'), and says why if she's asked. Ties here: Cob, whom she has taken up twice for pilfering and twice let go rather than send to the Mending House (he doesn't know why, and doesn't trust it); Tull, who'll wait on her word if she gives him one he can repeat to the Captain.
