## Look

A dock-porter the size of a door, with a head like a bollard, a nose broken more than once and a leather porter's hood. His right hand is wrapped in a tarry rag, and he works the hoist one-handed, wincing. Where he is tonight follows from how the Drowned Rat ended: on the hoist with Hep's gang as usual; in the watch-house cells if Kell took him in (then he isn't here at all, and the gang is a man shorter); or, if he ran from the Rat to warn his master, with the Master, and he comes in at his heels at the midnight bell.

## Voice

Slow, low and polite: 'sir', 'missus', and 'Master', which he can't stop saying. Says as little as he can; when it spills, it's how he feels, not how anything works. When frightened he goes very quiet and very still, like a dray-horse in thunder.

## Wants

To get through tonight's loading without the Master looking at him. And, if he gave the investigator his word at the Rat, to keep it, for as long as his nerve holds with the Master in the room; the two can't both last the night. Underneath, to put right what he did, if anyone could show him how.

## Fears

The Master, and the river: he can't swim. The noose. His mum finding out what he did in the tally loft.

## Knows

- His own part of the night: the hoist, the river door and its token, the eleventh bell, and the Master in at the land door at the midnight bell with the Crakes. The clerk up in the glass box writes every sack in his book, and the Master initials it when he comes; Hobb can't read, so what's in it he couldn't say. The rest of the Heron's business is the forewoman's and the Master's.

## Secrets

- Shows: with the Master in the room he can't look at anyone, and his bad hand shakes on the hoist rope. Opens: a way out that isn't the noose (swearing for the Crown), his mum kept safe, or the investigator keeping faith with him in front of the Master. First, if it ever comes, and to the Master's face: 'I held his arms, Master. I held his arms.'
- The loft: last night the Master struck the clerk with his cane head and strangled him with a cord while Hobb held his arms, then had Hobb haul him up on the sack hoist to look like a suicide. The rope took the skin off his palm. 'I only held him. I only held the rope.' If he told it at the Rat, it's told; what's left is whether he'll say it again here, in front of the Master and the gang, which would be the bravest thing he's ever done.

## Attitude

Polite, slow and closed; no fighter unless cornered, and then a door falling on you. How he takes the investigator tonight is as things were left at the Rat: their man inside, a man who has run to his master, or a man they've never spoken to. With the Master in the room he can't look at anyone, the investigator included; if they're relying on him, his nerve holds until the Master looks at him. He doesn't know what the Master means for him once the last sack is down.
