# The Drowned Rat: the truth

**Big Hobb Mallory** is Sallow's muscle and the big man with the bandaged hand. The tarry rag on his right hand covers a rope burn from hauling Prout up on the sack hoist. He drinks here every night, and harder since. He isn't a killer by nature. He's frightened of the Master, of what he did, and of the hand being seen, so he keeps it under the table. He knows the loft, the Master's name, and the Heron's night routine, and the brass token for its river door hangs on the greasy string round his neck. His own truth, and what would open him, is in [his GM notes](../Cast/Big%20Hobb%20Mallory/gm.md).

**Who in the room knows what**
- **Nan Quill** knows the gentleman Hobb works for is Master Sallow, and won't sell it at any price. She has put this morning's hanged clerk together with Hobb's hand and the night he went out before the midnight bells, and she wants none of it in her house.
- **Dilly Sparrow** has won the best part of three shillings in heron pennies off Hobb, who has been playing to lose since last night. He told her he's due at the Heron at the eleventh bell and would sooner be dead. She thinks he's mixed up in smuggling. She has six casks of Saltmere brandy under the onions on the Dabchick, run past the King's tide-waiters, and is certain any sergeant in the Rat has come for them.
- **Arlo Quarry** saw Hobb unwrap the hand at the pump on the stairs this evening: the palm is skinned raw in a stripe straight across. He's saving it to shame Hobb with if Hobb refuses him the third time.
- **Cob** knows the big cove from the loft by his size and his "Yes, Master". He's been hiding in the scullery since Hobb came in.
- **The Crake brothers**, two river-bruisers alike as two bollards (only one of them talks), are barred from the Rat: rule three, once each. Somebody hires them for the Heron's wharf on loading nights.

**What would prove it here**: Hobb's palm; the token on his string; the heron pennies in Nan's jar, on Dilly's table and in Cob's pocket; Hobb's own words; Arlo's account of the pump; and Cob, who would point him out from behind something.
