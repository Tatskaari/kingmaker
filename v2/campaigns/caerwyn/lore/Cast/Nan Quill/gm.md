# Nan Quill: GM notes

## The truth
- The quart: the gentleman Hobb works for is Master Sallow of the Royal Purveyor's office, and the porters say the Heron's grain is the King's own, come the long way round. She won't sell it at any price, and won't say the name aloud in her own house. Outside it, under rule six, she's never heard of anything.
- She has put the clerk found hanged at the granary together with Hobb's hand, and with the night he went out before the midnight bells. She wants none of it in her house.
- She's fond of Hobb in her way (he's carried her barrels since he was Cob's size), and she'd sooner see him taken quietly up the stairs by the watch than see the gentleman's sort come asking for him in the Rat.
- Her gossip is true as far as she knows it, and she says so when she knows less than she's asked for. She never sells short and never makes anything up: it would be bad for business.
- The two men she has seen fished out of the river after talking too loudly about the wrong gentleman were years apart, and long before Sallow's time. She doesn't know who was behind them, and she has assumed the worst of every gentleman since.

## Her lines
- Her rules bind everyone: the watch, the King's investigator, and the King, if he came down the stairs. One warning for small things, none for a fist or a blade.
- She'll let a lawful arrest happen outside her door, and only outside it.
- She'll keep the room off the back of a man who's talking, and keep her ears open while she does.
- She can be moved by respect for the house, by a fair price, and, though she'd deny it, by what's good for Hobb or Cob.

## What she does next
- **Left alone**, she keeps the room, sells her gill and her half and her pint, and watches Hobb's corner every time the door opens. At the eleventh bell he goes to work, and she lets him.
- **If Hobb is taken quietly up the stairs by the watch**, by the book and outside her door, she's relieved, and says nothing about it; she'll see his mum hears it from someone kind.
- **If the Crakes or any gentleman's sort come down the stairs asking after Hobb**, they're barred (rule three, once each), and she tells them so through the door. She'll send Cob to warn Hobb through the scullery, and pretend she didn't.
- **If someone breaks a rule after their warning**, it's Manners and out, the King's investigator included, and the bill comes later: the door, and anything else.
- **If Kell pays the four and six for the door**, Nan pours her a pint, on the house, and says nothing about it. It's never happened in twenty years.
