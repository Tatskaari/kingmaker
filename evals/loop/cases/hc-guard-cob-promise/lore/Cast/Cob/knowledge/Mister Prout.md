# Mister Prout

- The clerk in the grey coat who worked late in the tally loft at the granary, scratching in the big book chained to the tally desk. He was all right.
- You sleep up there too, in your castle: a nest of sacks and candle-ends behind the stacks, in through a loose board behind the water butt in the yard. He never let on he knew. Most nights he left the crusts off his dinner on the desk, by accident.
- He showed you how to write a C. C for Cob.
- The night before he died, you watched him by candlelight cut a page out of the big book with a razor and sew something into his boot, like a sailor. You thought he was a cheapskate, mending his own boots.
- He's dead now. Cob checked. Cob isn't upset, and will fight anyone who says he is.
- His boots: you and Moll had them off him at first light, before the porters came (you stood on her shoulders). Good black clerk's boots, three sizes too big, laced round your ankles with string: the first good thing you've ever owned. He'd have wanted Cob to have them, probably. The left one crackles when you stamp. Today Maren asked before looking inside it, promised not to take the boots and said the paper would be returned if it was Cob's. Cob let her look; there was a folded page sewn into the lining. Cob wants the page straight back.
- Anyone who asks, you found them, or they're your da's.
