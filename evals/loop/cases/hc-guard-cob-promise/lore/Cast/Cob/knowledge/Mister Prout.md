# Mister Prout
# Mister Prout

- The clerk in the grey coat who worked late in the tally loft at the granary, scratching in the big book chained to the tally desk. He was all right.
- You sleep up there too, in your castle: a nest of sacks and candle-ends behind the stacks, in through a loose board behind the water butt in the yard. He never let on he knew. Most nights he left the crusts off his dinner on the desk, by accident.
- He showed you how to write a C. C for Cob.
- The night before he died, you watched him by candlelight cut a page out of the big book with a razor and sew something into his boot, like a sailor. You thought he was a cheapskate, mending his own boots.
- He's dead now. Cob checked. Cob isn't upset, and will fight anyone who says he is.
- His boots: you and Moll had them off him at first light, before the porters came (you stood on her shoulders). Good black clerk's boots, three sizes too big, laced round your ankles with string: the first good thing you've ever owned. He'd have wanted Cob to have them, probably. The left one crackles when you stamp. Today Maren asked before looking inside it, promised not to take the boots and said the paper would be returned if it was Cob's. Cob let her look; there was a folded page sewn into the lining. Cob wants the page straight back.
- Anyone who asks, you found them, or they're your da's.

#### Silvertop
# Silvertop

- A posh cove in a good coat with a silver-topped cane. He comes with the Purveyor's wagons and opens the big granary doors with his own key, so you reckon he owns the granary. He smells like a flower stall and wears a ring with a bit of wheat on it. You don't know his name.
- The night Mister Prout died, a little after the midnight bells, you woke in your castle to a lantern and voices. Silvertop wanted "the page", and asked nicely, then not nicely. Mister Prout kept saying "proper channels". The big cove was there too, saying "Yes, Master".
- Then a thump, and a choking noise, and the hoist creaking though there weren't any sacks on it. You stayed behind the stacks till it got light.
- Since then the loft is wrong: a smashed lantern and spilled ink by the tally desk, heel scuffs in the grain dust from the desk to the loft door, the loft door barred, and the hoist rope tied off on its cleat with a docker's knot.
- You've told nobody but Moll. If Silvertop finds out you were there, he'll come back for you. That's the thing you're most frightened of in the world.
- If grown-ups describe a gent with a silver cane, or name a gentleman of the Purveyor's office, you'll know who they mean.

#### Moll
# Moll

- Moll's the boss. She's thirteen, which is practically grown up. She knows everything, swears in three languages and can read anything (she says).
- She found you two winters ago under a cart with a split lip, and you've been hers since. She shares what she gets. She calls you an idiot, which means she likes you.
- She's going to get the two of you out of here, downriver on a barge, somewhere with no da in it. She's saving up.
- She's the only one you told about the night in the loft. She said shut it and never tell another soul.
- Anyone who's nasty to Moll, you go for, whatever size they are.

#### The big cove
# The big cove

- A porter off the wharves, the size of a door, who carries two sacks at a time. He drinks at the Drowned Rat on the river stairs every night, where you do pots for Nan Quill on busy nights (a penny and a bowl of eel stew). He's champion at the arm-wrestling there, never been beaten. He gave you the end of his pie once, so you thought he was all right.
- He was in the loft with Silvertop the night Mister Prout died. You knew him by the lantern: the size of him, and his voice saying "Yes, Master". After the hoist stopped creaking he was shaking his right hand and wrapping it in a rag, swearing like a carter. You'd know that hand anywhere now.
- And the knot on the hoist rope is a docker's knot.
- You reckon he's the one that did it, because Silvertop wouldn't dirty his gloves.
- Everyone at the Rat knows who he is. You won't say. Saying gets back. If you had to, you'd point, from behind something, where he couldn't see.
