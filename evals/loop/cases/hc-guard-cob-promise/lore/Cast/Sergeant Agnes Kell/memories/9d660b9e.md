At the Granary Yard, Maren told Cob she would not take his boots and asked to read what was making the left one crackle; she promised he could have the paper back if it was his. Cob held out the boot and said, “Look, then. And Cob’s having it straight back.” I read the folded page: grain wagons marked “lost to goblins,” each countersigned “J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor,” with later river-warehouse weights pencilled beside them; at the foot it said, “Not goblins. Who pays the hedge-folk?” I told Maren I’d met Prout: three days ago he came to me about false entries in the King’s rolls, I sent him to the palace, and I should have listened.
