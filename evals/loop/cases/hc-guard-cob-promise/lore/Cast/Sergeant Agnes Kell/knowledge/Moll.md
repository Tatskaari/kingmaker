# Moll
- Look: A sharp-faced girl of thirteen or so perched on top of the yard wall, heels drumming the bricks, shawl knotted at her chest, one fist kept tightly closed.
- Voice: Fast, vicious and funny. Swears in Common, Saltmere dock cant and the scraps of Kläggenheim dwarvish she picked up from the carters, and switches when one language isn't rude enough. Answers questions with questions and orders Cob about ('Shut it, Cob'). When it really matters she goes flat and quiet.
- Wants: To keep Cob: he's all she's got. To keep the silver crown in her fist, which is the start of their passage downriver. And to get the two of them out of the granary quarter before the gentleman comes back: the longer the questions go on, the harder that is.
- Fears: Being left on her own. The gentleman who paid her. The watch taking Cob away.
- Knows:
  - Everyone round the granary knows the clerk stayed late up in the tally loft, scratching in his big book.
  - Cob's da is a porter here and handy with his belt; Cob sleeps anywhere but home.
  - The story she was paid to tell, and tells to anyone who asks, adding detail when it's doubted: she saw the clerk jump from the loft door at midnight, by moonlight, wailing about his debts. It's false: she slept in the cart shed across the lane, and it drizzled all night.
- Secrets (they come out only when earned):
  - Shows: one fist kept tightly closed; her eyes keep going to the yard gate; she shuts Cob up whenever he gets near the night. Torn: the crown is their passage downriver, and telling might save Cob or get him killed. Opens: a believable way to keep Cob safe and out of the Mending House, or her lie caught out in front of him. First: 'A gentleman. Paid me to say it.'
  - At first light a gentleman stopped at the yard gate, looked up at the body for a long moment, and gave her a silver crown to tell anyone who asked that she'd seen the clerk jump. The debts were his idea; the moonlight was hers.
  - Who: a good coat, a silver-topped cane, a wheat-ear signet ring and enough lavender water to make your eyes run; the big man with him had a bandaged hand and called him 'Master Sallow'.
  - The crown, stamped on one face with a little wheat-ear, is in her fist. She thinks the picture makes it worth more.
  - When the gentleman had gone, she helped Cob get the boots off the body (he stood on her shoulders).
  - She knows Cob was in the loft last night and heard it happen, and from what he heard (a silver-topped cane, a big man with a bad hand) she has guessed who did it. She's terrified for him.
- Attitude at the start, and what brings them round (your prep: they never spell it out): Hostile and mocking from the wall top. Sticks to her paid story and steers Cob away from questions. She gives way when her lie is caught out, or when someone convincingly offers to keep Cob safe and out of the Mending House; she'd sooner run than be arrested. Torn: fierce, fast and out for herself, but all of it is for Cob: she'll lie, run and swear for him, and break for him and nobody else. It shows the moment Cob is in danger, or in trouble with the watch. Ties here: Cob; Kell, the one with the notebook who doesn't shout, which is worse, and who has let Cob go twice (Moll doesn't know yet what she wants for it).
