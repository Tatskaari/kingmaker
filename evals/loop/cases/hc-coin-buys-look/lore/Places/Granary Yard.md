# The granary yard

The cobbled yard behind the King's Granary, in the granary quarter, where the palace's grain is stored and counted. You notice the smell of wet grain, pigeons and river mud; the hoist beam jutting out high above the loft door; and a yard swept bare for the Assembly.

**What's here**
- **The granary's back wall**, with the hoist beam high above the loft door. The sack hoist's rope runs over the beam's pulley and in through a slot above the loft door, where it's tied off inside.
- **The tally loft** behind that door, where the under-clerk of the Royal Granary Roll keeps the palace's grain accounts. The Roll is a great book chained to the tally desk.
- **The big yard doors** into the granary, padlocked.
- **A water butt** in one corner, and **the yard wall**, shoulder-high on the lane side.
- **The yard gate** onto the lane, a cart shed across the lane, and an old elm leaning over the wall, full of pigeons.

**Who's about, and why**
- **Porters**, who unload the Purveyor's grain wagons, carry sacks and drink. Fewer wagons this month means less to carry and more time to drink it. Nat Pike, a porter with a thirst and heavy hands, is one of them.
- **The granary warden**, Mistress Ida Garner, who keeps the keys. This week she's at the palace counting Assembly provisions.
- **The Purveyor's carters**, who bring the palace's grain wagons in under wheat-ear canvas.
- **The granary rats**: children who run errands, pinch things and sleep rough round the yards.

**What it needs, trades and resents**: it needs the wagons that keep not coming. Bays that should be full for the feast stand empty. Grain comes in by wagon and goes up to the palace. The porters resent the empty bays (no loads, no pay), and the Purveyor's carters, who never lift a sack.

**The rules of the place, and who keeps them**: the big doors stay padlocked. The warden keeps the key, and the Purveyor's under-purveyors have their own. The Roll stays chained to the tally desk. The porters' ladders are locked inside. Granary rats are chased off by the porters, and taken up by the watch for the Mending House if they're caught.

**How the town's troubles show here**: the "lost" wagons are the ones that should have come here. Dear bread means hungry granary rats. And the Assembly has the warden at the palace and the yard swept bare.

**What's happening now**: at first light on the eve of the Assembly the porters found Wendell Prout, under-clerk of the Granary Roll, hanged from the sack hoist, and the Watch Captain called it suicide. Before noon, Grunda Ironjaw dragged the full water butt beneath Prout and climbed up while Sergeant Kell steadied it; Cob and Moll watched. Grunda saw a slanting noose mark and a level line around Prout's throat, and told Kell her army experience led her to conclude he was strangled before being hoisted. Kell recorded the marks and murder finding in her notebook. Kell saw the marks; Cob and Moll heard Grunda's account. When Grunda asked Cob who Silvertop was and what he heard, Cob told her, with Kell and Moll present, that the man had a silver-topped cane, came with the grain wagons and smelled of flowers; he said another man called him “Master,” demanded the page with Prout, who answered “proper channels,” and that he then heard a thump, choking noise and the hoist rope creak. This is Cob's account, not an independently established identification of either man. After Grunda asked Kell to keep the children's names out of the watch book, Kell clarified they were not written there and entered “Witness: names withheld, as promised”; Grunda, Cob and Moll heard her. Kell now records Grunda's account of the page, found sewn into Cob's boot and bearing false grain entries signed J. Sallow, Cob's report that the men demanded that page before the thump, choking and rope creak, and Grunda's accusation that Sallow arranged Prout's killing. The notebook preserves what Grunda and Cob reported; it does not independently establish that the silver-caned man was Sallow or prove he ordered the murder. Grunda keeps the page in her armour. Kell says she will sign Prout in as murdered by Master Sallow, but has not yet made that entry in the Captain's book. She closes her notebook and leaves for the palace with Grunda, Cob and Moll. Prout remains hanging with the water butt beneath him; Kell's promised signing as murder and the dead-cart's arrival have not yet happened. The children's names remain withheld from the watch-book entry.
