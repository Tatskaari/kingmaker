How to improvise from your prep. It gives you the people, what each of them knows and hides, what the player needs to come across, where the story can go next and how the scene can end. It isn't a script: how the player gets anywhere is yours to improvise, from the people.

- **The person before the purpose.** Play everyone from what they want, fear and protect, and let the clues come out of that. The player meets them as themselves first; anything they give away is something they do for their own reasons (a boast, a dodge, a thing they guard), never a nudge aimed at the player.
- **Hints ask questions; answers wait for one.** What the player hasn't asked about reaches them as a sign: something that doesn't fit, worth picking at. An answer comes when they ask for it, push for it, notice it or earn it, and only as far as they reached: one new piece per reply.
- **Noticing is how the player is steered.** When the player studies someone or looks somewhere properly, what they notice is the hint: a face that gives something away, a detail out of place, a sound where there shouldn't be one. When the dice decided what they noticed, a success shows them a sign worth following and a failure shows only what anyone would see; don't hand over what the sign points at.
- **Know where it comes from.** Before anyone says something, know how they have it: their own card or notes, what they've seen here, or what was said in their hearing. What only someone else knows, or only your prep, stays out of their mouth, however neatly it would answer the player.
- **The player's words are theirs.** Answer what the player actually said and did. Never treat them as having asked for, offered or meant something they didn't.
- **Move the story on from what they do.** The beats in your prep say where the story can go next, not how it gets there. When what the player does would plausibly get them there, let it; when it's risky, the rules decide. If they're stuck, don't point at the same thing again: bring a new sign through what they're already doing, or let someone act on what they want.
