# The palace kitchens

Vaulted kitchens off the kitchen yard in the palace's back courts: spits, bread ovens, a pastry room, cold larders and a scullery. You notice more heat, steam and shouting than anywhere else in Caerwyn, a feast for four delegations in the making, and the cook in the middle of it.

**What's here**: the spits and bread ovens; the pastry room; the cold larders; the scullery; and the kitchen yard, where deliveries come in from the service entrance.

**Who's about, and why**
- **Mistress Pruett**, the palace cook, who runs the kitchens like a warship and hears everything. She wants the feast on the table at the hour the Marshal has written down, and every dish fit to set before four delegations.
- **Cooks, scullions and kitchen boys**, and on a feast day **extra hands**: the kitchens take anyone who'll scrub, turn a spit or carry a bucket, and feed them. Nobody asks a child with a bucket who they are.
- **The Purveyor's carters**, in and out of the kitchen yard with deliveries.

**What it needs, trades and resents**: it needs flour, and the office's wagons keep coming short. It trades scraps, and gossip, which comes up from the river with the eels and the fish and goes out again with the scraps. It resents the Royal Purveyor's office: its deliveries, its under-purveyors, and its purse silver, stamped with a wheat-ear so that the kitchens can't pinch it.

**The rules of the place, and who keeps them**: Mistress Pruett's word is law below stairs. Deliveries come in by the service entrance and are counted in the kitchen yard. Office silver is the office's, and a kitchen hand caught with a wheat-ear coin has some explaining to do.

**How the town's troubles show here**: the palace's own grain has been "lost to goblins", so the feast's bread is being baked from what the office could find, and the office has promised the rest by morning. The cushions haven't arrived either, and the under-purveyor for linen has been seen in the kitchen yard asking after them.

**What's happening now**: the eve of the Assembly. The feast is being cooked through the night for tomorrow, after the Assembly sits.
