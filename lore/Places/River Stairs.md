# The river stairs and the docks

The river stairs run from the quay at the bottom of the town down the steep bank to the wharves. You notice the worn stone steps, wet and green all winter; the porters waiting on the landings with their hooks and coils of rope; and the river at the bottom, brown and fast by day and black by night. Nobody has ever counted the same number of steps twice.

**What's here**
- **The landings**, where porters wait for work, and a **pump** on one of them, where they wash.
- **The Drowned Rat**, dug into the bank on a landing halfway down.
- **The wharves** along the river at the foot of the stairs. The warehouses have hoists jutting out over the water and river doors at water level, so barges can load straight from the store. The grain warehouses are at the downriver end, where the lamps give out, and the Heron is the last of them.
- **The Wharf Road**, which winds down the bank behind the warehouses at the downriver end: the way wagons get to the wharves, since nothing on wheels can take the stairs.
- **The river**, in winter spate: high, fast and freezing. What goes in at the wharves comes out at the sea, or doesn't.

**Who's about, and why**
- **Dock-porters**: big men, paid by the load, who drink it by the night.
- **Lightermen and bargees**, working the barges in the stream and moored at the foot of the stairs.
- **Eel-sellers**, and **mudlarks** picking the mud at low water for anything worth a penny.
- **Saltmere factors' clerks**, who pay best and ask least.

**What it needs, trades and resents**: the docks live on loads, and the Saltmere factors pay best: the Heron pays double for night loading and doesn't care for questions. Porters grumble about Saltmere barges going downriver full while Caerwyn goes short, and take Saltmere's money anyway.

**The rules, and who keeps them**: the stairs are the river watch's patch. The river watch walks them in fours after dark, or not at all. What a man does on the stairs is the river's business.

**How the town's troubles show here**: bread is dear, so the stew on the stairs is thin and the night work is welcome. Saltmere barges go downriver low in the water. And the docks shut for the procession tomorrow, so the river loses a day's pay.

**After dark** the wharves are black and quiet but for the odd barge loading by lantern. The watch comes down only when it must.

**What's happening now**: the eve of the Assembly. The docks shut tomorrow for the procession, so the river pays off early this evening and the Rat is full by dusk. The river watch is up on the Assembly route with the rest of the watch. At the downriver end a broad Saltmere barge lies alongside the Heron, and the ebb runs at the second bell after midnight, as every bargee knows.

**Room to improvise**: porters and their nicknames, what the mudlarks find, a lighterman's grudge about mooring fines. Tie each to dear bread, Saltmere money or the procession.
