# The granary yard: the truth

**The night.** A little after the midnight bells, in the small hours of the eve of the Assembly, Master Sallow and Big Hobb let themselves in by the big yard doors with Sallow's key and caught Prout working late in the tally loft. After an argument they struck him on the back of the head, strangled him with a cord, put a noose on him, hung it on the hoist hook and hauled him up from inside the loft. Hobb hauled, and the rope burned his right hand. They tied the rope off on its cleat with a docker's knot, barred the loft door on the inside, emptied Prout's satchel and dropped it by the gate, and padlocked the yard doors behind them. His spectacles fell into the gutter as he went up. Cob, asleep in his nest in the loft, heard it all and hid till dawn.

**At first light** Sallow came back to the yard gate with Hobb, looked up at the body for a long moment and paid Moll a wheat-ear silver crown to say she'd seen the clerk jump. When he'd gone, the kids pulled Prout's boots off (Cob stood on Moll's shoulders). Then the porters came.

**The loft**, as the killers left it: a smashed lantern and spilled ink by the tally desk; heel scuffs in the grain dust from the desk to the loft door; the loft door barred; the hoist rope tied off on its cleat with a docker's knot. The Roll is still chained to the desk with a page razored out. The pages either side list King's Road wagons "lost to goblins", each countersigned "J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor".

**The body** shows what a hanging doesn't make: a thin, level cord mark low round the neck beneath the noose's slanting burn, a swelling under the hair at the back of the skull, and clean palms.

**The boots**, now on Cob's feet: the page is sewn into the lining of the left one, and the backs of both heels are scraped raw from the dragging.

**Ways in**: the big yard doors (padlocked; the warden and the under-purveyors have keys); the loft door (barred inside, and high up); and a loose board behind the water butt, child-sized (a grown-up can squeeze through with effort), into the granary and up to Cob's "castle", a nest of sacks and candle-ends behind the stacks in the loft. The porters' ladders are locked inside.
