# The granary yard

The cobbled yard behind the King's Granary, in the granary quarter, where the palace's grain is stored and counted. It smells of wet grain, pigeons and river mud.

What's there:
- **The granary's back wall**, with the hoist beam jutting out high above the loft door. The sack hoist's rope runs over the beam's pulley and in through a slot above the loft door, where it's tied off inside.
- **The tally loft** behind that door, where the under-clerk of the Royal Granary Roll keeps the palace's grain accounts. The Roll is a great book chained to the tally desk.
- **The big yard doors** into the granary, padlocked. The warden keeps the key; the Purveyor's under-purveyors have their own.
- **A water butt** in one corner; **the yard wall**, shoulder-high on the lane side; **the yard gate** onto the lane, and a cart shed across the lane.

Who's about:
- **Porters**, who unload the Purveyor's grain wagons, carry sacks and drink.
- **The granary warden**, Mistress Ida Garner, who keeps the keys. This week she's at the palace counting Assembly provisions.
- **The granary rats**: children who run errands, pinch things and sleep rough round the yards.

At first light on the eve of the Assembly the porters found Wendell Prout, under-clerk of the Granary Roll, hanged from the sack hoist. The Watch Captain called it suicide.
