# The watch house

A narrow brick watch house at the edge of the granary quarter. You notice the lamp over the door, which burns all night; the bell on the front counter, to ring when nobody's behind it (this week, always); and the quiet.

**What's here**
- **The front room**: the counter and its bell, benches, and a board of notices. Alongside the usual thieves and lost dogs, this week the board carries the order of procession, the Marshal's arms order, and the Purveyor's office's Assembly delivery notices, signed "J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor".
- **The sergeants' room** behind it, where Sergeant Kell's desk is the tidiest thing in the building.
- **Two cells**, usually with a drunk in one.
- **The cold room** in the cellar, once an ice house, where the murdered dead wait for the watch surgeon. A body comes in only when a sergeant signs for it.
- **The back yard**, where the dead-cart and its long ladder are kept.

**Who's about, and why**: Sergeant Agnes Kell, when she isn't out; Corporal Abner Tull and his two constables, when they aren't on the route; in Assembly week, mostly nobody. The Captain works from the palace this week.

**What it needs, trades and resents**: it needs men, and the surgeon back from the palace. It trades in reports, which the Captain likes short. It resents the Assembly route, and the river stairs, which it leaves to the river watch.

**The rules of the place, and who keeps them**: the Captain's book says how each death is filed. A sergeant signs a body into the cold room, and answers for it. Whoever brings a prisoner in locks him up and keeps the key, because this week there's nobody to leave it with.

**How the town's troubles show here**: the Assembly has emptied it; the procession notices cover the board; and the dead-cart goes out to the north gate on the Captain's say-so.

**What's happening now**: the eve of the Assembly, and the whole watch is on the route through the night. Who is in the cold room or the cells depends on how the day has gone: the clerk from the granary, if a sergeant signed him in as a murder; anyone taken in since.
