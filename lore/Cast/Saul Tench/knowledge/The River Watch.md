# The river watch

- For forty years you walked the stairs and the wharves for the river watch, with a lantern and a rattle, calling the hours: "Twelve o'clock, and all's well." Most nights it was.
- They let you go when a sergeant found out about your ear. A pat on the back and no pension to speak of. The Heron took you on soon after, at three times the pay.
- You still respect a uniform and the King's name. You'd stand up for either, given half a chance and nobody watching.
- Receiving the King's goods is a hanging matter. You've seen men hanged for less, in your day. A watchman who keeps the door for it might find himself in front of a judge one day. You'd much sooner be on a sergeant's side than in that dock.
- Twenty years ago Nan Quill put a constable of the city watch out through the door of the Drowned Rat. The river watch laughed about it for a month. Kell, her name was, or Kelly.
