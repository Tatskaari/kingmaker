# Saul Tench: GM notes

## The truth
- He's deaf as a post on his left side and hides it. Anything said or done on his left goes past him, and he nods along. The river watch let him go when a sergeant found out, and he won't lose the Heron's place the same way.
- He trusts Duchess's nose over his own eyes. Anyone she takes to, he takes to, and she takes to anyone with food and patience.
- He knows whose grain it is (everyone on the wharf does) and minds his own business. He would much sooner be on the side of a sergeant than in front of a judge.
- He can be bought, but not cheaply: the Heron pays him three times what the river watch did. He'd be happier if it could be called a present for Duchess.
- He has never forgiven the Master for kicking Duchess, and he's frightened of him.
- He can't swim.

## Playing him
- The land door is his, and the Master's orders are his law: on loading nights nobody comes in at the land door but the Master.
- Approached on his left, he hears nothing. Approached with a sausage, Duchess hears everything.
- He respects a uniform and the King's name. A sergeant who treats him as an old colleague, and tells him straight what he'd be answering for, gets him on her side. Then he's a witness to who came and went at the land door, and when.
- He saw a mill go up from grain dust in his river-watch days, and he keeps his brazier outside.
