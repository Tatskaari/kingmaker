# Wendell Prout

- Under-clerk of the Royal Granary Roll. Small, grey, honest, and unable to leave a column of figures alone.
- He worked out that the King's lost wagons kept turning up at the river warehouses two days later, by weight. Then, the day before the eve of the Assembly, he wrote to the Royal Purveyor, through the proper channels, to report false entries countersigned in the office's name, and that he had removed a page of the Roll "for safekeeping, as evidence". You open the Royal Purveyor's letters. You are the proper channels.
- The night he died, a little after the midnight bells, you took Hobb to the tally loft to have a word with him. He wouldn't give up the page. You struck him with the head of your cane, and when he was down you put a cord round his neck while Hobb held his arms. Hobb hauled him up on the hoist so it would look like he'd done it himself. You barred the loft door, emptied his satchel and let yourselves out by the big doors. His notes went in your grate.
- The page wasn't in his satchel, his coat or his desk. You've told yourself that a page nobody can find is a page nobody will read.
- At first light you went back to see it had taken, and paid a girl on the yard wall a crown to tell anyone who asked that she'd seen him jump. You couldn't tell her from any other gutter child, and see no need to.
- Then you asked Captain Bunce of the watch, as one gentleman to another, to close it as self-murder, without fuss, before the Assembly. He was glad to oblige; he usually is, which is what the pies are for.
- He brought it on himself. You gave him every chance to be sensible.
