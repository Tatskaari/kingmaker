# Master Josiah Sallow: GM notes

## The racket
- King's Road grain wagons bound for the palace are written off in the Royal Granary Roll as "lost to goblins", each countersigned "J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor". The grain goes to the Heron warehouse by night, still in the palace's wheat-ear sacks, is re-sacked under the heron mark of a Saltmere grain factor and sold downriver.
- To keep the goblin story true he pays the hedge-goblins of the burnt mill, in the factor's heron pennies, to be seen on the road and to raid now and then.
- His cover, if questioned: the grain is condemned stock lawfully sold, with an order of sale signed "J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor". Nobody has ever checked.
- The proof is the Heron's true book, kept by Mister Scrope: every "lost" wagon by its Roll number, with Sallow's own initials, "J.S.", on every page. He and the factor don't trust each other, so the book exists.

## Prout
- Prout noticed that "lost" wagons weighed in at the river warehouses two days later. After Kell told him to take it to the palace, he wrote to the Royal Purveyor, properly, reporting false entries and that he had removed a page of the Roll "for safekeeping, as evidence". Sallow opens the Royal Purveyor's letters.
- The night before Prout was found, a little after the midnight bells, Sallow took Hobb to the tally loft "to have a word with a clerk". Prout wouldn't give up the page. Sallow struck him with the head of his cane and strangled him with a cord while Hobb held his arms, then had Hobb haul him up on the sack hoist to look like a suicide. The rope took the skin off Hobb's right palm. They barred the loft door, emptied Prout's satchel and left by the big doors with Sallow's key. Sallow burned the notes. He never found the page: it was sewn into the lining of Prout's left boot.
- At first light they went back to look, and Sallow paid a granary girl (Moll) a wheat-ear crown to say she'd seen the clerk jump. Then he asked Captain Bunce of the watch to close it as self-murder, without fuss, before the Assembly. The Captain isn't bought, just obliging: Sallow keeps him in quarter-day hampers and the watch in Assembly rations, and has promised him the head of the procession.
- He will never admit the murder, except to someone he believes is about to die. If he ever tells Kell how Prout came to his notice, let him: "He went through the proper channels, Sergeant. I am the proper channels."

## The courier (background)
In a grey hood, smelling of lavender, he paid the goblin chief Gubbin heron pennies to take "the Saltmere rider with the blue satchel" at the ford, with more promised once her letters were burned. Her sealed letter is Saltmere's credit for the King's treasury, the loan that pays for the Assembly. An Assembly short of grain and money is one the Purveyor's office gets to save: Sallow means to sell the King his own grain back at Assembly prices and be thanked for it with the Purveyor's chain. This thread can stay in the background.

## What he doesn't know
- Where Prout's page went: into the boots a granary boy is wearing.
- That the boy, Cob, was in the loft and heard it all.
- That the girl's crown is office silver, traceable to him, and that she still has it.
- That Scrope, who keeps the true book, was Prout's oldest friend.
- That the goblins know him by his lavender water, that Snikkit saw the grey hood, and that Gubbin kept the courier's letters for the seals.
- How frightened Hobb is, how much he's drinking, and how many people have noticed the hand.

## Playing him
- Dangerous at a desk, not in a fight: he has men for fights. Under the courtesy is a coward, and proof in front of witnesses he can't buy or drown unmans him.
- His tell is the lavender. The more frightened he is, the more he dabs.
- Cornered with the true book, he'd sooner burn it or drown it than see it taken.
