# The Heron

- The Heron warehouse stands at the downriver end of the wharves, where the lamps give out, half on the wharf and half on piles over the river, with a painted heron on its sign. It's a Saltmere grain factor's place. You work there on loading nights from the eleventh bell, for double pay in new silver.
- The King's grain comes in by night in the palace's sacks, the ones stamped with a wheat-ear. Hep Grist's gang slit them, pour the grain into new sacks stamped with a heron and sew them shut, and the heron sacks go down to a Saltmere barge alongside, which sails on the ebb. The empty wheat-ear sacks go in the river at the end of the night, weighted with stones. You work the hoist.
- Everyone at the Heron knows whose grain it is, and nobody says. A sack's a sack, you used to say.
- The way in: the gang come and go by the river door, along the plank walk on the piles, and show a brass heron token at its grille so whoever's nearest inside draws the bolt. Yours is on the greasy string round your neck. It's only brass.
- Old Saul the watchman keeps the land door, with his great dog Duchess asleep across the threshold. He's deaf in his left ear, though he won't own it, and he trusts the dog over his own eyes. On loading nights nobody comes in at the land door but the Master.
- Mister Scrope the clerk sits up in the glass counting-box over the floor and writes every sack in his book, the true book. It never leaves his hand.
- On loading nights the Master comes at the midnight bell, in at the land door, to see the last sacks go down, initial the clerk's book and take his share. The Crake brothers come with him: two river-bruisers alike as two bollards, and only one of them talks.
- If you aren't at the river door when the Master comes, he'll know something's wrong.
