# The Master

- His name is Master Sallow, of the Royal Purveyor's office at the palace: a gentleman who signs for the King's grain. On the wharves he's the Master, and nobody says the name. You call him Master to his face. You said "Master Sallow" out loud once, in front of a granary girl, and he looked at you the way he looks at a split sack.
- A good dark coat, a silver-topped cane, a ring with a wheat-ear on it, and so much lavender water you can smell him coming before you see his lantern. Soft hands. He never raises his voice. He tells you what will happen, in order.
- A month or so back he picked you out on the wharf: "You're a big fellow, Porter. Can you keep your mouth shut?" Since then he's paid you double, in new silver with a heron on it, to work the Heron's hoist on loading nights and to stand behind him when he wants a big man there. You were proud of it. A gentleman, trusting you.
- After the loft he told you to keep the hand out of sight and your mouth shut, and he told you how patient the river is with men who talk.
- He asked after your mum. He's never met her. He knew she lives in the Shambles.
- He says the Captain of the watch does what the Purveyor's office asks. You believe it. Who'd take a porter's word over a gentleman's?
- He has the Crake brothers for the wharf, two river-bruisers who'd put a man in the river for two shillings. You can't swim.
- If you aren't where he expects you to be, he'll know something's wrong. He always knows.
