# Cob: GM notes

## The truth
- The boots he wears are Wendell Prout's. He and Moll pulled them off the body at first light the morning Prout was found, before the porters came (he stood on her shoulders). He lies about them ("found 'em", "they're Cob's da's") and fights to keep them.
- Sewn into the lining of the left boot is the page Prout razored out of the Granary Roll. Cob doesn't know: he thinks the crackle is what good boots do. The backs of both heels are scraped raw, as if their owner had been dragged.
- He sleeps in the tally loft, his "castle": sacks and candle-ends behind the stacks, in through a loose board behind the water butt in the granary yard.
- The night before Prout died, Cob watched him cut a page from the Roll and sew something into his boot. The night Prout died he heard Silvertop (Sallow) demand "the page" before the killing. He hasn't put the page and the boot together. A player who hears both halves can.
- The big man in the loft was Big Hobb Mallory, whom Cob has seen at the Drowned Rat for years. Cob knew him by his size and his voice. He's frightened to name him, and would sooner point, from behind something, where Hobb can't see.
- He has told nobody but Moll.

## Playing him
- Bravado over terror: every cocky line is a boy checking whether this grown-up will hurt him.
- He gives up what he saw only to someone he believes will keep him safe from those men, or under a real fright, which costs the player Moll's and Kell's good opinion.
- He's eleven and a witness to murder. If Sallow or Hobb learn he was in the loft, he's in real danger.

## What he does next
- **If he trusts the investigator**, he follows them, at a distance he thinks is clever: to the market, down the river stairs, into Nan's scullery as her pot-boy for the night. He knows the big cove from the loft by sight, and he'll point him out from behind something.
- **If he's frightened off**, he and Moll go to ground in the granary quarter, and he doesn't sleep in the loft again.
- **If the men learn he was in the loft** (from a loud reveal in the wrong place), he's in real danger. Hobb would never hurt a child; the Master's people would.
- **If he's taken in**, he's in the watch-house cells, white and silent, and the Mending House is next unless someone with authority speaks for him.
