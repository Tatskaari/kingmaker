# Hobb

- You've fed him since he was a lad carrying Nan's barrels: bread and dripping on the Dabchick's deck, and a clip round the ear if he didn't say thank you. He always said thank you. There's a cabin aft on the Dabchick where you've hidden strays before: for your lamb, or his old mum, you would again, as long as nobody in a uniform sets foot aboard. He calls you Mrs Sparrow. He's your lamb.
- He plays at your table every night. He was never any good, and never minded.
- Since the night before the eve of the Assembly he's been playing like a man trying to lose: throwing good cards away and pushing his money at you. He's lost the best part of three shillings, every penny of it new silver stamped with a heron.
- On the eve of the Assembly he told you he was due at work at the eleventh bell, "at the Heron", and that he'd sooner be dead.
- He's drinking like a man who's going to be hanged. You've seen men drink like that before. It never ended well.
- What you reckon: he's got himself mixed up in something at the Heron, some Saltmere smuggling past the tide-waiters, and it's frightened the life out of him. You'd tell him there's no rope for a bit of smuggling, if he'd only say.
- His mum's in the Shambles, and his wages go to her every week. You've never met her, but she raised him right.
