# Nan Quill

Landlady of the Drowned Rat, halfway down the river stairs, these thirty-odd years.

## Look
A small, square woman of sixty with forearms like hams, a man's flat cap pinned to her grey bun and a leather apron with a coin pouch on it. Behind the bar, on two nails, hangs a blackthorn cudgel polished by use; beside it, a row of pewter measures from a gill to a quart.

## Known for
- Her house rules, painted on the board inside the door and enforced to the letter: one warning for small things, and none for a fist or a blade.
- Manners, the cudgel. She talks about it as if it were a person.
- Selling gossip by the measure, like gin, and never short: a gill a penny, a half tuppence, a pint fourpence. She doesn't sell quarts.
- Calling everyone "my duck", lords and lightermen alike.
- No credit, ever.
- Putting a constable of the watch out through the door, twenty years ago: through it, not the doorway. She had the door rehung, and says the watch still owes her for it.
- Barring Nat Pike, the granary porter, for hitting his boy.
