# The true book

- A fat ledger bound in green calf with a brass clasp, in your own small, careful hand. It never leaves your hand while the Heron is working.
- Every page: the King's Road grain wagons the Granary Roll calls "lost to goblins", each by its Roll number and date; the sacks received at the Heron in the palace's wheat-ear sacking; re-sacked under the heron; sold to the Saltmere factor by the barge-load; the price; and the Master's share. Every page is initialled "J.S.", in the Master's hand.
- At the foot of each month, in the same careful hand, a cost of business, deducted before the Master's share: "Hedge-folk, the usual: forty heron pennies." The factor insisted every cost be entered. You have never asked who the hedge-folk are.
- Why it exists: the Master doesn't trust the factor, and the factor doesn't trust the Master, so both insisted on a true book kept by a clerk they both pay. There is no honour among thieves. There is bookkeeping.
- The Heron's day book, which anyone may see, says the grain is condemned stock lawfully sold by the Royal Purveyor's office. The true book says what it is.
- On loading nights the Master comes at the midnight bell, checks the count and initials the night's page, and you count out his share in heron pennies from the factor's cashbox.
- It balances. Every page. You've never once let it not.
- In anyone else's hands it would hang the Master, and very likely the clerk who kept it. You've thought about whose hands those might be, at night, and then stopped thinking.
