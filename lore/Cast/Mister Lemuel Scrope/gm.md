# Mister Lemuel Scrope: GM notes

## The truth
- He and Wendell Prout were apprentices together under the old Keeper of the Granary Roll forty years ago, and have played draughts every Sunday since. On the eve of the Assembly he was told Wendell hanged himself, and he doesn't believe it: Wendell never left a column unbalanced in his life.
- He knows why Wendell is dead. Wendell kept the Granary Roll, and the wagons the Roll calls "lost to goblins" are in Scrope's own book, by their Roll numbers. He has spent the day sick with it, and with the thought that a man who keeps an honest book for the Master might be next.
- At their last Sunday game Wendell asked him, out of nowhere, whether the Heron was busy at nights and whose grain it took in. Scrope said "Saltmere's" and changed the subject. He believes Wendell already knew and was giving him the chance to tell the truth. Nobody can now say. Wendell found his weights without Scrope's help, but Scrope will never be sure of that.
- He'd know Wendell's hand anywhere, and his pencilled weights. Shown the page from Prout's boot, he knows it at once, and that puts it beyond doubt for him.
- The true book is the one thing that can hang the Master, and he knows it. It's also the one thing he'll fight for.

## What he doesn't know
- Who exactly killed Wendell, or how. He's sure it was the Master's doing. He doesn't know that Hobb held the rope.
- That the Master has no idea Wendell was his friend. He's terrified the Master knows.

## Playing him
- Timid, punctilious and evasive. Every frightened evasion comes out as arithmetic.
- He gives up the book, or swears to it, for Wendell's sake, for his own neck or for the King, once someone makes the account balance for him: proof of what happened to Wendell, and a way through that a frightened clerk can believe in. He hands it over in both hands: "It balances. Every page. Wendell would want it to balance."
- With the Master in the room he stands, presents the book with shaking hands, and can't look at anyone.
