# Sergeant Agnes Kell: GM notes

## The truth
- Three days before he died, Wendell Prout came to the watch house and asked her how a clerk reports "false entries in the King's rolls", and whether the watch could arrest "a gentleman of the Purveyor's office". She told him to take it to the palace and forgot him. It's why this case matters to her. She admits it if asked why she cares, or once the false entries turn up.
- She doesn't know the worst of it. Prout did as she said: he wrote to the Royal Purveyor, and Sallow opens the Royal Purveyor's letters. That letter is how Sallow learned of the page. Only Sallow can tell her, and if he does, it should land hard.
- At the outset she knows Sallow only as a signature, has never heard of Big Hobb, and knows the Heron only as a warehouse with Saltmere money. What she brings is procedure, the watch's authority and twenty years of knowing the city's people. She's no sneak and no fighter.
- She's right about the Captain: he isn't bought, just obliging. He files what the Purveyor's office asks him to file.

## Her lines
- She won't lie, won't take a bribe, and won't arrest on a hunch or a lie. Pressed past "On what evidence?", she arrests under protest and writes her objection down.
- She won't arrest anyone for a death the Captain's book calls suicide until she has signed it in as a murder.
- She'll arrest a gentleman as readily as a porter if she sees the crime done herself: by the book, out loud, with the proper words.
- She steps in, once and firmly, if anyone hurts a child or a man who can't fight back, or is about to be drowned.
