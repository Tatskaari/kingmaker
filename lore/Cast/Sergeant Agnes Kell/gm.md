# Sergeant Agnes Kell: GM notes

Purpose (behind the screen): the investigator's partner. She brings procedure, the watch's authority, a notebook that counts as evidence, and twenty years of knowing the city's people. She never brings the answer.

## The truth
- Three days before he died, Wendell Prout came to the watch house and asked her, very politely, how a clerk reports "false entries in the King's rolls", and whether the watch could arrest "a gentleman of the Purveyor's office". He wouldn't say more without the proper form. She told him to take it to the palace and forgot him until the morning he was found. It's why this case matters to her.
- She doesn't know the worst of it. Prout did as she said: he wrote to the Royal Purveyor, and Sallow opens the Royal Purveyor's letters. That letter is how Sallow learned of the page. Only Sallow can tell her ("He went through the proper channels, Sergeant. I am the proper channels."), and if he does, it should land hard.
- Nor does she know that Prout had written to the King a week earlier, and that the King put the letter on his after-the-Assembly pile. She and the King failed the same man the same way, and neither knows it of the other.
- At the outset she knows Sallow only as a signature on the Assembly delivery notices, has never heard of Big Hobb, and knows the Heron only as a warehouse with Saltmere money.
- She's right about the Captain: he isn't bought, just obliging. He files what the Purveyor's office asks him to file.

## Her secret, staged
- **Shows**: she knew Prout by sight, and goes very still at "false entries" or the Purveyor's office.
- **Torn**: she sent him away, by the book, and she still believes in the book.
- **Opens**: the page in her hands, or the investigator asking her straight why this one matters to her.
- **First**: "I'd met him."
- **Then, once**, in her own words: the visit, and "I should have listened." Told is told: she never tells it again, though she'll tell the King, once, in the morning.

## What she does next, if nobody stops her
She acts on her own errands, never on the investigator's behalf.
- **The granary, before noon**: if what's found holds up, she signs Prout in as a murder over the Captain's word and answers for it; if it doesn't, she writes down exactly what she saw and lets the dead-cart have him, and writes her objection beside it.
- **The afternoon**: she goes back to the granary porters at the noon break with a pie (porters will talk to a pie when they won't talk to the watch) and comes away with word of a big dock-man with a bandaged hand. At dusk she goes down the river stairs to the Drowned Rat after him, with the investigator or without, though she'd rather not walk into Nan Quill's house alone. If Prout went into the Captain's book as a murder, a runner brings word that the Captain will have her report "after the Assembly"; she means to have the case made before he reads it.
- **The Rat**: she keeps rule four (lent, not on duty), writes down whatever is said, and takes Hobb in, by the book and outside Nan's door, only if the investigator wants it and the evidence holds.
- **The Heron**: she keeps the wharf and the land door with her whistle, where she can see who comes and goes, unless the investigator wants a witness inside. If she sees Sallow take the King's grain, she arrests him when the investigator calls for it or gives her the moment.
- **The King's morning**: she brings her notebook to the palace, swears to what she saw, and tells the King about Prout's visit, whatever it costs her.

## Her lines
- She won't lie, won't take a bribe, and won't arrest on a hunch or a lie. Pressed past "On what evidence?", she arrests under protest and writes her objection down.
- She won't arrest anyone for a death the Captain's book calls suicide until she has signed it in as a murder.
- She'll arrest a gentleman as readily as a porter if she sees the crime done herself: by the book, out loud, with the proper words.
- She steps in, once and firmly, if anyone hurts a child or a man who can't fight back, or is about to be drowned.
- She never takes the decisive moment out of the investigator's hands.

## How she could change
If the King acts on her record, she has her answer: the proper channels can be made to work, if someone carries the case properly. If he puts it off, she writes the true record anyway, and keeps a copy.
