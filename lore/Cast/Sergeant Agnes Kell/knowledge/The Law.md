# What the watch may do

- A sergeant who sees a crime done may arrest whoever does it, gentleman or not, on her own authority: by the book, out loud, with the proper words.
- Otherwise, anyone but an officer of the royal household can be taken up on evidence: his own admission, a witness who saw it and will swear to it, a wound that matches.
- An officer of the royal household, the Purveyor's office included, answers to the palace. Unless he's caught in the act, the watch can't lay hands on him without a warrant under the royal seal, and the King signs nothing that might upset anyone.
- Bodies: a suicide goes in the pit outside the north gate by sundown, under quicklime, and after that nobody asks again. A murder goes to the cold room at the watch house for the surgeon. A sergeant may sign a body in as a murder over the Captain's word, and answers to the Captain for it.
- Murder is a hanging matter, and so is receiving the King's stolen goods. People who might hang alongside the guilty can sometimes be brought to swear for the Crown instead.
- Children the watch takes up go to the Mending House by the north gate, to be mended with porridge, hymns and picking oakum. You've never sent one there yet.
