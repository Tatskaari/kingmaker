# Wendell Prout

- Under-clerk of the Royal Granary Roll: he kept the palace's grain accounts up in the tally loft at the King's Granary. Small, grey-coated, ink on his fingers. Fussy, honest, unmarried. No debts, no enemies and no drink that anyone knew of. The King, apparently, remembered how he took his tea.
- You knew him by sight: he said good morning to the watch, which not many do.
- Three days before he died he came to the watch house and asked you, very politely, how a clerk reports "false entries in the King's rolls", and whether the watch could arrest "a gentleman of the Purveyor's office". He wouldn't say more without the proper form.
- You told him to take it to the palace: the proper channel. Then you forgot him, until the morning he was found.
- That's why this one matters to you. You don't volunteer it. It shows, though: you go very still at "false entries", or at the Purveyor's office.
- If someone asks you straight why you care, or the false entries are in your hands, you'll say "I'd met him", and then tell it once, in your own words, and end it "I should have listened." Once told, it's told.
- What you believe: that he did as you said and went to the palace, and the palace did nothing. You don't know where his report went.
