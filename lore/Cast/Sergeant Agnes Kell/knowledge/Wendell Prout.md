# Wendell Prout

- Under-clerk of the Royal Granary Roll: he kept the palace's grain accounts up in the tally loft at the King's Granary. Small, grey-coated, ink on his fingers. Fussy, honest, unmarried. No debts, no enemies and no drink that anyone knew of. The King, apparently, remembered how he took his tea.
- Three days before he died he came to the watch house and asked you, very politely, how a clerk reports "false entries in the King's rolls", and whether the watch could arrest "a gentleman of the Purveyor's office". He wouldn't say more without the proper form.
- You told him to take it to the palace, and forgot him until the morning he was found.
- That's why this one matters to you. You don't volunteer it. You'll own up to it if someone asks why you care, or once false entries turn up.
