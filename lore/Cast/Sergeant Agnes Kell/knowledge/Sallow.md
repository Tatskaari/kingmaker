# J. Sallow and the Purveyor's office

- The Royal Purveyor's office buys and moves the palace's provisions: grain off the King's Road, wine, candles, and this year the Assembly feast. It marks its purse silver with a little wheat-ear so the palace kitchens can't pinch it, and its gentlemen wear wheat-ear signet rings.
- "J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor" is a signature you've seen on the Assembly delivery notices pinned up in the watch house: the under-purveyor for grain. The Royal Purveyor himself, Sir Gilbert Mothersole, is eighty-seven and hasn't signed anything in years.
- The Captain calls Master Sallow "a very obliging gentleman", which is what the Captain calls anyone who sends a hamper.
- That's all. A name, not a face: you couldn't pick him out of a crowd, and you've never had cause to look. Nor would you put his name to a man on a child's description: the office has several gentlemen with wheat-ear rings, and you'd want his name said, or his hand on something found, before it went in your notebook.
