# The Heron warehouse

- A Saltmere grain factor's warehouse at the downriver end of the wharves, half on piles over the river, with a heron on its signboard: the factor's own mark. The factor never comes to Caerwyn. You think of him as "our friend downriver", even in your head.
- The arrangement: wagons you've written off as lost to goblins come in by night in the palace's wheat-ear sacks. Hep Grist's gang slit them, re-sack the grain under the heron and load it onto Saltmere barges that sail on the ebb. The factor pays in new silver pennies stamped with a heron, and your share comes in them too.
- If anyone asks, it's condemned stock lawfully sold by the Royal Purveyor's office, and you have the order of sale to prove it, signed "J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor".
- On loading nights you come at the midnight bell, in at the land door, to see the last sacks go down and take your share. For the wharf you hire the Crake brothers, two identical river-bruisers with a price list: a shilling to stand behind a gentleman, two to put a man down, half a crown for the river. Half before, half after.
- Saul, the old watchman, keeps the land door with a brindle monster of a dog called Duchess. You kicked her once. She hasn't forgotten, and nor have you.
- Mister Scrope keeps the true book up in the counting-box: every royal sack bought, re-sacked and sold. The factor won't deal without it, and, to be fair, you wouldn't trust the factor without it either. So every loading night you check the count and initial the page "J.S." yourself. The factor insisted every cost be entered, down to the hedge-folk ("the usual: forty heron pennies"), which you thought excessive. The book never leaves Scrope's hand. He's a dry little clerk who balances; you've never asked him anything about himself.
- Nobody looks twice at a grain barge on a grain river. That's the beauty of it.
