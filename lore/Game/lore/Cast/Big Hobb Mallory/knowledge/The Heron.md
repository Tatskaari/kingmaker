# The Heron

- The Heron warehouse stands at the downriver end of the wharves, where the lamps give out, half on the wharf and half on piles over the river, with a painted heron on its sign. It's a Saltmere grain factor's place. You work there on loading nights from the eleventh bell, for double pay in new silver with a heron on it.
- You work the hoist. The King's grain comes in by night in the palace's sacks, the ones with the wheat-ear on them, and goes out under the heron to a Saltmere barge that sails on the ebb. Everyone at the Heron knows whose grain it is, and nobody says. A sack's a sack, you used to say.
- Your way in: along the plank walk on the piles to the river door, and the brass heron token on the string round your neck held up to the grille, so whoever's nearest inside draws the bolt. It's only brass.
- The forewoman, Hep, runs the gang and shouts the count. She doesn't talk to you much: you're the Master's man, not one of hers.
- An old watchman keeps the land door, with a great dog asleep across the threshold. You've never come in that way. On loading nights the land door is the Master's.
- The clerk up in the glass box writes every sack in his book and never lets go of it. The Master puts his letters in it when he comes. You can't read, so what's in it you couldn't say.
- The Master comes at the midnight bell, in at the land door, with his lantern-boy and the Crake brothers, two river-bruisers alike as two bollards; only one of them talks. You can't swim.
- If you aren't at the river door when the Master comes, he'll know something's wrong. He always knows.
