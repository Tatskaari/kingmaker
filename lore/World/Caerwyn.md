# Caerwyn

The King's city, where the King's Road meets the river: grain comes in by road and goes out by barge, and the palace on the rise lives off both. This winter it's host to the Centennial Assembly, and everything costs more.

**The court.** King Aldren rules from the palace on the rise above the river. He is warm and hospitable, remembers how everyone takes their tea, and is famously unwilling to sign anything that might upset anyone. Marshal Garran Holt keeps the roads, the garrisons and the Assembly timetable, to the minute. Magister Corvin keeps the royal seal and the exact wording of the law.

**The city**
- The palace on the rise, with its offices and kitchens in the back courts.
- The market square: stalls, the market cross, the procession route, and the Corn Exchange, where grain factors trade, chalk up prices and keep the wagon lists.
- The granary quarter: the King's Granary, warehouses, porters' lodgings and a watch house.
- The river stairs, down to the docks and the wharves, with the Wharf Road winding down at the downriver end for wagons.
- The Shambles, the butchers' quarter across the city from the river, where porters' families rent rooms over the shops.
- The north gate, where the city keeps what it would rather not look at: the Mending House inside it, the suicides' pit outside it.

**Why it's like this**: Caerwyn is roads, courts and garrisons. The palace buys first and the court runs the roads; the river carries away whatever pays better downriver. Most of the city works for one or the other: at a desk in the palace, or with a hook on the river.

**Who does well out of it**: the palace and its officers, the grain factors and the Saltmere buyers on the wharves, and, this week, every innkeeper with a bed.
**Who pays for it**: porters, carters and granary rats. A porter earns about eight pennies a day, and the price of bread is the first thing anyone on the river stairs will tell you.

**The rules, and who keeps them**
- The City Watch keeps the King's peace in the streets. Officers of the royal household answer to the palace: the watch may not lay hands on one without a warrant under the royal seal, unless he's caught in the act.
- The river stairs are the river watch's patch.
- The palace bells ring the hours and the half hours for the whole city.
- The Mending House takes in strays and wayward children and mends them with porridge, hymns and picking oakum. Every granary rat would sooner take their chances with the goblins.

**Everyday things**
- Money: twelve silver pennies to the shilling, five shillings to the crown, so a silver crown is more than a week's wages. Caerwyn's pennies carry the King's face, and the chandlers want to see it.
- Magic is Nine Furrows business: weather, wards and grain. Nobody in Caerwyn talks to the dead, and the dead stay dead, which people find a comfort.

**What people say, and who says it**
- Anyone with a desk at the palace: "After the Assembly."
- The river stairs: visitors notice the gilt; the city notices the bread.
- Everyone, about anything that goes missing: "lost to goblins".

**What's happening now**: the eve of the Assembly. There's bunting on every rope that will hold it, the watch is out lining the procession route, delegations' servants are everywhere, and prices are up, bread most of all.
