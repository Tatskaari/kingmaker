# The Royal Palace

King Aldren's palace stands on the rise above the river: gilt, tapestries and a great deal of new bunting, in a winter when bread is dear. You notice the polish on the gate guards, the bunting on everything, and the queue of wagons in the back lane.

**What's here**
- **The front**: the main gate and courtyard, the Great Hall where the Assembly will sit, and the state rooms given over to the four delegations. Off the long gallery is the King's dressing room, all wardrobes, mirrors and a fire, with tall windows over the courtyard.
- **The back courts**: the working palace. Offices (the Treasury, the counting-house and the Royal Purveyor's office), the kitchens and the kitchen yard, stores and stables.
- **The service entrance**: a broad stone archway onto the back lane. Every delivery comes in through it, from flour to cushions.

**Who's about, and why**
- **King Aldren**, at rehearsals and with the delegations all day, and at their dinner tonight.
- **Marshal Garran Holt**, with his pocket watch and the palace guard, keeping the timetable. **Magister Corvin**, with the royal seal.
- **Captain Bunce** of the watch, seeing to the procession route. The **watch surgeon**, on duty for the Assembly. **Mistress Ida Garner**, the granary warden, counting Assembly provisions.
- **The under-purveyors**, in and out of the back courts with their wagons, keys and lists.
- Delegations' servants, porters, cooks and palace pages, all in a hurry.

**What it needs, trades and resents**: it needs the feast fed and the hall ready by morning. It trades in favours, invitations and places in the procession. The kitchens resent the Purveyor's office, and the city resents the gilt.

**The rules of the place, and who keeps them**
- **Getting in**: guests, courtiers and officials come by the main gate, with an invitation or a name the guards know; servants and tradesfolk by the service entrance. By day on the eve the back courts are so full of porters, cooks and delegations' servants that nobody carrying something gets a second look.
- **After dark on the eve** the palace is shut to everyone but the delegations' dinner and the procession rehearsal.
- **The King's time is the Marshal's to give.** Until the Assembly opens, the King belongs to the timetable. The gate has been told about the King's investigator: the King will see them in the morning, before the procession, and not before.
- **Messages**: the gate guard takes written messages for the Marshal. He reads them by evening, and acts at first light on whatever he can act on without the King's word.
- **Arrests**: inside the palace, or of an officer of the Crown, an arrest is the Marshal's to make, at once, on the King's word, and not a moment before.

**How the town's troubles show here**: visitors notice the gilt, and the city notices the bread. The kitchens are short of flour for the feast while the palace's own wagons are "lost to goblins". The cushions for the Assembly seats haven't arrived. And on procession morning the Purveyor's grain cart waits in the courtyard in garlands, to walk fourth "for the people's confidence".

**What's happening now**: on the eve, rehearsals by day and the delegations' dinner by night. On procession morning the delegations form up under their banners in the courtyard, the King is got into his robes, and the procession leaves the gate at the ninth bell.
