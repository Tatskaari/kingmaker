# The Royal Palace

King Aldren's palace stands on the rise above the river: gilt, tapestries and a great deal of new bunting, in a winter when bread is dear. Visitors notice the gilt. The city notices the bread.

- **The front**: the main gate and courtyard, the Great Hall where the Assembly will sit, and the state rooms given over to the four delegations.
- **The back courts**: the working palace. Offices (the Treasury, the counting-house and the Royal Purveyor's office), the kitchens and the kitchen yard, stores and stables.
- **The service entrance**: a broad stone archway onto the back lane. Every delivery comes in through it, from flour to cushions.
- **Getting in**: guests, courtiers and officials by the main gate, with an invitation or a name the guards know; servants and tradesfolk by the service entrance. By day on the eve of the Assembly the back courts are so full of porters, cooks and delegations' servants that nobody carrying something gets a second look. After dark the palace is shut to everyone but the delegations' dinner and the procession rehearsal.

On the eve of the Assembly the King, the Marshal, the Watch Captain, the watch surgeon and every official worth asking are at the palace, and all of them are busy.
