# Bjork and the Kept Hearth

Bjork the Hearthkeeper is the smith-ancestor most often invoked in Kläggenheim. In the familiar tale, he kept the first mountain settlement alive through a winter by burning his treasured workbench when the fuel ran out. He is usually carved with an enormous beard, a hammer and a loaf of bread.

Some dwarves believe he still watches over their hearths. Others regard him as an ancestor whose example is enough. Whether he was the first king, the first smith or simply the first dwarf sensible enough to share his fire depends on who tells the story.

## Everyday customs

A small hearth shrine is common in workshops and homes. At a shared meal, the first piece of bread is set aside for a late arrival. Letting a guest go cold while there is fuel in the house is shameful.

Good workmanship honours Bjork. Concealing a fault in something you sell is an offence against both the buyer and the ancestor; an honest repair is nothing to be ashamed of.

## Things people say

- “On Bjork’s beard.” — A serious assurance, also used carelessly enough to annoy devout relatives.
- “Bjork save the hinges.” — Something heavy, badly made or ill-advised is about to move.
- “There’s room at the hearth.” — An offer of hospitality, or a way to welcome someone into a crew.

Invoking his beard means staking your word on the honour of an ancestor. It is not a claim that every dwarf wears one.
