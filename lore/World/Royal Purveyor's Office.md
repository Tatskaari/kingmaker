# The Royal Purveyor's office

The office that buys and supplies everything the palace eats, drinks, burns and sits on, from grain and wine to candles, coal and cushions. It has offices in the palace's back courts, and wagons, carters and stores all over the city.

- **Purveyance**: by ancient right the office buys for the King at the King's price, which farmers call robbery and purveyors call tradition.
- **The Royal Purveyor**, Sir Gilbert Mothersole, is eighty-seven and hasn't signed anything himself in years. His under-purveyors sign "for the Royal Purveyor".
- **The under-purveyors**: four gentlemen with a department each (grain and flour; wine and cellars; wax, oil and coal; linen and furnishings). Master Josiah Sallow has grain and flour. He rides out with the grain wagons and, like every under-purveyor, holds keys to the stores he supplies, the King's Granary among them. The under-purveyor for linen and furnishings is having a very bad week about the cushions.
- **The wheat-ear**: the office's mark, on its seals, its wagon canvas, its sacks and its officers' signet rings.
- **Purse silver**: the office's purse money is stamped with a little wheat-ear so the palace kitchens can't pinch it, which the kitchens have never forgiven. A wheat-ear coin in a stranger's hand means someone from the office paid them.
- **Write-offs**: palace grain lost on the road is written off in the Royal Granary Roll, and the office countersigns the entry.
