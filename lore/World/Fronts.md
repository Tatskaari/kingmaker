# What's in motion

Caerwyn gets on with its own troubles whether or not the investigator is looking. Each front is someone, or something, that wants something, with a clock of steps.

**How to use it**
- A step ticks at a scene boundary when play has made it true or its time has come, never because it would be dramatic. Tick it (`- [x]`). Strike out a step that play has made meaningless, and say why.
- Nobody acts on what they couldn't know: each step says who learns what, and from whom.
- A step shows once, as something seen or someone who'd say it, sign before consequence: mostly in the interludes and the first replies of the next scene. One visible step per front per boundary, and no more than two fronts surfacing in a scene.
- A front changes circumstances inside the scenes the player is in. It never adds a scene, a route or an ending, and never answers a stakes question. What anyone could see or hear goes in [Caerwyn now](Caerwyn%20now.md); the why stays here.

## The Purveyor's man
**Who**: Master Josiah Sallow, under-purveyor for grain, with Big Hobb Mallory, the Crake brothers (hired for the Heron's wharf on loading nights), a lantern-boy, and the Heron's people, who are his by pay, not loyalty.
**Wants**: the clerk Prout's business closed for good, tidily; tonight's barge away on the ebb and tonight's page initialled; to walk in the procession and be thanked for feeding the feast; in time, the Royal Purveyor's chain.
**Resources**: the office's signature and keys; the Captain's goodwill (hampers, Assembly rations, a promised place at the head of the procession); an order of sale for "condemned stock, lawfully sold"; heron silver; the Crakes; the river.
**Limits**: he never fights himself and never goes down the river stairs. He's careful with paper and careless with coins, smells, servants and children. He doesn't know about the page in the boot, Cob in the loft, Moll's crown being traceable office silver, or Scrope being Prout's friend.

- [x] Prout is filed as self-murder at Sallow's request, before anyone has looked. *Shows as*: Corporal Tull's written order.
- [ ] **If Prout goes into the Captain's book as a murder** (the granary ends "The case is made" or "Murder by persons unknown"; `prout_death` "murder, Sallow named" or "murder, unnamed"): by evening the Captain has mentioned it to Sallow at the palace, as one gentleman to another. Sergeant Kell, the one he lent the King's investigator, has signed the clerk in as a murder, and he'll have it straightened out after the Assembly. Sallow is wary, not warned: he knows who is asking, not what they have. Before the tenth bell he sends the Crakes down the river stairs to find out who's been asking after his porter, and has a second lantern hung at the Heron's land door. *Shows as*: two big men alike as bollards at the pump on the stairs, asking who's been asking about Big Hobb (they're barred from the Rat); later, two lanterns at the Heron's land door.
- [ ] **If the wheat-ear crown is shown or spent where the office will hear of it** (the palace, its kitchens, the Corn Exchange, or a shopkeeper who sends word up the hill): Sallow learns that a granary girl kept his coin. *Shows as*: his yawning lantern-boy in the granary quarter, asking the granary rats where the girl in the shawl sleeps. If nobody stops it, a Crake comes asking after dark.
- [ ] **The loading night.** If Hobb ran to him from the Rat (`sallow_warned`), Sallow knows the King's investigator and a watch sergeant are asking about the loft: he comes at the midnight bell sharp and hurrying, with Hobb at his heels and the Crakes walking the wharf ahead of him, and wants the true book sewn into oilcloth and aboard the Patience first of all. If Hobb simply isn't at the river door (taken in at the Rat), Sallow finds his porter missing at the bell, and that alone makes him wary: the Crakes look along the wharf, and the loading is hurried. Otherwise he comes on time, finds fault, initials the page and takes his share. At the second bell after midnight the Patience casts off on the ebb with the King's grain, unless someone stops her. *Shows as*: the Crakes on the wharf before the bell; the Master early, or asking after his porter; by morning the Heron shut and swept, and the empty wheat-ear sacks in the river, weighted with stones.
- [ ] **Procession morning**, unless he's in a cell or has fled: he walks fourth with the Purveyor's party and the garlanded grain cart. If he knows the true book has left the Heron, he's at the palace before dawn to get his story in first: the order of sale, "condemned stock", a ledger stolen by intruders, and a word with the Captain. *Shows as*: Sallow in the long gallery with the Purveyor's party and a folio of papers, courteous and ready.

**Doom**: the office "saves" the Assembly feast with the King's own grain, bought back at Assembly prices on credit his friends downriver arrange. He's thanked for it, and has the chain when old Sir Gilbert goes. Prout stays in the pit, or in the book as persons unknown.
**Stakes**: Will Hobb live to swear to what he did? Will Moll keep her crown, and her neck? Will Kell's notebook count for more than the Captain's book?
**Ticked by**: `hanged-clerk` case-made and open-case (the other endings leave him unhurried tonight: one lantern, on time, bored); `drowned-rat` hobb-fled and taken-in; `heron-warehouse` barge-sails, proof-taken and caught.
**If he's arrested at the Heron** (`caught-in-the-act`), strike the front out. In its place, the office closes ranks the next morning: his order of sale, Sir Gilbert's name on it, and the Captain's surprise. *Shows as*: the Purveyor's party walking without him, the grain cart still in garlands. **If the Heron burns or the barge sinks** (`up-in-smoke`), his grain is gone, and he blames the goblins, then the fire, then the factor.

## The Captain's book
**Who**: Captain Aubrey Bunce, commander of the watch, with Corporal Tull, the constables and the dead-cart.
**Wants**: the route lined and everything else closed before the bells, without fuss; to ride at the head of the procession, as Master Sallow promised him; a short report and a quiet life.
**Resources**: the Captain's book, where his word stands; the whole watch, all of it on the route; the cells and the cold room; the Assizes after the Assembly.
**Limits**: his men are honest, tired and underpaid, and none of them is bent. He won't cross the palace without the King's say-so. He isn't bought, just obliging (see [the City Watch: the truth](City%20Watch%20%28truth%29.md)).

- [x] Prout is called a suicide before anyone has looked, and the dead-cart is ordered for noon.
- [ ] **Noon on the eve**: the dead-cart comes. Unless a sergeant signs Prout in as a murder, he goes out of the north gate and into the pit under quicklime by sundown, and nobody asks again. *Shows as*: the dead-cart's bell going through the market towards the north gate.
- [ ] **If a sergeant signs Prout in as a murder**, he goes to the cold room under a sheet to wait for the surgeon. Tull tells the Captain on the route by the afternoon bells. The Captain's first instinct is to make it go away without fuss: he wants Kell's report "after the Assembly", and he mentions it to Master Sallow, which ticks *The Purveyor's man*. *Shows as*: a constable sent with the Captain's word for Kell; granary porters telling anyone that a sergeant signed the clerk in over the Captain's word. **If a granary child is taken in for it instead**, the Captain gladly files a murder that embarrasses nobody. *Shows as*: the market saying a granary brat did it, and the granary rats saying otherwise, loudly.
- [ ] **Through the night** the whole watch lines the route, the river watch with it. Whatever happens at the wharves happens without the watch: Kell's whistle brings Kell. Anyone put in the cells tonight is locked in and left, with the key in the pocket of whoever brought him. *Shows as*: nobody behind the watch-house counter.
- [ ] **Procession morning**: the Captain rides at the head of the procession, very pleased. If an officer of the household was taken up in the night, his second instinct takes over: he has suspected the man all along, and says so to anyone. *Shows as*: the Captain on his horse in the palace courtyard.

**Doom**: the Captain's book stands. Prout lies in the pit as a suicide, Kell's report is read after the Assembly and filed, and the Captain rides at the head of the procession, grateful to the Purveyor's office for the rations.
**Stakes**: Will Kell's notebook count for more than the Captain's book? Will anyone in the watch go down the river stairs for a gentleman?
**Ticked by**: every `hanged-clerk` ending; `drowned-rat` taken-in; `heron-warehouse` caught-in-the-act.

## Dear bread
**What**: the price of bread and the city's patience: nobody's plan, and everybody's grievance. Behind it are the poor harvest, the palace buying first, the "lost" wagons, the factors who chalk up the price, the bakers who shrink the loaf before they raise it, and Saltmere barges going downriver full. Porters on eight pennies a day pay for it, and so do carters and granary rats. The factors, the Saltmere buyers and the office do well out of it.
**Clock**: a tug-of-war. Time and bad news fill it; good news empties it.

- [x] Tuppence a loaf, a penny dearer than at harvest. "Lost to goblins" stopped being funny about a fortnight ago.
- [ ] **The noon and evening bells on the eve**: the Corn Exchange chalks up a rise each time, and by evening the tuppenny loaf is smaller. *Shows as*: the price board, with something rude written beside it by evening; Nan's eel stew thinner; Cob hungrier.
- [ ] **The afternoon of the eve**: a preacher at the market cross calls dear bread a judgement on the bunting. *Shows as*: the sermon, preached to a bread queue.
- [ ] **After dark on the eve**: a crowd at the Corn Exchange board, and a cabbage thrown at a Purveyor's wagon taking flour up to the palace. *Shows as*: by morning, a dented wheat-ear canvas at the palace service entrance.
- [ ] **Procession morning**: threepence at the bakers who open, and people along the route who've come to shout "lost to goblins!" at the Purveyor's grain cart. *Shows as*: the shouting, heard through the King's windows.

**Doom**: a bread riot on the procession route, in front of the delegations.
**Ticks down if** people hear the King's "lost" grain has been found (an under-purveyor taken up at the wharves, or the King acting on the proof): then the crowd asks the grain cart "Found the goblins, then?" **Ticks up if** grain burns or sinks (`up-in-smoke`): "lost to goblins" becomes "lost to fire", and bread jumps.
**Stakes**: Who will Caerwyn blame: the goblins, Saltmere or the office? Will the procession pass without a riot?
**Ticked by**: time, at each boundary on the eve; `heron-warehouse` up-in-smoke (up) and caught-in-the-act (down, by morning); `kings-morning` justice (down).

## The Assembly week
**Who**: Marshal Garran Holt, who keeps the timetable to the minute and commands the palace guard; King Aldren, the host; the Treasury and its levy; the Purveyor's office and its feast.
**Wants**: the procession out of the gate at the ninth bell, and the Concord renewed without a scene in front of the delegations. The King, above all, wants not to have to decide anything that might upset anyone.
**Underneath**: the Treasury is waiting on Saltmere's credit, which was coming upriver with a courier and hasn't come: Sallow paid the goblins to take her at the ford (see [the Grain Crisis: the truth](Grain%20Crisis%20%28truth%29.md)), unless she got through (`warren-hostage` completed the `saltmere-courier` quest). That's why the levy is collected so keenly, and why the office's promise to see the feast fed is so welcome at the palace.

- [x] **The eve, morning**: the delegations have arrived, every official is at the palace, and the whole watch is lining the route. "After the Assembly."
- [ ] **The eve, afternoon**: rehearsals in the Great Hall. The gate takes written messages for the Marshal, and he reads them by evening. He acts at first light on whatever he can act on without the King's word: he sends a page for whoever wrote, and puts a guard on whatever they named. He keeps his guard at the palace while the delegations dine, and arrests no officer of the household on a letter. *Shows as*: gate guards who've been told the King will see the investigator in the morning.
- [ ] **The eve, after dark**: the palace is shut to everyone but the delegations' dinner and the procession rehearsal. *Shows as*: lit windows, carriages, a band rehearsing.
- [ ] **Procession morning**: half an hour before the ninth bell the King, half into his robes, sees the investigator in his dressing room. At the ninth bell the procession leaves the gate, whatever has been said; then the Assembly sits, and then the feast. *Shows as*: a palace page at first light; the bells.

**Doom**: everything goes off on time. The Concord is renewed over a feast the office fed with the King's own grain, and anything that might upset anyone waits until after the Assembly.
**Stakes**: Will the King decide anything before the ninth bell?
**Ticked by**: time only. The public timetable is in [The Centennial Assembly](Centennial%20Assembly.md).

## The river tonight
**What**: the river in winter spate, and the docks on the night before they shut for the procession. The Saltmere factors want their barges loaded and away before then. The porters and lightermen are paid off early with no work tomorrow. The river watch, whose patch the stairs are, is up on the route.

- [x] The river is high, fast and cold, and Saltmere barges have been loading at the Heron by night all month.
- [ ] **Dusk on the eve**: the river pays off early, and since tomorrow's wages are lost, tonight's are drunk. *Shows as*: paid-off porters crowding the stairs; the Rat packed.
- [ ] **The eleventh bell**: the night shift goes down, and the Heron's gang and its hoist man are due at the river door. *Shows as*: lanterns going down the stairs; the Heron's hoist working.
- [ ] **Midnight to the second bell after**: the tide turns, and fog comes off the water. At the second bell the ebb runs and the Patience casts off. *Shows as*: bargees singling up their lines; a stern lantern going downriver.
- [ ] **Procession morning**: the docks are shut and the wharves quiet. What went into the river in the night turns up downriver, or doesn't. *Shows as*: empty wharves; mudlarks at the low-water mark.

**Doom**: whatever and whoever goes into the river tonight is gone by morning. Every Assembly somebody falls in.
**Stakes**: Will the Patience sail? Will anyone go into the river who shouldn't?
**Ticked by**: time; `heron-warehouse` barge-sails and up-in-smoke.

## Bills still to come due
A hard ending or a daft act sends its bill later. Each has an "if" and where it will show; play adds more, and strikes them out when they're paid.
- **If Nan threw the investigator out of the Rat** (`thrown-out`): by the eleventh bell the stairs have it that the sergeant's friend got Manners. If they go back, they're barred until they've paid for whatever broke, at Nan's valuation.
- **If a granary child was taken in on the investigator's word** (`wrong-man`): the other one has gone to ground, and every granary rat knows whose word it was.
- **If the investigator was thrown in the river or locked up at the Heron** (`caught`): Sallow believes he's rid of them. If they walk into the palace in the morning, the first look on his face is the true one.
- **If the Heron burned or the Patience sank** (`up-in-smoke`): somebody must explain it on the morning of the Assembly, and the Marshal asks before the King does.
