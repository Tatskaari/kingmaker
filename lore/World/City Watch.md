# The City Watch

The watch keeps the King's peace in Caerwyn's streets: a Captain who likes a parade, a handful of sergeants, corporals and constables, and never enough of any of them. Rain capes and truncheons, and notebooks for the sergeants.

**Why it's like this**
- The city pays for the watch and has never paid for much of one.
- The palace looks after its own. Officers of the royal household answer to the palace, so the watch keeps the streets and leaves the palace's people to the palace.
- The Captain, Aubrey Bunce, is a fine figure on a horse, fond of a parade, the palace and a quiet life. His favourite words are "without fuss": he likes a short report, and a case closed before it's opened.

**Who does well out of it**: the Captain, who gets a quiet life and a hamper from the Purveyor's office every quarter-day (the men have never seen more of it than the straw); and the palace's officers, whom the watch can't touch.
**Who pays for it**: a sergeant who signs a death in against the Captain's word, and answers to him for it; the dead nobody signs for; constables lining the route all night on watch pay; and the river stairs, which the watch leaves to the river watch.

**The rules, and who keeps them**
- **The Captain's book**: every death in the city goes in it as natural, accident, self-murder or murder, and the Captain's word stands. A sergeant may sign a body in as a murder instead, and answers to the Captain for it.
- **A murder** goes to the cold room at the watch house to wait for the watch surgeon. **A suicide** goes into the pit outside the north gate, under quicklime, by sundown on the day it's found. No grave, no stone, and after that nobody asks again.
- **Arrests**: a sergeant who sees a crime done may arrest whoever does it, gentleman or not, out loud and with the proper words. Anyone else can be taken up on evidence: his own admission, a witness who'll swear, a wound that matches. An officer of the royal household, the Purveyor's office included, can't be touched without a warrant under the royal seal unless he's caught in the act.
- **Children** the watch takes up go to the Mending House by the north gate.
- **The river stairs** belong to the river watch, which walks them in fours after dark, or not at all.

**How it touches ordinary people**: the dead-cart (a handcart with a long ladder lashed on top, pushed by two constables, with a bell); watchmen walking in pairs after dark; and a bell on the watch-house counter to ring when nobody's behind it.

**What people say, and who says it**
- Nan Quill, and the river stairs with her: in thirty years the watch has never once come down the stairs for a gentleman.
- The stairs: Sergeant Kell writes everything down, takes nothing from anybody, and is still a sergeant because her reports say what happened.
- Lightermen: the watch are bluebottles who fine you a shilling for mooring where everyone moors.
- Granary rats: the watch works for good coats, and believes a good coat over a granary kid every time.
- Corporal Tull: an order stands until the Captain writes a new one, or the Sarge signs for something and takes it on herself.

**What's happening now**: the whole watch is out lining the Assembly route, hanging bunting, moving carts and chasing pigs, and it stays out through the night. The Captain is at the palace seeing to the route, and the watch surgeon is on duty at the palace. Until after the Assembly nobody can be spared for anything else.
