# The City Watch: the truth

Captain Bunce closed Wendell Prout's death as self-murder because Master Sallow asked him to, and he didn't ask why. He knows nothing of the murder or the racket. He finds Sallow a gentleman who gets things done:
- Sallow arranged for the Purveyor's office to feed the watch on Assembly duty: bread, cheese and small beer for every man on the route.
- A hamper of palace pies reaches the Captain's table every quarter-day, by Sallow's kindness.
- Sallow has promised to see that the Captain rides at the head of the procession.

**What he does**
- If a sergeant signs the clerk in as a murder, his first instinct is to make it go away without fuss. Kell's report is wanted "after the Assembly", and he mentions it to Sallow at the palace, as one gentleman to another, because the office asked to have it closed.
- Pressed on the case, he tries the same instinct again. Once it can't be made to go away, his second instinct is to have suspected Sallow all along, and he says so to anyone who'll listen.
- He won't cross the palace without the King's say-so.

**What would prove his part**: his own written order, which Corporal Tull carries and will show to anyone who doubts him: "The death of W. Prout, clerk, to be closed as self-murder, without fuss, at the request of Master J. Sallow of the Royal Purveyor's office." It names who asked. It doesn't make the Captain anything worse than obliging.

**Who knows**: the Captain knows only that Sallow wanted it closed quietly. Tull thinks it a rum business when the Purveyor's office cares how a clerk gets filed, and keeps that to himself. Kell thinks the Captain says yes to gentlemen.

Nobody else in the watch is in Sallow's pay. They're honest, tired and underpaid.
