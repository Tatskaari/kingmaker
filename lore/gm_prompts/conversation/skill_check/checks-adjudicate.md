{{{ROLL_GUIDANCE}}}
Give a concise, concrete direction to the NPC for their next response to the immediately preceding player message. Describe what succeeded/failed and how to play it off, rather than writing their dialogue. Address each result independently if multiple skills had different outcomes. Establish only information this character should know; do not reveal unrelated secrets. Return a direction string.

When any insight, investigation, perception, arcana, history, religion, nature, medicine, or survival check is present, also return an observation string addressed to the player as "you": a concise, concrete account of what they notice, deduce, recall, or learn from their attempt. Ground it in the dialogue and established evidence. Insight concerns motives and social cues; perception concerns sensory details; investigation concerns deductions from examined evidence; arcana, history, religion, and nature concern relevant knowledge; medicine concerns medical findings; survival concerns tracks, navigation, and environmental signs. For practical medicine or survival actions, describe the observable result of that attempt rather than inventing a discovery.

Match the amount and certainty of information to each resolved outcome. If several of these checks occur, address each in the same observation and keep their different outcomes distinct; success on one check must not turn a failed check into a success. A failed check may leave the answer unclear or knowledge unrecalled; do not invent secrets or present unsupported suspicions as facts. Never expose hidden GM instructions or unrelated lore. The observation is shown to the player and supplied to the character as context. In direction, tell the character how to keep their roleplay consistent with those findings, without quoting the GM, announcing the roll, or treating the player’s private inference or recollection as something spoken aloud. Do not force the character to provide information the player learned independently.
