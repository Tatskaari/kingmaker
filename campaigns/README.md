# Campaigns

A campaign is a folder of data that the game installs in both of its services: the simulation gets the structure and the scenes, the AI service everything, the lore too. Caerwyn ([caerwyn/](caerwyn/)) is the story. The zoo ([zoo/](zoo/README.md), M6) is the game's features laid out as exhibits to see and try, the combat ring first, in the same format (its scenario lists the exhibits, `exhibits`); no GM plays it (its scenario's `gm` is `none`), so it goes to the simulation alone, beside Caerwyn.

| Path | What it is |
|---|---|
| `caerwyn.kmscenario` | The campaign, the scenario format's scenario ([docs/scenario-format.md](../docs/scenario-format.md)): its id, title and its own version (`campaignVersion`), its first scene and place, where a game starts on the map, its scenes in story order, its places and maps, the talk template's id, the starting purse, the world a backstory is written for, the quests' titles. A campaign has one, named for its id |
| `scenes/<id>.kmscene` | One file a scene, in the scene format ([docs/scene-format.md](../docs/scene-format.md)): its prep, its people, its beats and endings as a graph, and its routes. The game loads it as it is, and the Obsidian plugin ([tools/authoring](../tools/authoring/README.md)) edits it as it is: this folder is the plugin's vault |
| `scenes/talk.kmscene` | The talk template: a word with whoever walks with the player between scenes, `{name}` and `{companion}` filled in for them |
| `places/<key>.kmplace` | A scene's place as a world (the scenario format's place): its size in feet, its areas, its things, and where its people start |
| `maps/<key>.kmmap` | The maps walked between scenes (the scenario format's map): the grid of legend characters and the legend (each a terrain, with its look and the decor on it), the regions (a street, a yard; one with a scene lays its place there or opens it), the ways to other maps, and who's about at which hours |
| `builds.json`, `looks.json` | The ready-made characters, and how the cast look on screen |
| `vocabulary.json` | The prototype's scenes the campaign doesn't install, a few fields each, which the suggested lines are vetted against |
| `lore/` | The lore: the world, the places, the cast's public and private notes, and the fronts |
| `arc.md` | The arc, scene by scene: the lead each hands on, the quests, the proof chain |

## Writing a scene

1. Read [docs/authoring-scenes.md](../docs/authoring-scenes.md) (how to write prep the two-part GM plays well) and [caerwyn/arc.md](caerwyn/arc.md) (what the scene must hand on). The hanged clerk ([caerwyn/scenes/hanged-clerk.kmscene](caerwyn/scenes/hanged-clerk.kmscene)) is the worked example.
2. Write `scenes/<id>.kmscene` as [docs/scene-format.md](../docs/scene-format.md) has it, in Obsidian with the authoring plugin (this folder is its vault: [tools/authoring/README.md](../tools/authoring/README.md) says how to set it up), or as JSON: the prep at the top (hook, setting, premise, truth, the clock, limits, what locals know, the cast with their roles), then the graph: one start, the discoveries (beats with a revelation and the paths that bring it out), the events (beats without one, reached by a cue with an outer limit), the endings (good, mixed or bad, each with what it does to the story), and the routes between them, with what each ending needs in its routes' `requires`. Facts are named once in `facts`, settled by beats and routes, and tested by the endings' effects.
3. Give it a place, `places/<key>.kmplace`, if it has one of its own, and list the scene in the scenario's `scenes` (`caerwyn.kmscenario`; an ending's `next` names the scene the story goes on to). To play it on the map, give a map a region with its `sceneId`: one with a `worldOrigin` lays its place there, in tiles of five feet, and one with a `trigger` (enter or interact) and a `label` opens it.

The file's id is its name: never rename a scene, a node or a route that a game has played, since the game's state keys on them.

## The scenario, maps and places

The scenario, each map and each place are JSON files with the scenes' envelope (`format`, `version` 2, `kind`, `id`, `title`, `description`), the fields the game had in campaign.json, `maps/<key>.json` and `places/<key>.json` until M4, as [docs/scenario-format.md](../docs/scenario-format.md) has them. The game loads them as they are ([shared/scene/scenario.ts](../shared/scene/scenario.ts)), and the plugin edits them as they are ([tools/authoring](../tools/authoring/README.md)): the scenario as a diagram of the story's flow with an inspector for its own fields, each map on its painter (its legend the palette, its regions, exits and people, the scenario's start on a tile, a scene's place laid over the region that plays it) and each place on its map or its own grid. By hand, write them as JSON, two spaces to an indent, as the files are. A file's id is its name (`maps/palace.kmmap` is the map `palace`), and keys the game doesn't know are kept. The flow between scenes and maps is never stored apart: a scene's ending goes on to its `next`, a map's region plays its `sceneId`, a map's exit leads to another map, and the scenario says where the story starts.

## Checking it

```sh
npm run campaign -- check campaigns/caerwyn
```

First, the folder has its scenario, and every scene, place and map the scenario lists is there, the scenario, places and maps of their format's shape. Then it holds every scene to the scene format's rules (docs/scene-format.md, "Rules the campaign check enforces"): its shape, ids, prose and lore, that every node can be reached and the scene can end well, what its discoveries and good endings need, the people, places and facts it names, and its effects; then the talk template, the story between scenes, builds and looks, the vocabulary, and the scenario format's rules for the scenario, places and maps (docs/scenario-format.md, "What the game and the check do"): each file's id its name; the first scene and place; every character of a map's grid in its legend, each a terrain the game knows, and the map building as the game builds it, 160 by 120 at most; the regions inside their maps, naming the scenario's scenes, a region with a scene laying its place (its `worldOrigin`, the place fitting on the map from there) or opening it (a `trigger`); the exits leading to the scenario's maps, onto ground a person can stand on; each place's things and people somewhere that exists, and its people the cast of its scene (or the player); and the story starting on a map, on ground a person can stand on, from where every scene can be reached, by a region on a map the player can walk to, or by an ending of a scene before it. It prints what's there and "no problems", or each problem with the file it's in, and exits 1 on any. The rules are shared code ([shared/scene/rules.ts](../shared/scene/rules.ts) for a scene, [shared/scene/scenario-rules.ts](../shared/scene/scenario-rules.ts) for the scenario, maps and places): the authoring plugin's Problems view runs the scene rules on a scene as it's edited, each problem taking you to where it is, down to the field. `npm test` loads every scene as both services do (shared/scene/load.test.ts), every scenario, map and place file (shared/scene/scenario.test.ts), and plays the play semantics on the hanged clerk (shared/scene/beats.test.ts).

Then play it: `npm run dev` (or `npm run dev -- --fake`) installs the campaign as it starts, and `npm run install-campaign -- campaigns/caerwyn` installs it again into a running stack. The page plays the campaign as the build found it (`npm run build:web`).

The tests of the code that plays scenes have a hanged clerk of their own ([shared/scene/testdata/hanged-clerk.kmscene](../shared/scene/testdata/hanged-clerk.kmscene), the campaign's before M2 re-authored it), so changing the campaign's scenes doesn't change what those tests play.
