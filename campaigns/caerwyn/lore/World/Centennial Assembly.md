# The Centennial Assembly

Every hundred years the kingdoms come to one court and say, in public, who their common sovereign is. This winter they've come to King Aldren's palace in Caerwyn, and nothing else in the city gets done.

**Why they must**: the custom is older than anyone's grandmother's grandmother. This is the first Assembly since King Edric the Peacemaker made his Concord a generation ago. Under the Concord each kingdom keeps its own rulers and laws, pays tribute, and brings its quarrels to the King instead of to war. Recognising Aldren renews it.

**The delegations, all arrived, and their banners**
- **Caerwyn**, the host (roads, courts and garrisons): King Edric's open hand.
- **Nine Furrows** (wizards of the ancient university, who see to weather, wards and grain): the wheat-sheaf.
- **Kläggenheim** (dwarven miners and engineers, and old King Gurt): crossed axes.
- **Saltmere** (ships, warehouses, insurance and credit, and Prince Peregrine): the salt-gull. The heron on the sign of the Heron warehouse is one Saltmere grain factor's own house mark, not Saltmere's.

**Who wants what from it**
- **King Aldren**: to be recognised again as common sovereign under the Concord, with nobody upset.
- **The delegations**: their laws kept and their tribute argued down, and to be seen to argue it.
- **Marshal Holt**: everything on time, to the minute.
- **The Purveyor's office**: the feast is theirs to supply, grain crisis or no grain crisis, and they mean to be thanked for it.
- **The city**: cheaper bread and a day off. It is getting a procession.

**Who does well out of it**: innkeepers and lodging-house keepers, chandlers and bunting-sellers, anyone with brandy to sell to a delegation's steward, and anyone who'll scrub or turn a spit in the palace kitchens, where extra hands are fed.
**Who pays, and resents it**: travellers paying the Treasury's shilling a head; carters and drovers paying the Marshal's two pennies a weapon on a road where goblins shoot oxen; porters and lightermen, whose docks shut for a day without pay; the watch, lining the route through the night; and anyone who has noticed the gilt and the bread on the same day.

**The rules, and who keeps them**
- **The Treasury's Assembly levy**: a shilling a head, "to defray the costs of the Centennial", collected from "such travellers as are able and willing" at the gates and at Garrow's toll bridge.
- **Marshal Holt's arms order**: two pennies for each weapon borne on the King's Road in Assembly week. Weapons peace-bonded with red cord and wax go free.
- **The timetable** is the Marshal's. The procession leaves at the ninth bell whatever anyone has said.
- **"After the Assembly"**: every office in the palace gives the same answer to every request this week.

**The week, bell by bell**
- **The eve**: every official is at the palace, the whole watch is out lining the procession route, and rehearsals run in the Great Hall. The docks shut tomorrow for the procession, so the river pays off early tonight.
- **The eve, after dark**: the palace is shut to everyone but the delegations' dinner and the procession rehearsal. The watch lines the route all night.
- **Procession morning**: the delegations form up under their banners in the palace courtyard. At the ninth bell the procession leaves the palace gate and winds through the market square: the three visiting delegations under their banners, then the Purveyor's party fourth with a grain cart in garlands, "for the people's confidence", and then the court, and the King under Caerwyn's open hand.
- **Then** the Assembly sits in the Great Hall, and after it comes the feast.
- **After the Assembly**: officials, the watch surgeon and the granary warden can be found again, the Assizes sit, and a case can be laid before the King, who signs nothing that might upset anyone.

**What people say, and who says it**
- The delegations' servants: everyone has come to recognise one sovereign and argue about the wording.
- Watchmen on the route: which end of the route is the beginning.
- Porters on the river stairs: a day's pay lost for a procession they'll be standing in the cold to watch.
- The palace kitchens: the cushions for the Assembly seats, which haven't arrived, and the under-purveyor for linen, who is having a very bad week about them.

**What's happening now**: the delegations have arrived and the route is being lined. The procession leaves at the ninth bell tomorrow. How things stand at this hour is in [Caerwyn now](Caerwyn%20now.md).
