# The Grain Crisis

Bread in Caerwyn is tuppence a loaf, a penny dearer than at harvest, and a porter earns about eight pennies a day. Ask anyone why and they'll say goblins.

**Why it's dear**
- The harvest was poor after a wet summer, and the Nine Furrows granaries are thin.
- The palace buys first. By the King's right of purveyance the Royal Purveyor's office buys at the King's price, so farmers bring the palace what they must and sell the rest where it fetches more.
- The palace's own grain keeps going missing. A dozen wagons bound for the King's Granary have been "lost to goblins" on the King's Road this past month, each one written off in the Royal Granary Roll and countersigned by the Purveyor's office. What the palace loses on the road, it buys again in the market.
- Saltmere pays better than Caerwyn, and Saltmere barges have been going downriver full.
- The Assembly feast must be fed regardless. The office says it has the matter in hand.

**Who does well out of it**: the grain factors at the Corn Exchange, who chalk up a new price twice a day; the Saltmere buyers on the wharves, whose barges go downriver low in the water; and the Purveyor's office, which expects to be thanked for feeding the feast at all.
**Who pays for it**: porters, carters and granary rats, for whom a loaf is a quarter of a day's work; farmers, who sell to the King at the King's price; the granary porters, with bays standing empty and less to carry; and the Treasury, which writes off every lost wagon and this week is collecting a shilling a head from travellers.

**The rules, and who keeps them**
- Palace grain lost on the road is written off in the Royal Granary Roll, and the Purveyor's office countersigns the entry. Nobody else checks.
- The Corn Exchange chalks the day's prices on its board at the noon bell and again at the evening bell. The factors set them, and the Exchange's clerk chalks them up.
- A baker keeps the loaf at tuppence as long as he can by making it smaller. The market warden fines short weight, and this week he's at the palace with every other official.
- The watch keeps the King's peace, not the price of bread.

**How it touches ordinary people**: queues at the bakers from first light; a smaller loaf for the same tuppence; thinner stew on the river stairs. And "lost to goblins" is now what Caerwyn says about anything that goes missing, whether it's a hat, a husband or the rent. It stopped being funny about a fortnight ago.

**What people say, and who says it**
- Carters: they won't take the King's Road after dark without a patrol, and there are no patrols.
- Farmers at market: they've never seen goblins carry off a whole wagon of wheat, and wonder where a goblin would sell one.
- Dock-porters: Saltmere barges are taking grain downriver while Caerwyn goes short. They take Saltmere's money for loading them all the same.
- The palace kitchens: the feast will be fed because the office says so, and the office's word is stamped on its pennies.
- The preacher at the market cross: dear bread is a judgement on the bunting.
