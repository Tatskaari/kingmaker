# The Grain Crisis: the truth

There are goblins on the King's Road, but they aren't what's taking the wagons.

## The racket
- **Who**: Master Josiah Sallow, under-purveyor for grain in the Royal Purveyor's office. A good coat, a silver-topped cane, a wheat-ear signet ring and too much lavender water. His muscle is Big Hobb Mallory, a dock-porter the size of a door; on the wharf he hires the Crake brothers.
- **How**: palace grain wagons on the King's Road are written off in the Royal Granary Roll as "lost to goblins", each countersigned "J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor". The grain goes by night, still in the palace's wheat-ear sacks, down the Wharf Road to the Heron warehouse, where it's re-sacked under the heron mark of a Saltmere grain factor and sold downriver. If anyone asks, it's condemned stock lawfully sold, on an order of sale in Sallow's own hand. Nobody has ever asked.
- **The true book**: Sallow and the factor don't trust each other, so both insisted on a ledger kept by a clerk they both pay, Mister Lemuel Scrope. It records every "lost" wagon by its Roll number, with Sallow's initials on every page.
- **The goblins**: Sallow pays the hedge-goblins of the burnt mill to be seen on the road and to raid now and then, so the story holds. Their war-cry is "Be seen!", because that's what they're paid for, and they never take the grain. Two days before the Assembly their war-boss, Grisk, brings the band down to Garrow's toll bridge for the biggest show yet (the prologue). He pays them in heron pennies from the factor's cashbox, and since the book has to balance, Scrope enters it: on the last page of each month, in the same careful hand, "Hedge-folk, the usual: forty heron pennies." The book answers Prout's question. To the goblins Sallow is a longlegs in a grey hood who smells of lavender.
- **Why**: money, and standing. An Assembly short of grain and money is one the Purveyor's office gets to save, and Sallow means to be the man who saves it, with grain he happens to have, and to be thanked with the Royal Purveyor's chain.
- **The Saltmere courier** (background): in the same grey hood, Sallow paid Chief Gubbin to snatch Hester Quayle, the Saltmere courier, at a ford on the King's Road. Her sealed letter is Saltmere's credit for the King's treasury: the loan that pays for the Assembly. Unless she got through, the Treasury is short, which is why the levy is collected so keenly.

## Prout
Wendell Prout, under-clerk of the Granary Roll, read the Corn Exchange's wagon lists, where every load sold in the city is weighed and entered, and found the "lost" wagons weighed in at the Heron two days after each loss, as condemned palace stock sold by order of the Purveyor's office. He pencilled the weights beside the entries, razored the page out of the Roll and sewed it into his boot. He asked Sergeant Kell how a clerk reports false entries, and wrote to the Royal Purveyor through the proper channels. Sallow opens the Royal Purveyor's letters. In the small hours of the eve of the Assembly, Sallow and Hobb caught him in the tally loft, killed him and hoisted him on the sack hoist to look like a suicide. At first light Sallow paid a granary girl to say she'd seen him jump, then asked the Watch Captain to close it quietly.

## Who knows what
- **Sallow**: all of it. **Hobb**: the murder, the wagons and the Heron's night routine. He carries a brass token for its river door.
- **The Heron's people** (Hep Grist and her gang, Saul Tench, Scrope) know whose grain they're handling and have never said so. Scrope knows why Wendell Prout is dead.
- **Gubbin**: a grey hood, lavender and heron pennies, but no name.
- **Abel Fennick**, the Corn Exchange's clerk, let Prout read the wagon lists. He doesn't know why Prout wanted them.
- **Nan Quill** knows the gentleman's name and what the porters say about the Heron's grain, and won't sell it.
- **Cob** heard the murder from his nest in the loft. **Moll** took Sallow's silver and heard his name. **Kell** knows Prout came to her. **The Captain** knows only that Sallow wanted it closed quietly.

## The proof
- Prout's page, and the Roll chained to the tally desk with the page razored out.
- Moll's wheat-ear crown: office purse silver.
- Heron pennies, new this month and paid out only at the Heron: in Nan's jar marked FOREIGN, in Dilly's winnings, in Cob's pocket, and in the goblins' warren.
- Hobb's token and his word, and the words of the Heron's people if they'll swear for the Crown.
- The Corn Exchange's wagon lists: the "lost" wagons weighed in at the Heron.
- Garrow's toll ledger at the King's toll bridge: every "lost" palace wagon crossing inward to the city, laden, under wheat-ear canvas, and paying its penny, on its day. It shows they weren't lost on the King's Road, not where they went.
- Sallow's own order of sale, which calls "condemned stock, lawfully sold" the very wagons his countersignature in the Roll calls "lost to goblins".
- The true book, which would hang him.
