# Caerwyn now

What anyone in town could see or hear, hour by hour, from the evening two days before the Centennial Assembly, through the eve, to the morning after. Go by the scene's time and by what has actually happened: an "If" line holds only if it did. Talk belongs to the people named, and is said once, by someone it matters to.

## Evening, two days before
- **The town**: the delegations arriving all day, the bunting half up, and the city gates shutting at the ninth bell this week. The King takes supper on a tray in his parlour while the delegations dine in the Great Hall, and goes down for the toasts at the ninth bell.
- **The road**: no patrols on the King's Road all week but one, sent out at the King's own wish to bring the Assembly's cushions in from the Nine Furrows.
  - If the goblins were broken at Garrow's toll bridge: by dark the palace guardroom has it that a traveller saved the cushion patrol, and the cushions, from goblins and a war-boss on a worg, and the gate guards are telling anyone who'll listen. The Kläggenheim delegation's baggage guard tell it too, in Kläggenheim, with gestures.
- **The talk**:
  - The palace kitchens: the cushions have come, three hundred in the King's green, and the under-purveyor for linen has had to lie down.
  - Carters: goblins at Garrow's bridge, nine miles out, and never so close before.

## Morning, the eve
- **The town**: bunting going up on every rope that will hold it, and the whole watch out lining the procession route. Every official is at the palace, and the answer to every request is "After the Assembly."
- **Bread**: tuppence a loaf, a penny dearer than at harvest, with queues at the bakers since first light. The Corn Exchange chalks up the day's prices at the noon bell.
- **The talk**:
  - Granary porters: a palace clerk hanged himself off the sack hoist behind the King's Granary. The Captain says so, and the dead-cart comes at noon.
  - Carters: nobody takes the King's Road after dark, with the patrols all called in to line the route.
  - Dock-porters: the docks shut tomorrow for the procession, so the river pays off early tonight.
  - The palace kitchens: the under-purveyor for linen is having a very bad week about the King's cushions.

## Afternoon, the eve
- **The town**: the route swept, the delegations' banners up round the market square, rehearsals at the palace. The palace gate takes written messages for the Marshal, who reads them by evening.
- **Bread**: the noon price on the Corn Exchange board is up again, and people stop to read it and grumble. A preacher at the market cross is calling dear bread a judgement on the bunting.
- **The clerk**:
  - If he went out on the dead-cart as a suicide: its bell goes through the market towards the north gate, and nobody looks up. He's in the pit by sundown.
  - If a sergeant signed him in as a murder: he went to the watch house under a sheet, not out of the north gate, and the granary porters tell anyone that a sergeant signed him in over the Captain's word.
  - If a granary child was taken in for it: the market says a granary brat did for the clerk and the watch has the child. The granary rats say otherwise, loudly.
- **The talk**: travellers at the gates grumbling about the Treasury's shilling a head; palace porters saying the King's cushions are still stuck in the wagon queue at the service entrance.

## Evening, the eve
- **The town**: lamps lit along the procession route. The palace is shut to everyone but the delegations' dinner and the procession rehearsal, and the King sees no one before morning.
- **Bread**: the evening chalking is up again since noon, and someone has written something rude beside it. The same tuppence buys a smaller loaf, and stew on the river stairs is thinner.
- **The river**: paid off early, so the Drowned Rat and every dive on the stairs is full by dusk. The river watch is up on the route with everyone else.
- **The talk**: on the stairs, Saltmere barges have been loading at the Heron by night all month and going downriver low in the water, while bread goes up.

## Night, the eve
- **The town**: the watch lines the route through the night. The market square is empty but for sweepers and a watchman asleep upright against a lamppost.
- **The river**: high and black, with fog coming off it after the eleventh bell, when the night porters go down to the shifts. At the downriver end the Heron's hoist is working over the Saltmere barge Patience, which sails on the ebb at the second bell after midnight.
- **The talk**: the Heron's gang grumble that they're on double pay and still can't buy bread.

## Procession morning
- **The town**: bells, a band tuning up, bunting everywhere, and the delegations forming up under their banners in the palace courtyard. At the ninth bell the procession leaves the palace gate for the market square, the Purveyor's party walking fourth with a grain cart in garlands, "for the people's confidence". The docks are shut.
- **Bread**: threepence, at the bakers who open.
- **The talk**:
  - Along the route: people who've come to shout "lost to goblins!" at the Purveyor's grain cart.
  - If the Heron burned or its barge sank in the night: the whole waterfront saw it, and "lost to goblins" has become "lost to fire".
  - If an under-purveyor was taken up at the wharves in the night: the palace porters have it by breakfast, and the Captain says he suspected the man all along.
  - If the barge sailed: the Heron's doors are shut, its floor is swept, and nobody on the wharves saw anything.

## The King's investigator
What Caerwyn says about them, a line for each public deed as it gets about, with who's saying it. To begin with nobody knows them, beyond the palace guardroom's tale of the traveller who saved the cushion patrol on the King's Road (if they did); the palace gate has been told the King will see them in the morning.
