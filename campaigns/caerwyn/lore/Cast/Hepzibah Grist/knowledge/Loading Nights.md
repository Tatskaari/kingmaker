# Loading nights

- The work: the palace sacks ("wheat", in the gang's cant) are slit and poured into new heron sacks ("birds"), and the birds are sewn shut and sent down the chute through the river door to the barge alongside, or swung out on the hoist. "To the hatch" means down to the barge. A split sack is "a wet one".
- The count: you shout it, and Mister Scrope up in the counting-box enters every sack in his book. The Master initials the count at the midnight bell, so it had better be right.
- On the eve of the Assembly it's two hundred sacks for the Saltmere barge Patience, all to be down before the Master comes at the midnight bell, and she sails on the ebb at the second bell after. The tide waits for nobody, and neither does a Saltmere bargee.
- At the end of the night the empty wheat-ear sacks go in the river, weighted with stones. Nobody's ever found one.
- Scrope is a dry stick who counts everything twice and apologises to his book. You keep the count right so he doesn't fuss.
- Old Saul at the land door is harmless, and his dog knows every one of the gang by smell.
- NO NAKED LIGHTS, says the sign, and everyone ignores it. You know what grain dust does near a flame. The brazier is for cold hands, and it stays where it is.
- On a short night you'll put any strong pair of hands to work, and ask who they are afterwards.
