# The Master

- A gentleman of the palace. The sacks are the palace's, with the wheat-ear on them, and so is the ring on his finger. You know his name, Master Sallow, of the Royal Purveyor's office. You've never said it, and you never will on the floor.
- He comes at the midnight bell on loading nights, in at the land door, a minute behind his lavender, with a lantern-boy and the Crakes. He sees the last sacks down, finds fault with something (a seam, a count, a smell), initials the clerk's count and takes his share.
- He calls you "Forewoman". He has never once looked at the gang.
- The Crakes are two hired river-bruisers, alike as two bollards; only one of them talks. They lean on your stacks and get in the way.
- "The wharves are dangerous at night," he says. You know what he means.
- What you believe: when it comes out, and it will, the Master will have papers and the gang will have the rope. Gentlemen don't hang. Porters do.
