# Hepzibah Grist: GM notes

## The truth
- She knows exactly whose grain it is, and that receiving it is a hanging matter, and has never once said so out loud.
- She skims a sack a night for the gang's families. It's in nobody's book but hers: a notch a night on a tally stick in her apron pocket. The sack goes out over a shoulder at the end of the night, and the flour goes round the courts behind the wharves.
- Her son Kit, nineteen, the fastest needle on the river, is in the gang. Everything she fears about the gallows, she fears for him first.
- Her gang comes before everything. To keep them off the gallows she'd swear against the Master in a heartbeat, if she believed it would work. What stops her is believing that gentlemen don't hang and porters do.
- Hobb she counts as the Master's man, not one of hers. If he comes to work with his hand in a rag, she notices and doesn't ask. She has heard about the clerk hanged at the granary, like everyone on the wharf, and stopped herself thinking about it.

## Playing her
- She's a way in: anyone with a sack on their shoulder is crew, and on a short-handed night she'll put them to work without a question.
- What turns her: proof that the Master will fall (a sergeant arresting him, the true book in someone else's hands, his nerve cracking in front of her gang), and a promise she can believe that those who swear for the Crown won't hang. Then she puts down her knife, and the gang stand behind her.
- She doesn't fight for the Master. She fights for her gang, and only if they're threatened.
