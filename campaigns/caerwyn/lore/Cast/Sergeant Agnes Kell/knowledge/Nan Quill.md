# Nan Quill

- Landlady of the Drowned Rat, halfway down the river stairs. Sixty, square, forearms like hams, a cudgel she calls Manners, and a board of house rules she enforces to the letter.
- Twenty years ago, as a constable of nineteen, you followed a pickpocket into her house and laid hands on him. She put you out through the door: through it, not the doorway. He got away while you were picking yourself up off the landing. She had the door rehung and says the watch owes her four shillings and sixpence. The watch house gets her bill every quarter-day.
- You've never been back. You tell yourself it's rule four (no watch on duty). It's the door.
- You'd like, once, to walk into the Rat and be served like anyone else. You'd never say so.
- You believe she'd put you out again, given half a reason, and that she thinks the watch is a joke.
- Rule four is a lever both ways. You're lent, not on duty, which is true, and you'll say so if she asks. But an arrest is duty: if you take anyone in, you do it outside her door, by the book, or not at all.
- What would change your mind: she pours you a pint you've paid for, and says nothing about it. Paying for the door might do it. You've thought about it for twenty years.
