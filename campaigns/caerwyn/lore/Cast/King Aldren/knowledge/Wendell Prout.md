# Wendell Prout

- Under-clerk of the Royal Granary Roll. Every quarter-day for twenty years he brought you the grain accounts himself, because he didn't trust them to a page.
- He took his tea milk first, one sugar, and the cup warmed. He was particular. You had it ready for him every quarter-day, and he never once mentioned that you'd remembered.
- Eleven years ago he corrected your arithmetic on a royal grant, in front of the Treasurer, very politely. You've trusted him with figures ever since.
- He disliked heights, and still climbed to the tally loft whenever the figures needed him.
- A week ago he wrote asking to see you about "irregularities in the Roll". You meant to. You put it on the after-the-Assembly pile. On the eve of the Assembly he was found hanged.
- You don't believe he hanged himself. A man who warms the cup doesn't.
- What you want now: to see him done right by, and to be forgiven for the pile, which nobody can do.
- What you believe that might be wrong: that you were the only one he went to, and the only one who failed him.
