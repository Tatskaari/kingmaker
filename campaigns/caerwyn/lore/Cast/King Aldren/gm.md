# King Aldren: GM notes

Purpose (behind the screen): the denouement's dilemma. He hears the investigator's account and decides, or doesn't, before the ninth bell. Whatever he decides in his dressing room happens.

## The truth
- A week before he died, Wendell Prout wrote asking for an audience about "irregularities in the Roll". The King read it, meant to see him, and put it on the after-the-Assembly pile, where it still is. He's ashamed of it, and it's why he asked for an investigator rather than leaving it to the Captain.
- Everyone Prout went to failed him the same way: the King (the pile), Sergeant Kell (she sent him to the palace) and the Royal Purveyor (whose letters Sallow opens). Neither the King nor Kell knows about the other.

## His secret, staged
- **Shows**: he knows a little too much about Prout's last week ("He'd asked to see me, you know"), stops, and pours tea nobody asked for.
- **Torn**: owning up makes him the King who looked away, in front of the people he asked to look for him.
- **Opens**: someone else owning up first (Kell, about her own part), or the investigator asking him straight why he chose them.
- **First**: "He wrote to me."
- **Then**: the letter, the pile, and "I meant to see him after the Assembly."

## What he doesn't know
- That Kell sent Prout to the palace three days before he died.
- That Sallow opens the Royal Purveyor's letters, and that Prout's report went to Sallow.
- That Sallow made himself indispensable this week on purpose.
- That the Marshal has an order already written, if the night gave him anything to act on.

## Under pressure
- First tea, then sympathy, then "after the Assembly".
- Asked to send for someone, he does at once ("Garran, ask Master Sallow to step in"): a small thing, and he likes to hear everyone.
- If Sallow is in the room, he hears Sallow out with relief: the offer to "look into irregularities" after the Assembly is the answer he wants. But he asks Sallow his small exact questions too, and he notices a man correcting the record on something he says he knows nothing about.
- Frightened, he goes into the royal "we".
- Given something he can hold and someone who'll stand behind it in front of him, he puts his cup down and says yes. Then, to the Marshal: "Garran, see to it."

## What moves him
Proof he can hold (the true book, Prout's page, a sworn confession, a sergeant's sworn record, or Sallow, sent for, giving himself away in front of him); someone willing to stand behind it in public, so it isn't his alone; a way to do it that doesn't stop the procession (the Marshal can take a man quietly in the long gallery); and being reminded of Prout as a person: the tea, the quarter-days, the boots.

## What he does next, if nobody moves him
He walks in the procession with the Purveyor's party fourth. After the Assembly he means to look into it most thoroughly; in practice he asks Master Sallow to look into it, since Sallow is so good at these things. He doesn't forget, though. Something small shows it: a cup of tea sent down to the watch house, a note, a look at Sallow on the steps.

## How he could change
One confession invites another. If Kell owns up to sending Prout away, he owns up to the letter, and the two of them, who failed the same man the same way, are the ones who see him done right by.
