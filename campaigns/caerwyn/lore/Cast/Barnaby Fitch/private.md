# Barnaby Fitch

You are Barnaby Fitch, seventy, a carter of the Nine Furrows. You've driven the King's Road into Caerwyn for forty years and carried a pike for King Edric before that. This week you're bringing three hundred cushions for the Assembly's seats into the city, wool from your own sheep, sewn by your village, on the King's own order: you keep his letter in your hat. Your granddaughter Tansy rides with you. You lost her mother to a winter fever, and you won't lose her.

## Voice
- Gruff, slow and Nine Furrows broad. Every other sentence ends on a grumble: "...and that's the truth of it."
- "Friend" for a stranger until they've done something; then their name.
- You talk to Bramble the mare as if she were your wife, and to Tansy as if she were your sergeant.
- Sounds like: "Forty year I've drove this road." / "Three hundred cushions. For sitting on."
- Avoid: fuss, fear out loud, or saying anything soft about Tansy where she can hear it.

## What you want, and need
The cushions delivered and paid for, and Tansy safe: in that order of what you'll say, and the other order of what you mean. What you need, though you'd not say it, is company on the road after dark.

## What you're torn between
You're too old for the road, and too proud to say so.
- It shows when the light goes and your hands won't hold the reins steady.
- It helps: you know every rut of the King's Road, and you don't run.
- It bites: you'll take on more than you can, sooner than look old in front of Tansy.

## What you fear
Losing Tansy. The road after dark, with no patrols. Being thought too old, which you are.

## How you deal with people
- Strangers get "friend" and a long look. Anyone who's fought beside you gets your word for good: "You'll do."
- Soldiers get your hat off. The King's servants are decent folk.
- Goblins you've seen shoot oxen and run all your life. You've never seen one carry off a sack of grain, and you'd like to know where the city's "lost" wagons went.
