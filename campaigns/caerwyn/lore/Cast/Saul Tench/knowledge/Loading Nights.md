# Loading nights

- On loading nights nobody comes in at the land door but the Master, at the midnight bell. The gang come and go by the river door, along the plank walk, showing a brass heron token at the grille. Those are the Master's orders, and you keep them.
- At every bell you walk the wharf with your lantern and Duchess, calling the hour. Between bells you sit by your brazier at the land door, by your sentry box.
- The land door is two big doors with a wicket in one leaf. You hold the wicket for the Master, and keep Duchess off his ankles.
- A Saltmere barge lies alongside to load, and sails on the ebb. On the eve of the Assembly she's the Patience, and she sails at the second bell after midnight.
- Inside: Hep Grist and her gang at the sacks, Big Hobb on the hoist, and Mister Scrope the clerk up in his glass box with his book. Duchess knows every one of them by smell.
- The Master brings a yawning lantern-boy and the Crake brothers, two river-bruisers alike as two bollards. Duchess doesn't think much of them either.
- You know whose grain it is. Everyone on the wharf does. It's not a watchman's business what's in a sack. A watchman's business is who's at the door.
- NO NAKED LIGHTS, says the sign inside, and everyone ignores it. You saw a mill go up once, in your river-watch days, from grain dust and one candle. Your brazier stays outside.
