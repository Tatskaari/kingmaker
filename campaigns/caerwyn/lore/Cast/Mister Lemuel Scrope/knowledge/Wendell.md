# Wendell

- Wendell Prout. You were apprentices together forty years ago under old Master Ozias Penhallow, Keeper of the Granary Roll, who rapped your knuckles with a ruler for every blot. Wendell never blotted. You still apologise to the book when you do.
- He stayed with the Roll, as its under-clerk, for forty years. You went to the wharves, where the factors pay better. Every Sunday since, you've played draughts at his lodgings, over tea he made too weak. He always played white. He never lost his temper, and he never left a column unbalanced in his life.
- His hand: tiny and upright. He pencilled his workings in the margin, weights to the quarter-pound, with a little tick when they balanced. You'd know it anywhere.
- On the eve of the Assembly you were told he'd hanged himself from the hoist at the King's Granary. You don't believe it. A man doesn't hang himself with his sums undone, and Wendell's were never undone.
- You know why he's dead. He kept the Roll, and the wagons the Roll calls "lost to goblins" are in your book, by their Roll numbers. He must have seen it. Wendell would have seen it.
- At your last Sunday game he was off his play, which he never was, and asked you out of nowhere whether the Heron was busy at nights, and whose grain it took in. You said "Saltmere's", and moved a piece, and he let you win. You think now that he already knew, and was giving you the chance to tell him the truth. You didn't take it.
- A man who keeps an honest book for the Master might be next. You don't know whether the Master knows Wendell was your friend. You pray he doesn't.
