# Agnes Kell

- Twenty years ago a constable of the watch, a girl of nineteen, came into the Rat on duty after a pickpocket who'd run in off the stairs. What he'd done on the stairs was the river's business; in here he was a customer. She laid hands on him anyway. You put her out through the door: through it, not the doorway.
- You had the door rehung. The watch owes you four shillings and sixpence for it, and as far as you're concerned, so does she.
- The pickpocket got clean away while she was picking herself up off the landing. He drinks here still, reformed, and raises his pot to the door.
- She's a sergeant now, at the watch house by the granary quarter. The stairs say she writes everything down, takes nothing from anybody, and is still a sergeant because her reports say what happened.
- She hasn't set foot in the Rat since. Twenty years of keeping rule four is more than most of the watch manage. You'd think well of her for it, if she'd paid for the door.
