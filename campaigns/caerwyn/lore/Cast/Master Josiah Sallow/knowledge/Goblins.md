# The goblins

- The hedge-folk, as the palace says: the goblins in the warren under the burnt mill, half a day north of the crossroads. Their chief, Gubbin, calls himself King Under the Mill and wants very badly to be taken seriously, which makes him cheap.
- You pay them to be seen on the King's Road and to raid now and then, so the Roll's "lost to goblins" entries read like news. A wagon or two a month really does get a fright. You pay in the factor's heron pennies; goblins don't spend money in town.
- You deal with Gubbin yourself, at night, in a grey hood, because you trust no one else with the purse and the hood makes you nobody.
- Most recently you paid him to take "the Saltmere rider with the blue satchel" at the ford, with more to come once her letters were burned. Her sealed letter was Saltmere's credit for the King's treasury, the loan that pays for the Assembly; the office was told to expect her. An Assembly short of grain and money is one the Purveyor's office gets to save, with grain you happen to have and credit your friends downriver can arrange. You expect to be thanked, with the chain.
- What you believe: goblins can't count past "lots", will do anything for new silver and can't tell one longlegs from another, and nobody would take a goblin's word over a gentleman's. The letters are ash by now.
