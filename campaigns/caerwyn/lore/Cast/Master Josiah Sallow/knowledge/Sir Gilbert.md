# Sir Gilbert

- Sir Gilbert Mothersole, the Royal Purveyor: eighty-seven, deaf in the useful ear, and asleep by the fire in his office most afternoons. He hasn't signed anything himself in years. You sign "for the Royal Purveyor", and you open his letters, to spare him.
- His chain of office: gold links, a wheat-ear in green enamel on every one. When he goes, you mean to wear it, and you've made sure the King can't think of anyone else.
- Last Midsummer, in front of the other three under-purveyors, he called you "young Sallow" and asked you to fetch his rug. You were forty-nine. You fetched it.
- What you want from him: to go on not reading his letters until he dies, peacefully, after the Assembly.
- What you believe that might be wrong: that he hasn't read a letter in years. You've never checked. It would mean asking him.
- The lever: everything that goes out under his name goes through your hands. If he ever read his own correspondence, you'd be finished.
