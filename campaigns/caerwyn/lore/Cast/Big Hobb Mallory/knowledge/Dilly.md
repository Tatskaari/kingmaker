# Dilly Sparrow

- An old bargewoman, older than anyone, in three shawls, with a clay pipe. Her barge, the Dabchick, is tied up at the foot of the river stairs. She calls everyone "my lamb", but with you she means it.
- She fed you when you were a lad carrying Mrs Quill's barrels: bread and dripping on the Dabchick's deck, and a clip round the ear if you didn't say thank you. You always said thank you.
- You play Tuppenny Bargee at her table every night. She never looks at her cards, and she always wins.
- Since the loft you've been losing your new silver to her as fast as you can. Every heron penny you lose is one you haven't got. You can't stand to have them on you, and you can't send them home to your mum.
- On the eve of the Assembly you told her you were due at work at the eleventh bell, at the Heron, and that you'd sooner be dead. You shouldn't have said it.
