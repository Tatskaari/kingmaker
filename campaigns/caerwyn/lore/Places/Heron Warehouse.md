# The Heron warehouse

The last warehouse at the downriver end of the wharves, where the lamps give out. You notice a tall, tarred timber store standing half on the wharf and half on piles over the river; a painted heron on its signboard; and a brazier at its land door, with an old watchman beside it and a vast dog asleep across the threshold.

**Why the heron**: Saltmere lets its great merchant houses strike their own silver, each with its house mark, and a house's mark downriver is as good as its name. The heron is the mark of one Saltmere grain factor, new to Caerwyn this season, who took the last warehouse on the wharf and hung out his sign. Sacks go out of it stamped with the heron, and his people are paid in new silver pennies with a heron where the King's face should be. Nobody on the river had seen one before this month. A heron penny spends without a blink in Saltmere; in Caerwyn the chandlers want the King's face. Saltmere's own banner is the salt-gull, not the heron.

**What's here**
- **The land door**: big double doors with a wicket in one leaf, facing the wharf and the foot of the Wharf Road, with the watchman's sentry box and brazier beside it.
- **The river door**: a low loading door at water level with a grille in it, reached along a slippery plank walk on the piles.
- **Hoists** jutting out over the water, and barges moored alongside to load straight from the store.
- After dark, lantern light shows through the cracks in the boards, and a lamp burns in a high window.

**Who's about, and why**
- **Old Saul**, the night watchman, with a lantern, a rattle and opinions about loiterers. He used to walk the river for the river watch. His mastiff, Duchess, knows everyone who works there by smell.
- **Porters in grain-white** on loading nights, at double pay.
- **Saltmere bargees**, who care about the tide and nothing else. The factor himself never comes to Caerwyn.

**What it needs, trades and resents**: it needs strong hands on loading nights, and pays double for them in heron silver. It trades grain downriver to Saltmere. The wharf resents its barges going down full while Caerwyn goes short, and takes its pay all the same. It doesn't care for questions.

**The rules of the place, and who keeps them**: the watchman keeps the land door, and on a loading night his orders are to let nobody in by it. The night gang come and go by the river door. One lantern hangs at the land door on an ordinary night; when the Heron is expecting trouble there are two, and men walk the wharf with a third.

**How the town's troubles show here**: barges going downriver low in the water while bread is dear, and porters on double pay who still can't afford a loaf.

**What's happening now**: dock talk says the Heron has been busy at night all this past month. A broad Saltmere barge, the Patience, lies alongside, and the ebb runs at the second bell after midnight.
