# The market square and the Corn Exchange

The middle of Caerwyn, between the palace on the rise, the granary quarter and the top of the river stairs. You notice the square half hung with bunting and half scaffolded for tomorrow's procession; the Corn Exchange's price board, with people stopped in front of it; and the bread queue.

**What's here**
- **The procession route**, across the square from the palace road to the far side, lined with bunting and the delegations' banners, with sweepers on it.
- **Stalls**: bakers, a pieman, chandlers, and anyone else with something to sell before the square shuts for the procession.
- **The market cross**: stone steps where criers cry and preachers preach.
- **The Corn Exchange**, a columned hall on one side of the square. Its price board hangs outside. In the front hall the factors trade grain by the sample, and the back rooms are where the Exchange keeps its wagon lists: every load of grain sold in the city, weighed and entered, by the cart and by the sack.
- **Streets off it**: up the rise to the palace gate, along to the granary quarter, and down to the top of the river stairs.

**Who's about, and why**
- **Market folk and stallholders**, who want to sell up before the procession shuts the square.
- **Bakers and their queues.** The bakers want flour they can afford; the queues want bread they can afford.
- **Grain factors**, Saltmere buyers among them, who want their price.
- **Abel Fennick**, the Exchange's clerk, who chalks up the board and keeps the wagon lists. He wants the lists straight and the factors happy.
- **Sweepers, and watchmen lining the route**, moving carts and chasing pigs.
- **Delegations' servants**, buying for their masters.

**What it needs, trades and resents**: it needs the square clear for the procession by morning. It trades in everything, and in grain by the sample. It resents the price board, and the procession that will shut the market tomorrow.

**The rules of the place, and who keeps them**
- **The price board** is chalked at the noon bell and again at the evening bell. The factors set the prices, and Fennick chalks them up.
- **The wagon lists** are for the factors and the palace's own clerks. Fennick shows them to nobody else without a factor's word or the Purveyor's seal, and this week every other answer is "After the Assembly".
- **Short weight** is the market warden's to fine, and the market warden is at the palace this week.
- **The route** is the watch's: carts off it, pigs off it, and nothing left on it overnight.

**How the town's troubles show here**: the board, the queues and the smaller loaves; the bunting and the banners going up over people who can't afford bread; "lost to goblins" said at every stall about anything missing. And a preacher at the market cross calls dear bread a judgement on the bunting.

**What's happening now**: the eve of the Assembly. The banners go up, the noon price is chalked up, and by evening it's up again with something rude written beside it. At night the square is empty but for the sweepers and a watchman asleep upright against a lamppost. On procession morning the crowd lines the route from first light.

**Room to improvise**: stallholders, the pieman, the criers, the preacher's text. Tie each to the bread, the Assembly or the goblins.
