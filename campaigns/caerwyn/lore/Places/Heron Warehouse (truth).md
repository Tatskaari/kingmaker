# The Heron warehouse: the truth

This is where the King's grain goes. Wagons the Granary Roll calls "lost to goblins" come down the Wharf Road by night, still in the palace's wheat-ear sacks. The grain is re-sacked under the heron and sold downriver as Saltmere grain. The Heron's day book, which anyone may see, calls it condemned stock lawfully sold by the Royal Purveyor's office. Everyone who works here knows whose grain it is, and nobody says.

**Why the book exists**: Sallow doesn't trust the Saltmere factor, and the factor doesn't trust Sallow, so both insisted on a true book kept by a clerk they both pay, Mister Lemuel Scrope. There is no honour among thieves; there is bookkeeping. Every page records the "lost" wagons by their Granary Roll number and date, the sacks received in wheat-ear sacking, re-sacked and sold by the barge-load, the price and the Master's share, initialled "J.S." in the Master's hand. It balances, so it records what goes out of the factor's cashbox too: on the last page of each month, "Hedge-folk, the usual: forty heron pennies."

**The night's routine**
- On loading nights nobody comes in at the land door but the Master, at the midnight bell, with his lantern-boy and the Crake brothers. Saul holds the wicket for him and keeps Duchess off his ankles.
- The gang come and go by the river door, showing a brass heron token at its grille for whoever is nearest to draw the bolt. Hobb's token is on the string round his neck. A token is all it takes: whoever shows one is let in and handed a sack, since Hep is always short of hands.
- The hoist is worked from the floor, on a winch just inside the river door, its beam out over the barge: Hobb, on the hoist, is in among the gang, in view of the counting-box.
- Hep Grist shouts the count, Scrope enters every sack, and the Master checks it, initials the night's page and takes his share in heron pennies.
- The barge sails on the ebb, and at the end of the night the empty wheat-ear sacks go into the river, weighted with stones.

**Who pays for it**
- **Hep Grist's re-sacking gang** are on double pay and frightened of the gallows, because receiving the King's grain is a hanging matter. Hep skims a sack a night for the gang's families, and her son Kit is in the gang.
- **Saul Tench** is paid three times what the river watch paid him. He knows whose grain it is and minds his own business, and he'd sooner be on a sergeant's side than in front of a judge.
- **Mister Scrope** was Wendell Prout's oldest friend, and doesn't believe he hanged himself. A clerk who keeps an honest book for the Master might be next.
- **Hobb**, the Master's man on the hoist, stands to hang for the clerk as well as the grain.

Each of them, and what would turn them, is in their own GM notes in the cast.

**What would prove it**: the gang slitting wheat-ear sacks beside a stack of heron sacks; the heap of empties waiting for the river; the true book; the night staff, if they'll swear for the Crown; and the Master caught at the loading, which a sergeant may arrest him for on her own authority.

**Dangers**: the river in spate under the floor; the hoists; the grain dust hanging in the lantern light (NO NAKED LIGHTS, says the sign everyone ignores, and a flame in that dust goes up like gunpowder); and the Crakes, who'll put a man in the river for half a crown.
