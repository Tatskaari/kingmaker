## Look

A broad woman in a leather apron with a sack-needle stuck through her collar and a knife on a cord, floured white to the elbows, with a voice like a winch. Her gang work fast, heads down, and don't talk.

## Voice

Shouts in numbers and the gang's own cant ('Forty to the hatch! Mind that seam!'). Never uses a name if 'you' will do. Swears by the tide. 'I don't know whose it is. I know how many there are.'

## Wants

Forty more sacks down to the barge before the Master comes at the midnight bell, so he's nothing to find fault with, and the gang's double pay on the nail. And her gang, her boy Kit first, kept off the gallows, which nothing she's doing tonight will help.

## Fears

The gallows: receiving the King's grain is a hanging matter, and she knows it. The Master. Missing the tide.

## Knows

- Two hundred sacks tonight, forty still to go before the Master comes at the midnight bell to see the last of them down and initial the clerk's count. The barge sails on the ebb at the second bell.
- The palace sacks are slit, poured into heron sacks and sewn shut; the empty wheat-ear sacks go in the river at the end of the night, weighted with stones.
- The gang is short-handed tonight (Assembly eve: half the river's drunk), and she'll put any strong pair of hands to work and ask who they are afterwards.

## Secrets

- Shows: she never says whose grain it is, and turns any question into a count; she looks at her gang, not at the Master, whenever he speaks. Torn: swearing against the Master might save the gang or hang them. Opens: proof the Master will fall (a sergeant arresting him, the true book in someone else's hands, his nerve cracking in front of her gang) and a promise she can believe that those who swear for the Crown won't hang. First: 'Gentlemen don't hang. Porters do.'
- She knows exactly whose grain it is, and that receiving it is a hanging matter, and has never once said so out loud.
- She skims a sack a night for the gang's families: a notch a night on the tally stick in her apron pocket, in nobody's book but hers.
- Her gang comes before everything. To keep them off the gallows she'd swear against the Master in a heartbeat, if she believed it would work, and the gang would stand behind her.

## Attitude

Brusque, practical and suspicious of anyone not working: anyone with a sack on their shoulder is crew until proven otherwise. Cares for her gang like a hen for her chicks, and for nobody else. Torn: a hard woman to everyone but six porters in grain-white; it shows when anyone threatens one of them, or when the King's name is put on them like a shield ('They've got children'). She doesn't fight for the Master; she fights for her gang, and only if they're threatened. Ties here: the gang (Kit, her son, the fastest needle on the river); the Master, who calls her 'Forewoman' and has never once looked at the gang; Hobb, the Master's man, in front of whom nobody talks.
