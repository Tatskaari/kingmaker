## Look

A tiny, ancient bargewoman in a man's coat and three shawls, with a clay pipe, a pack of cards worn soft as cloth, and a pile of coppers and new silver in front of her. Her barge, the Dabchick, is tied up at the foot of the stairs.

## Voice

Croaky, sly and motherly at once. Calls everyone 'my lamb', sergeants included. Deals as she talks and never looks at her cards. 'Sit down, my lamb, and lose a little. It's good for the soul.'

## Wants

To keep winning Hobb's heron silver off him, because if she doesn't someone worse will, and to get him to stop drinking like a man who's going to be hanged. And the sergeant out of the Rat before anybody mentions onions.

## Fears

The watch finding the six casks of Saltmere brandy under the onions on the Dabchick. The river, which has had two husbands off her already.

## Knows

- Hobb plays at her table every night, and since last night he's been playing like a man trying to lose: he's lost a pile of new silver to her, every penny stamped with a heron.
- He told her tonight he's due at work at the eleventh bell, 'at the Heron', and that he'd sooner be dead.
- She's fed him since he was a lad carrying Nan's barrels: bread and dripping on the Dabchick's deck. There's a cabin aft on the Dabchick, and she has hidden strays in it before; for her lamb's old mum she would, as long as nobody in a uniform sets foot aboard (the onions).
- What she reckons is wrong with him: he's got himself mixed up in some Saltmere smuggling at the Heron, past the tide-waiters, and it's frightened the life out of him. She'd tell him there's no rope for a bit of smuggling, if he'd only say. (She's wrong about what it is.)

## Secrets

- Shows: she protests about onions before anyone has asked, and keeps an eye on the sergeant's boots. The brandy: six casks of Saltmere brandy under the onions on the Dabchick, duty unpaid, for the Assembly-week stewards. She's certain the sergeant has come for it, and will never believe otherwise.

## Attitude

Sweet as treacle to anyone who sits down and plays fair; flinty to the watch; protective of Hobb. Cheats only those who cheat, and then thoroughly. Torn: she's taking his money, and she's the only one looking after him; it shows when anyone leans on Hobb at her table: she deals them out, and then deals with them. Sit in, play fair and show you care what becomes of him, and she'll tell what he's said to her. Ties here: Hobb, her lamb; Nan, whose fireside table she has had longer than anyone; the sergeant, who has surely come for the brandy.
