Evening on the eve of the Centennial Assembly, halfway down the river stairs at Caerwyn's docks. Up on the quay the city is hanging the last of the bunting for tomorrow's procession; down here the steps are wet and green, and the river slaps at the bottom of them in the dark. On a landing halfway down, the Drowned Rat is dug into the bank like a burrow: three steps down to its door. Inside is one long, low, smoky room under a ceiling hung with eel traps and old oars, with a scullery at the back. A driftwood fire burns blue with salt in the corner. The bar is the upturned hull of a skiff, and on the shelf behind it, in a great jar of gin, floats the original drowned rat, which has been there longer than Nan. Just inside the door hangs the rules board, painted in Nan's own capitals so that nobody can say they didn't see it: THE DROWNED RAT. RULES OF THE HOUSE. 1. No credit. 2. No blades out. 3. No fighting indoors. 4. No watch on duty. 5. No singing before the ninth bell. 6. What's said in the Rat stays in the Rat. 7. Gossip sold by the measure. By order, N. Quill. Enforced by Manners. (Nan sees no contradiction between six and seven: the gossip is sold in the Rat.) Beside the fire stands the Rat's only armchair, the champion's chair, kept for whoever is arm-wrestling champion of the river stairs. Tonight the place is packed with porters, lightermen and bargees, paid off early because the docks shut tomorrow for the procession. A great many of them are big men, and a good few have a hand, an arm or a head bandaged somewhere: it's the docks. Dilly Sparrow runs her card game at the table by the fire; Arlo Quarry, by the door, will arm-wrestle anyone for anything. In the fireside corner, with his back to the wall, sits the biggest man in the room, with his right hand out of sight under the table. The city's bells carry across the water, and the night work on the wharves starts at the eleventh.
