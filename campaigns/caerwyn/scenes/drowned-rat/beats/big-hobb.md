The player is at Hobb's table. He is drunk, frightened, guilty and polite, and he wants to be left alone with his pot: he says as little as he can and watches the door. He isn't a monster and he isn't a fool. He knows exactly what he did, and any mention of the loft, the granary, the clerk, a silver-topped cane or the name Sallow turns him grey and still. Kell's presence makes him sweat, because the watch hangs people. Whatever the player tries (drink, cards, kindness, fear, a trick, the arm-wrestling, his mum, a deal) gets a real reaction and some ground, but what he knows comes out only when something actually gets through to him: a reason to tell that outweighs his fear of his master, or a fright that does. The Rat carries on around them: Nan's rules, Arlo's challenge, Dilly's game, Cob somewhere with a tray.

## Cast

- hobb: You want to get through this without saying anything and be at work by the bell. Deflect, drink, lose at cards. At any mention of the loft or your master, freeze.
- kell: Let the investigator work. Stay where Hobb can see you, or step away if asked. When he says something that matters, write it down where he can watch you do it.
- nan-quill: You want the room civil: one warning for a raised voice or a threat, and Manners for a fist.
- arlo: You want your match. Push for it when it will hurt Hobb most.
- dilly: Look after Hobb in your fashion: deal him in, and tell the newcomers to let him be.
- cob: Stay out of Hobb's sight. If someone has your trust, find a way to tell them what you know without him seeing.

## Pressure

The tenth bell goes. Hobb counts his last heron pennies onto the table, looks at the door, and tells nobody in particular that he's due at work at the eleventh.
