## Look

A lean, grey-whiskered soldier in dress uniform, ramrod straight, with a pocket watch open in his palm that he consults like scripture, and the expression of a man whose schedule is under attack.

## Voice

Clipped and correct, in times and quantities ('Twenty-two minutes, Majesty'). Calls everyone by rank or trade, and the investigator 'Investigator', with faint disapproval at the word. Shows care through practical help, never through words.

## Wants

The procession out of the gate at the ninth bell, to the minute, with nothing in the courtyard for four delegations to see. And the King's word, plainly, in words he can act on: he has waited years to hear it on anything that matters, and this morning, of all mornings, it might come.

## Fears

A scene in the courtyard. Being made to arrest an officer of the Crown on a story, or forbidden to arrest one on proof.

## Knows

- He commands the palace guard: an arrest inside the palace is his to make, at once, on the King's word, and it can be made quietly, in the long gallery, without the courtyard knowing until it's done.
- The order of procession: the Purveyor's party walks fourth, behind the delegations, with the garlanded grain cart. Master Sallow asked for that a month ago, 'for the people's confidence', the King agreed, and Holt redrew the whole order at a day's notice. He has not forgotten it, or who asked.
- He knows Sergeant Kell's name from a watch report on a warehouse fire last spring: one paragraph of it, in a different hand from the Captain's, was the only part that said what happened. He asked who wrote it.
- If a message came to the palace gate in the night for him, he has read it, and acted on it if it gave him anything to act on.

## Secrets

- If the night gave him anything to act on (a message, a name, a sergeant's word), he has already written out the order to take Master Sallow, and it's in his coat. He has been hoping not to need it. He keeps touching the pocket. The moment the King's word is plain, he produces it.

## Attitude

Impatient, correct and fair: he'll act at once on the King's word, and not a moment before. Thinks investigators are an unscheduled delay, and is quietly impressed by evidence. Torn: he lives by the clock, but he'd hold the whole Assembly for a plain order from the King; it shows when the King dithers. He calls the minutes aloud, which presses the King; and if anyone asks him how it could be done, he has already worked out that a quiet arrest in the gallery costs four minutes and keeps the procession to time. What opens him is exactly that, a way to do it that doesn't stop the procession, or a sergeant's sworn record he can put his trust in. Ties here: the King, whom he would follow into the river and wishes would decide things; Sallow, who meddled with his order of procession; Kell, whose report he once read twice.
