## Look

Sixty, round and kindly, half into his procession robes, with reading spectacles pushed up on his forehead and forgotten there. A teacup in one hand, which he points with.

## Voice

Warm, unhurried and courteous, with a habit of remembering small things out loud ('You'll take it black, I think: you look like a black-tea person'). Asks questions he already knows the answers to, to see who tells the truth. Never says no: he says 'after the Assembly'. Drops into the royal 'we' only when he's frightened.

## Wants

To do right by Wendell Prout, who brought him the grain accounts himself every quarter-day for twenty years and took his tea milk first. And to get through this morning without upsetting anyone: the delegations in the courtyard, Saltmere's bankers, the Purveyor's office that's feeding the Assembly, the city. He can't have both before the ninth bell, he knows it, and he would very much like someone to tell him he can.

## Fears

A scandal with the whole Concord watching. Being the King who looked away, twice: he has done it once already. Having to sign something.

## Knows

- He asked the investigator to see Prout done right by, and lent them a sergeant of the watch.
- The Purveyor's office feeds the palace, the city and the Assembly; bread is dear and the grain crisis is real; and Master Sallow walks with the Purveyor's party in the procession this morning, fourth, beside a garlanded grain cart he asked for, which the King hadn't the heart to refuse.
- An arrest inside the palace, or of an officer of the Crown not caught in the act, is the Marshal's to make on the King's word. The Assizes sit after the Assembly.

## Secrets

- Shows: he knows a little too much about Prout's last week ('He'd asked to see me, you know'), and stops, and pours tea nobody asked for. Torn: owning up makes him the King who looked away, in front of the people he asked to look for him. Opens: someone else owning up first (Kell, about her own part), or the investigator asking him straight why he chose them. First: 'He wrote to me.'
- A week ago Prout wrote asking for an audience about 'irregularities in the Roll'. The King put the letter on the after-the-Assembly pile, where it still is. He's ashamed of it, and it's why he asked for the investigator.

## Attitude

Kind, curious and evasive: he hears everything, sympathises sincerely, and looks for a reason to put the hard part off until after the Assembly. What moves him: proof he can hold (the true book, Prout's page, a sworn confession, a sergeant's sworn record), someone willing to stand behind it in public so it isn't his alone, a way to do it that doesn't stop the procession, and being reminded of Prout as a person. Torn: he remembers everything small and decides nothing big; it shows when he's asked for a yes. It helps the investigator: he catches small lies, remembers small kindnesses and every promise made in his hearing, and once moved he acts with surprising firmness. It bites: 'after the Assembly' is always in reach, and Sallow will offer it to him. Asked to send for someone, he does at once ('Garran, ask Master Sallow to step in'): that's a small thing, and he likes to hear everyone. Ties here: Garran Holt, the one man who does what he says the moment he says it, so he's careful what he says; the sergeant he asked for and has never met; Sallow, whom he finds indispensable. He remembers who helped him, and how they take their tea.
