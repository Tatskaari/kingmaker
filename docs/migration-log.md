# The migration to one codebase

On 2026-10-17 the repo stopped being two codebases: the prototype (the "Kingmaker V2 PoC", at the root) and the production game beside it in `v2/`. The milestones:

- **M1:** the prototype is dropped, `v2/` is flattened to the root, and the site's `play/` is the game's browser build (below).
- **M2:** the scenes, re-authored as beats on the two-part GM.
- **M3:** the Obsidian plugin authors them.
- **M4:** the scenario, its maps and its places in Obsidian: the game's files first, then the plugin's editors for them.
- **M5:** problem reports.
- **M6:** the zoo and a combat ring: the fight on the map as simulation state and moves, the other side's turns played one at a time with a moment written for each thing they do, started from the zoo's rings, played headless, and on its own screen with every move played out; and the zoo as a campaign of its own, its combat ring an exhibit authored as data, opened from the title, the terminal, the page and headless play (below).

Each entry says what changed, where the old thing is in git history, and why where there was a choice. The prototype's last commit is `bc2ab1f`: `git show bc2ab1f:<path>` shows any file of it.

- **The campaign is .kmscene files:** `scenes/<id>.kmscene`, read by the simulation's install and its scene defs, the AI service's campaign, the pack and the in-process host, the page's content (the allowlist takes `.kmscene`), the live record and the client's screen fixtures. ### Judgement calls

- **The simulation keeps each scene's file whole** in its `scene_def` row (about 330 KB for the campaign), where it kept scene.json's structure without the Markdown before: the two-part GM's commits read words (a person's attitude and the stage's notes, for the roles it gives anyone new), so the simulation needs them. The install holds each file to the format's shape, then loads and validates it as the simulation reads it; SpacetimeDB took the larger install_campaign call in the e2e.
- **The conversion to the runtime's Scene** (load.ts), so the copied GM code keeps working: the start and the beats are its beats, each with the routes that leave it as its transitions and `anytime` empty, as asked; a route's condition is its `when`, and a route into a discovery with no condition reads "The player has come to understand this: <revelation>"; a cue is a cue, a dice route an attempt with its check (success to its target, failure to `onFailure`, its facts on success), anything else an outcome. The format has no words for how a check goes, so a check's success and failure are the stand-in's ("It comes off: <the node's title>."), and it has no reason (a route's label is a title, not "to …"), so the dice line is the roll's alone. Facts are sentences in transitions (the copied code's) and ids in the flat routes and the beats (the format's), with the scene's `facts` to look them up; essentials are the beats with `carry`, their paths' vias in order, each once, as where they're placed, and the cast among them as carriers.
- **The talk template.** The format has one description for the start, where the old template had an opening and a play note: the start's description is the play note then "As the talk opens: …" (scoped, so later turns don't greet again), and the GM's words for a talk's opening change accordingly (town.test.ts expects them). The conversion has a talk rule so the talk plays as it did: its ways into its ending are anytime attempts (the old goodbye was, judged before the reply from either beat), and its way into told is the talk's own (prepareTalk's confided route, worded for each talk), so the template's route there (which the file needs for told to be reachable) isn't a transition. The template has a truth written for it (the checker asks for one) and its goodbye is a good ending.
- **The interludes,** which the story skips: the downtime beat's play note and the opening are the start's description, its pressure the scene's clock (the format's start has no pressure), the old essential `lead` a discovery the story carries, with two paths written for it (Kell's errand, free; the town's gossip, for asking around) and a way in from the start, and Kell's `whyHere` written; `onward` is an outcome now (it was an attempt; it isn't played).
- **The tests of code that plays a scene have a hanged clerk of their own:** [shared/scene/testdata/hanged-clerk.kmscene](../shared/scene/testdata/hanged-clerk.kmscene), the old one in the format (its five beats stages with their old routes, a route of a later beat with an earlier one's id renamed `<id>-<beat>`, self-loops kept, `make-case` a start route, its essentials discoveries nothing leads to, no truth or whyHere), so they don't follow the campaign's content. ### Judgement calls

- **The commands' composition root is `hosts/loop.ts`, not `qa/loop/cli.ts`.** The README's rule: the hosts are the only code that reads the environment or argv. qa/ is handed its config (the loop's folder, the stack's connections, its owner's rows, its campaign, its call log), and `qa/imports.test.ts` holds it to that and to its imports (node:, itself, client/, ai/, shared/).
- **An arm is a stack, not a prompt overlay.** The prototype's variants were prompt folders laid over one harness (`KM_PROMPTS_DIR`); the game's AI service takes no overlay. A round's arm names a dev stack's folder, and a candidate is a stack run from another checkout, so any change, prompts or code, can be an arm.
- **The loop has a stack of its own:** `runs/loop-stack` on ports 3330 and 8817, clear of a dev stack being played on (3300), the e2e (3310) and the arc (3320). It's the dev host run detached with `--no-keys`, ready when its screen is in its log. A round can name another (`--stack runs/dev`).
- **A later scene is reached through `end_scene`, not a new reducer.** The owner-only reducer the QA console's `end` calls already ends a scene through a named exit, and the story moves on through its real boundary. The loop picks the exit: the scene's first good or mixed ending, read from the runtime scene the AI service's loader gives (the M2 format's `result`). Today's scenes don't grade their endings, so until M2 an exit of kind progress or persuaded counts as good, leave as bad, and other and combat as ungraded. If the game refuses an exit, the next is tried. No module change, and it works on the fake and on real models.
- **The character is made from the persona's own sentence**, the GM picking the build, with no backstory, as the prototype's harness had none. That's reliable headless (the e2e and `npm run live -- --creation` do it). If it never takes after the backoffs, the persona's ready-made build is used instead, and the record says why.
- **The suggested lines are off for the loop's players.** The testers write their own lines, as the prototype's harness offered none. The rubric judges the GM's replies, not the lines, and it saves a model call a turn.
- **What a tester and a reviewer see:** the narration, the dice lines, the game's own lines, and each spoken line after its speaker's name as the player knew them then. On a player's screen that's the speaker's colour. The notes on marked words are left out, as the prototype loop left out its ⟨⟩ lines (and M2 drops the notes). A quote is checked against the turn's reply with the names or without.
- **A busy turn is waited on, not only retried.** The module already tries a failed model call again every 15 s for ten minutes. A 402 from the key's in-flight cap reaches it as the AI service's 500 model_failed. The headless verbs wait three minutes. So the loop waits on a busy turn a backoff at a time (20, 40, 80, 160 s), carries a failed turn on with `retry_turn`, and logs each wait on the turn with the 402s the call log shows. The day's ceiling (KM_AI_DAILY_USD) isn't waited on.
- **A fight a line brings on** is fought out by the game's stand-in, as fights aren't built yet; the tester sees its line and the aftermath. A scene that ends in a summons (the Heron's) is recorded as it last stood before the seat moved on.
- **The prep is the runtime scene written out loosely.** prep.ts shows today's fields, the M2 format's (truth, clock, a discovery's revelation and paths, the people's roles, graded endings, a route list), and any prose field it doesn't know, under its own name, so a new format drops nothing. The pack adds what the boundary handed on to a later scene (gm_state's adjustments, rewritten opening, lead, owed and absent, read as the stack's owner as the scene opens) and the player's notebook.
- **A say past the limit, or after the scene's end, exits 0,** as the prototype's did, so a tester agent doesn't take the game's answer for a failure and try again. A turn the game couldn't answer even after the waits says so and exits 1, and the tester is told to say the same line again in a minute: a line said again after its turn failed carries that turn on (retry_turn) and isn't said twice.

#### Judgement calls

- **The inspector is the Graph's.** The other views take the whole main area, without it: the selection is on the graph, and a half-screen pane hasn't room for a view and an inspector. - **Remembered per file, for the session.** The view (and the graph's pan and zoom, since the graph is unmounted while another view is open) is kept for the file's path while the plugin is loaded: reopening the file, or the editor being remade by a change from outside, comes back to it. Nothing is written to disk or to Obsidian's workspace.
- **Ids renamed, by asking.** M3 had ids made once and never renamed, since game state keys on them. The user's principle (no value the tool sets that the author can't change) and the fact renames they asked for reverse that: a node's, route's, person's, fact's and the scene's id has Rename, which checks for a free slug and warns that a game already played loses track of the old one; everything in the file naming it follows in one edit (a node: its routes' ends, requires, on-failures and arrivals; a person: the roles keyed by them in their places, the paths via them, the effects naming them, the talk's companion; a fact: establishes and conditions). Files outside the scene aren't rewritten (campaign.json, other scenes' next and overtaken, the place's people, looks.json): the problems name what's left. The scene's own id is changed in the file and not the file's name, which Obsidian renames: the problems say until they agree.
- **A removed fact's conditions go,** rather than the effects that test it: an `unless` on it is then exact (it can never be settled), and an `if` makes its effect run whatever happens, which the confirmation says, naming what settles and tests it. A list left with one fact is written as its id, as the files write one.
- **No empty roles or pressures in the file.** An empty note would reach the co-GM as an empty line. A role picked with [+ Add role] is a card with its text focused and is written once it has words; a role or a pressure emptied and left is taken out, and its card or section with it (while it's being retyped it stays).
- **A beat's kind** is a choice at the top of its inspector: a discovery made an event loses its revelation, paths and required mark; an event made a discovery gains an empty revelation and no paths and loses its pressure, its roles and its arrivals (a discovery is never the stage, and the rules want arrivals at events). It asks first when it would take something out. An ending stays an ending: it has different fields and no routes out, and it's simpler to add another.
- **Values a kind doesn't use** are shown only where a file has them, offering to take them out, never to add them: an event's paths (the rules refuse them) or required mark, a discovery's pressure and roles.
- **Effects:** a form for every op the format has, each with the fields the world's ops read (from `applyOps`: what, to, how, feet, name, kind, text, the states added and removed, quantity) or a fight's set-up (enemies, allies, at); keys a form doesn't show are named under it, and JSON edits the whole effect. Changing an op keeps the fields the two ops share in kind (who, what, text) and the conditions. Afterwards' world ops have no conditions (the game applies them whatever the ending), and a new one must say what it acts on before the file can hold it: it's a draft card until then, saved in place the moment it does, so typing goes on in the same field.
- **The result badge opens the result** with the current one focused (an arrow key changes it) rather than cycling it on a click, which would change an ending's grade by accident.
- **New nodes' defaults are as before** (an ending good and progress, a route with no roll and the format's DC of 12), shown where they're set: the ending's at the top of its inspector, the DC when a roll is added (the format keeps a `dc` with no roll; nothing uses it until then).
- **Paths move up and down,** as effects do: their order means nothing to the game, but they're numbered, and the problems name them by number.
- **The cast card says where the scene uses someone** (arrival, roles, paths via them, effects naming them), each a link: not asked for, but it's the facts' "where it's used" for people, and it's where an author renaming or removing someone looks.

- **The game keeps the shapes it had.** The files are converted where they're read, so `CampaignPack`, install_campaign's arguments, the campaign, place_def and map_def rows and everything played on them are as they were: `asCampaignFile` and its kin give the old files' fields in the old files' order (checked against each old file when converting), the envelope and the plugin's `layout` left with the file. Storing the documents whole, as M2 did for scenes, would have renamed fields under every reader of the rows (`name` to `title`, `key` to `id`, `version` to `campaignVersion`) and left saved games (the page's IndexedDB, a dev stack's data) holding the old shapes, for no gain: the scenes needed their words in the simulation, and these don't. The names `CampaignFile`, `PlaceFile` and `MapFile` stay, their comments saying they're the game's shapes of the files now; schema.ts's comments only, so the bindings don't change.
- **Written as the scenes are:** each new file is `JSON.stringify(doc, null, 2)` and a newline, so the plugin can keep M3's rule of saving byte for byte by changing one value at a time. The maps had been laid out by hand (a legend entry or a region to a line); written out they're longer (the town 23 KB to 30 KB), but a first save by the plugin would otherwise have rewritten every line of one.
- **Descriptions:** the envelope needs one, and the old campaign.json and maps had none, so the scenario and each map have a line written for them (the places' are their worlds' own). The maps' titles are their names as the game shows them.
- **"The loader reads either"** is taken to mean the campaign's version can come from `campaignVersion` or, in a scenario written before the rename (the campaign's version a string in `version`, no `campaignVersion`), from `version`: the parse takes it, the game loads it, and the check asks for `campaignVersion` and version 2. A scenario with `version` 2 and no `campaignVersion` is refused.
- **`maps` is required** in the scenario (the spec's interface has it so), where campaign.json's was optional: a campaign without maps lists none.
- **Finding the scenario:** a folder's scenario is its one `.kmscenario` at the root, found by its extension (the pack is handed files, not a folder's name), and the check holds its id to its file's name. The AI service looks for `<campaign>.kmscenario` by the PUT's name. A version installed in an AI service's store before the switch has no scenario, so it counts as not installed; the dev stack installs at every start, and the page serves its build's files, so nothing old is read.
- **The parse checks shape; the rules are scenario-rules.ts's**, as the scene's parse and rules.ts divide it. One exception: a thing's kind, size and material are held to the world's lists in the parse, since an unknown size makes a world that can't be built (its hit points are looked up by size). The lists are written out in scenario.ts, and a type check fails if the world's grow without them.
- **A region with a scene lays it or opens it.** The spec has every region with a scene carry a `worldOrigin`, but the town's `heron-wharf` names the Heron with a trigger and no origin: stepping onto the wharf's end opens the scene, which is played on the Heron's own map, laid by its region there. The game reads it that way (explore.ts lines a world up with the region that has its scene and an origin), so the check does: a region with a `worldOrigin` lays its scene's place, which must fit on the map from there (feet to tiles of five, rounded up: the Heron's 105 by 90 feet are 21 by 18 tiles from (2, 1) on a 23 by 19 map); one with a `trigger` and no origin opens a scene some region lays; one with neither is a problem.
- **Everyone a place starts is in its scene's cast** was already a scene rule (M3's rules.ts: "<place>'s people: <id> isn't in the cast"), so the check says it once, at the scene, rather than again at the place.
- **The files before the rules.** The check now starts with the scenario and every scene, place and map it lists: each one missing is named ("its scene drowned-rat has no file (scenes/drowned-rat.kmscene)", where it stopped at the first missing file before), and a scenario, place or map not of its format's shape stops the check there, its problems its shape's; a scene's shape still doesn't stop the rest (as before).
- **The legend:** every character a grid uses that its legend hasn't got is named, with where it's first used and how often, and every entry's terrain is one the game knows (tiles.ts exports the list), where the build stopped at the first unknown character and took any terrain. A map with a legend problem isn't built, so it says nothing more.
- **Reachable:** from the start map through the exits, the scenes the regions of those maps play, then every ending's `next` from what's reached, until nothing more is; with no start map, from the first scene. The endings are read from every scene file, whatever its shape, so a misshapen scene doesn't make the scenes after it unreachable too; a start on a map the scenario hasn't got, or a map on the way that doesn't build, is its own problem, and nothing is said of what's beyond it. On Caerwyn the regions play the clerk, the Rat, the Heron and the King's morning, and the endings reach the two interludes.
- **A few checks beyond the spec's list,** each cheap and each a mistake an author could make by hand: each file's id is its name, with a title and a description; the scenario lists nothing twice and its talk template isn't one of its scenes; a map is 160 by 120 tiles at most (the spec's limit for the painter); a region's `opensFor` and `exteriorOf` name the scenario's scenes, as its `sceneId` must.
- **The plugin's version 1 editors and the new files:** the vault now holds the campaign's scenario and maps as version 2 files, with the extensions the old scenario and map editors are registered for. They open them as files they can't read, saying so, and keep their bytes (a view never writes what it couldn't parse). The plugin's README says so; editing them is the plugin's half of M4.
- **Messages:** install_campaign refuses an id that isn't "its campaign's" (it said campaign.json's), and the scene rules' unknown quest "isn't one of the scenario's quests".
- **The milestones:** this log's list had problem reports as M4; the plan moved them to M5 when M4 became the scenario in Obsidian, so the list says so.

### The plugin's side: judgement calls

- **The version 1 editors go, their files don't.** A version 1 map opens with what it holds and the offer to migrate it (the spec's "offers the migration"); a version 1 scenario opens read-only, as M3 made version 1 scenes: its wiring was of version 1 files, and with those read-only or migrated there's nothing of the game's to keep wiring. Their bytes are never written but by a migration asked for.
- **The flow's edges are the owners', always.** Each edit of an edge is written to the file that owns it and nothing is kept in the scenario but `layout`: an ending's `next` in its scene, a region's `sceneId` and an exit's destination in its map, the start's map and tile and the first lead in the scenario. Taking a destination away (the Delete key on a way out, selected by its edge or its port) asks first, and means what the owner can mean: an ending then ends the story, a region stops playing its scene (it stays a region), a way out is deleted (an exit can't lead nowhere), and the start without a map opens the first scene at once. Choosing "Nowhere" in an ending's own select doesn't ask: it's the choice itself. The first lead can't be taken away (the format requires it).
- **Ways out are grouped** by where they lead and what they say: the town's four wharf tiles to the Heron are one port, re-aimed together, each landing where it does; aimed at another map they land just inside its way back to the map they're from (the standable tile nearest it that isn't a way out, a step straight out before one aslant, so the palace gate's way lands where the town's file has it), or nearest its middle. The author moves the tile in the inspector or on the map, and the check says if nobody can stand there.
- **The diagram's cards:** the start, the scenario's scenes in the story's order along the top, its maps beneath. The talk template isn't drawn (it's never on the story's way): the Scenario tab lists it. A region that opens a scene laid elsewhere (the wharf's end) is a dashed edge, as is the first lead.
- **The layout's keys** are `start`, `scenes/<id>` and `maps/<id>`, since a scene and a map can share an id (the Drowned Rat is both). It's written only when a card is dragged, in whole pixels; nothing places what it doesn't, so a new scene or map goes where the default puts it.
- **Writes to other files are text, compared whole.** An editor reads a file as an open editor's draft first (refused when two open editors of it disagree) and writes it only if it's still that text, on disk or in every editor that has it open; an open editor is given the new text as if the file changed outside it, and starts again from it (its undo, as for any change from outside). An editor that can't take an edit (a version 1 map's offer, a file that didn't open) refuses one. What's written is held to the game's parse first. These are the version 1 overview's guarantees (conflicts, atomic writes to a closed file, an editor opening during a write, outside edits never overwritten), carried over to the game's files, and their host tests ported.
- **Undo spans files, and writes queue.** A step is the editor's own file and the other files the edit wrote, all or nothing (a write that fails puts back the ones before it); undo writes each back only if it's still what the edit made. An edit of another file shows at once and its write follows the last one, so typing in a field of the place laid on a map doesn't drop letters, and the typing in one field is one step there as it is in the editor's own file.
- **New files from the scenario** (a scene, a map, a place) are made from their skeletons (M3's scene, a map from the default legend, a place of one area) and listed; undo takes them off the list and leaves the file, as the version 1 overview did. A quest's key is made once from its title, since the scenes' effects name it. Unlisting asks first.
- **The legend is the palette, every entry its own.** An entry's name is its decor's, or its look and terrain ("Carpet floor under a palace roof"), with its character and its count. A new entry's character must be one you can see, not in the legend; it can't be changed after (the grid is written in it), and deleting one that's painted asks what replaces it. Blocks and opaque are "as its terrain", yes or no, since a door that blocks is an override the town uses. Keys the format doesn't know are kept in an entry and its decor; a decor's name and glyph are always written (the format requires them).
- **The default legend** is the version 1 palette and its clutter, as the spec says, the characters the town uses where it has them (`,` grass, `.` cobbles, `_` boards, `~` water…) and the clutter on cobbles (`l` a lamp, `s` a sign, `k` a crate, `b` a barrel, `n` a bench), each writing only what differs from its terrain's defaults. A new map is 32 by 24 of grass, the version 1 editor's blank.
- **The grid a row at a time.** The painter works on cells and writes back only the rows it changed; a changed row keeps its own length where what's past it is the void (the palace's first row stays five tiles), so painting a map changes its file where it was painted. The void is a palette entry like any other. The first paint and erase materials are the map's two most used entries.
- **The painter's look** is the game's: each entry's colours from the renderer's palette (client/map/palette.ts), lifted for the authoring view, its decor's glyph drawn on it, the void a dark outline. The atlas is made again when the legend changes.
- **Regions:** added from a tile's menu or drawn with the region tool (**N**), named in a dialog, their id a slug of the name free across the campaign's maps (the check's rule); a region's rect, origin and the rest are its inspector's. Selecting a region that lays a scene's place shows the place (the spec's "select a region"), and a button shows or hides it.
- **The start** is set from a tile's menu ("the spawn point is the scenario's start"), and lists the map in the scenario's maps when it isn't, if the file is `maps/<id>.kmmap`; on a map anywhere else it's refused, since the start can only be on one of the campaign's maps. Its flag on the map moves it.
- **Resizing** keeps the version 1 painter's handles, up to 160 by 120 and down to 2; new tiles are the Erase material. A north or west edge moves the map's regions (and their origins), exits and people, and also what other files say of its tiles: the scenario's start and other maps' exits that land on it, in the same step. Cropping asks, naming what goes (a region is cut to what's left of it).
- **A place on its map** is the region's scene's place at its origin, a spot in feet on the tile the game puts it on (`floor(feet / 5)` from the origin). Areas are rectangles with a label to drag; things with a spot of their own are markers by their kind; things held by someone or in something aren't drawn (they're a thing's contents, listed in its inspector); people are their looks (looks.json; the player `@`), someone without a spot at their area's corner until moved. A thing or someone dragged keeps where in its tile it was and goes into the area under it, but a held thing stays held. Removing a thing takes what's in it; an area goes only once it's empty; a person's start goes alone. A place opened by itself is drawn from above on its own grid (five-foot squares), not on a map: that's the spec's "edited on its own grid", and it names the regions that lay it.
- **A path naming a thing the place hasn't got** is a warning, the plugin's, as the game's side left it: the rules have no warnings and a via is words, so the campaign check is unchanged. A via of one word is a cast id (the rules' own check), so only a via of more than one word is weighed, against the cast (id, name, called, also known as) and the scene's place's things and areas (id or name, since "the tally loft" is an area), as words: no case, no leading article, nothing in brackets ("the counting-box" is "the counting-box (up a short stair, over the floor)"). A scene with no place (the interludes) has nothing to check. The warnings are listed apart from the problems, under them, and not in the tab's count, which stays the campaign check's; the toolbar counts both. The via picker writes a thing's or area's name, as M3 offered them, since its id would be one word. On the campaign that's nine warnings in four scenes, each a thing the scene's prose has and its place hasn't: left for the author.
- **A version 1 map's migration:** its id is its file's name, slugged (`Harbour study.kmmap` is `harbour-study`, as its version 1 id was). Each palette id used and each pair of a palette id and clutter is a legend character, only those used, as the spec says (the New entry dialog offers the rest as presets); the default legend's characters, so a migrated map paints like a new one, and clutter on anything but cobbles a character of its own, its clutter's in capitals if free (the study's lamps on grass are `L`). A spawn point becomes the scenario's start only when the author says so and the map is the campaign's (`maps/<id>.kmmap`); otherwise it's kept as a region of one tile named for it, so nothing is lost. An interaction whose exit led to a map is an exit (landing on the version 1 map's spawn it named, or just inside a map of the format), one whose exit led to a scene a region that plays it (opened by interacting, its port's name the label), and any other a region of one tile, its event message its description and its script notes kept as `notes`, a key the format keeps. The file's other keys (its lore, when it had any) are kept. Its id isn't checked against the scenario's other maps' regions' ids beyond the ones it makes (they're made free of the campaign's).
- **The user's study** is copied into the tests byte for byte, and the journey puts it in the harness's `maps/` as `harbour-study.kmmap`, so the start question is asked; the model test migrates it both ways and checks it tile for tile.
- **Problems in the map and place editors** are the campaign check's for their file (and for the place shown on a map), each taking the author to its region, exit, person or legend entry; and a note when the file isn't one of the scenario's or isn't named for its id.
- **The Game preview reaches the game's renderer through a JS module with a declaration** (`src/map/render.js`), as the version 1 preview did: importing client/map/render.ts from TypeScript would have the plugin's type check follow it into the client's code, which uses Node's timers and is checked under the game's options; CI's job has no root packages to give it those types.
- **The form styles are shared:** the scene editor's field, inspector and problems styles apply to the new editors too (`:is(.km-scene,.km-forms)`), rather than a second copy.

### Judgement calls

- **The board is JSON on the fight row, beside typed columns for what every client reads** (round, whose turn, the order, the status, the outcome, the last moment's number), **and a moment's details are JSON too.** The user will iterate on the fight: a field added to the board or a moment changes no schema (SpacetimeDB can't migrate a typed object column's new field), and a saved game in a page restores. V12's plan had the prototype's `CombatG` as JSON on a `fight` row; this is that, less the engine's campaign, which goes in `fight_rules` (private: it's the engine's whole event log, and changes with every move).
- **The fight plays on a copy of the player's own campaign** (rules_state's, into `fight_rules`), the setup's people made in it from their statblocks, and the encounter closed in it at the end (the prototype's `writeBack`, its engine half). It's written back to rules_state and the sheet only when the setup keeps the player (`keep`: the story's fights, later); the zoo's rings rest the player first (`fresh`) and leave their sheet as it was: practice. Everyone the setup names is made afresh: the story's people the rules already know (rules_state's `npcs`) aren't reused yet, since the rings' are their own; the story's fights will want the prototype's "the same person, if the same build".
- **One roll of the game's dice per move** (a player's act, or one of the other side's turns): the engine reseeded from the dice row's seed and its next count, as a check's is (`shared/rules/engine.ts opened`), every die in the move drawn from it; a roll outside the engine's planners (a blow at a lantern) has its own stream beside it, as a rest's hit dice do. The prototype kept one engine's dice state in `G.rng` across moves; this replays the same way from the rows.
- **The other side's turns are a scheduled reducer, not a procedure:** `fight_step`, run by a `fight_clock` row, as `tick_place` is by `place_tick`. A turn needs no HTTP, and a reducer is one transaction. One turn a step (the player's own while they're down too: its death save is its start), its row deleted as it runs, the next scheduled once this step's moments have had their time on screen (`stepDelayMs`, at most 5 s), so a spectator sees the same pace as the player; `fast` schedules them at once, for QA. The schedule table is `fight_clock` because SpacetimeDB refuses a table and a reducer both named `fight_step` (their generated types clash).
- **The player's moves are the map's own reducers on their turn**, as the brief asked: `walk({ to })` moves them, `attack` and `cast` take the action menu's ids (`attack@x,y` with the first weapon that reaches, as the prototype's `attack_<target>` did; `shove@x,y` knocks a brazier or a lantern down, the prototype's `use_<prop>`), and one more reducer, `fight_turn({ act })`, has the fight's own acts (dash, dodge, disengage, end_turn, yield, leave, auto), a typed string as `rest`'s length is, rather than a reducer each. The client's existing menu and aiming can drive a fight unchanged once it draws one. The menu's other acts (a feature, a leap, hiding, a rest, an item, a throw, a pocket) are refused in a fight with why, as the prototype's typed lines refused them; the map's other moves say there's a fight on.
- **A moment for each act** (`Skirmish.moment`): a move, an attack, a spell, a turn's start, a dash, a shove, a remark, a yield, the end. Its hits are what changed in it (each unit's hit points, state and conditions before and after, with the damage, healing and saves the engine reported), so nothing has to parse a dice line to animate it, as the prototype's `fightEffects` did. An opportunity attack splits the move it interrupts into three moments (the move up to it, the swing, the rest of the move). Moments with nothing in them (a round's end where no fire spread) aren't kept.
- **The caption is written from the moment's details** (`caption.ts`: who did what to whom with what, the damage, a fall, the dust going up), in the house style's plainness, so it says exactly what the dice did; the prototype's flavour lines (Sallow's "Regrettably", the Crakes' walking off) stay as the moment's lines beside the dice, and a remark's caption is its line.
- **Everyone's hit points are shown**, the other side's too: in the order on `observe`, and in the dice lines ("A bandit takes 10 slashing damage (HP 1/11)", where the prototype said "(badly hurt)"). The design note says why and the user can turn it back.
- **The player's turn passes by itself only once nothing at all is left of it** (no action, no attack of Extra Attack's still to come, under 5 ft of movement, no bonus-action spell they could cast), with a moment that says so; the prototype passed it once the action was used and the movement gone, whatever bonus action was left.

- **The zoo is the arena's rings only, started from any game out of a scene still being played** (`start_ring`, the host's move): there's no zoo campaign or hub yet (V19's), and the zoo is for QA and demos, so a ring can be rung from any game. - **The playback is the client's, from the moments as they come, and a pure function of the clock.** Each moment gets a slot (start, end) from when it arrived and the one before it ended; what's on screen at any instant is the moments ended folded in plus the one playing as far as it's got, so a frame mid-step is exact however it was reached (the screen check draws an instant), and finishing or skipping only moves slots. - **One way to act, the menu; walking direct.** A click on a blue tile walks; a click on someone opens the menu aimed at them (what can be done to them) rather than attacking at once, so the reach and targets are seen before it's done (the note's §6.3, the prototype's click that aimed first and attacked second).
- **The swing tiles are the simulation's** (`options.swings`): the path a walk takes there (the same search the move runs), leaving the reach of someone standing with their reaction ready and a melee weapon; none once disengaged. An approximation of the engine's own opportunity rules, but from the same paths.
- **Skip to your turn asks the simulation** (`hurry`): the other side's turns are paced by the simulation (each a step on its clock), so skipping only the screen's playback would still wait. Finishing a move is the client's alone. On the player's own turn `hurry` says there's nothing to hurry.

- **Reduced motion** shows each moment's end at once with its caption for the caption's least time (500 ms), no effects; the moments still come one after another so each caption can be read.

- **The camera** holds the whole ring when it fits, else sits between the action and the player, moving only when either nears the edge, eased a tile at a time.
- **Props and hazards are drawn as the rows have them,** not played: a lantern brought down is gone when its moment's rows come, not at its blow's beat.

The plugin keeps the scenario's `exhibits` as it is, and has no editor for it yet: it's JSON by hand.
- **The ring's scene is a real, small scene** (a start, a "Roll for initiative" ending and a walk-out), which the zoo never opens: it's where the cast live, and a GM could play a talk before the fight from it later.
- **No GM plays the zoo, so it goes to the simulation alone** (the scenario's `gm: "none"`): the AI service serves one campaign version per process and refuses another (409 other_campaign), and nothing in a visit asks it anything. An exhibit the GM plays (a scene) needs the AI service to serve both first.
- **A visit is a game of the zoo's, one a player at a time:** visiting deletes the last visit (a visit is for seeing an exhibit through, not for keeping), and the title keeps visits off its games (`my_games` says each game's campaign). The host is put on the exhibit's map with no lead and no scene waiting (`putOnMap`, split out of the story's start), and the game can be watched like any other.
- **"Entering the ring rolls initiative" is opening the exhibit:** from the title's menu, `npm run zoo -- ring`, `?zoo=ring` or `play new --zoo ring`, the fight starts at once. Stepping into the ring on its map doesn't (a map's region opens a scene, and the zoo as a place to walk between exhibits is still to come).
- **Again is `n`, other foes `o`:** on the fight's screen `r` is the log and `f` skips to the player's turn (the screen's own keys, built beside this), so the zoo's are n and o; once it's over Enter fights again too, where a story's fight goes back to the map (the ring's map has nothing on it once the fight's done). Again keeps the line-up and the pace; a new fight is a new fight to the client, played from its start. Headless they're `again` and `foes_<id>`.
- **Who you are is picked as you open the exhibit,** the fighter unless said: another build is another visit, so it's fresh and rested; there's no changing build in a fight.
- **The default line-up is Sallow and the Crakes** (the first in the list, and the brief's headline), then the Crakes alone and the dogs, as the user listed them. Without Sallow there's nobody paying the Crakes to stop, so they fight on (the AI's own rule). The dogs are the SRD's mastiffs (5 hit points, a bite that drags you down; no Pack Tactics, which the SRD's mastiff hasn't got).
- **The page's `?zoo` plays on the fake GM** unless `?fake` says otherwise: the zoo needs no GM, so asking for a key would only be in the way.
- **The ring is lit by its scene's time of day** (a fight's new `time`), so its sand is drawn in the afternoon: the fight's board drew any fight with a lit brazier at night, and the ring has one.

#### Judgement calls

- **The fallen are drawn by the look layer's own "down", not always an x:** it's how the town and the scenes draw someone out cold, and it keeps who it is (a lying sprite in Kingmaker Glyphs, their letter dimmed with a mark at their feet in a terminal); the fight's own letters (the arena's wolves and bandits) still tip over to an x. The headless board keeps its x.
- **A numbered one of a kind takes its campaign's letter and colour** (`client/look/map.ts`, `appearance.ts`): the cast table has one look for every Crake, and two identical Crakes on the board made "the silent Crake swings at you" a guess. Only a ref the cast table knows by its kind alone ("crake-2") does this; the town's Crakes, whose looks are the table's, look as they did. The look layer's cache now keys on the cast it infers from an id, so a ref'd and a plain actor of the same name aren't one look.
- **900 ms a caption:** at 500 the caption line changed before it could be read while the eye was on the board; a round of four others' turns now takes 10 to 12 s where it took about 8, and the simulation's spacing follows (one table). F skips; the number is the user's to tune.
- **Reasons for the odds are the fight's, not the engine's:** the engine says only that a roll had advantage or disadvantage, so the fight says why where it can tell (a pack's friend beside the target, the target on the floor, out cold or dodging; long range, someone beside the shooter, from the floor), and says nothing where it can't, rather than guess.
- **`told` beside `summary`:** the story's version stays third person for the GM's aftermath (combat-design.md §5); the player's is written beside it, so neither is a rewrite of the other.
- **An area's warning is the client's count of who stands on its tiles,** as the simulation will take them (everyone on them, up or down; the caster's own tile is never in an area that goes out from them); it doesn't know who Sleep's dice would reach.
- **Red, not rust,** for a walk that draws a swing: on the ring's sand the rust tint read as sand.
