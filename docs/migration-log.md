# The migration to one codebase

On 2026-10-17 the repo stopped being two codebases: the prototype (the "Kingmaker V2 PoC", at the root) and the production game beside it in `v2/`. The milestones:

- **M1:** the prototype is dropped, `v2/` is flattened to the root, and the site's `play/` is the game's browser build (below).
- **M2:** the scenes, re-authored as beats on the two-part GM.
- **M3:** the Obsidian plugin authors them.
- **M4:** problem reports.
- Combat later.

Each entry says what changed, where the old thing is in git history, and why where there was a choice. The prototype's last commit is `bc2ab1f`: `git show bc2ab1f:<path>` shows any file of it.

## M1: v2 is the game

## M2: scenes as beats, played by the two-part GM

### The format in the game (the scenes' foundation)

- **The format's code** is [shared/scene/document.ts](../shared/scene/document.ts) (the types as [scene-format.md](scene-format.md) has them, and a parse that holds a file to its shape and the references the game follows, naming the file and every problem), [load.ts](../shared/scene/load.ts) (`sceneOf`: a file as the runtime's Scene, extended rather than replaced) and [beats.ts](../shared/scene/beats.ts) (where a scene is: `reachedOf`, `stageOf`, `targetsOf`, `openRoutes`, `enteringAt`, `takeRoute`), the same code on both sides. Tested on the hanged clerk ([beats.test.ts](../shared/scene/beats.test.ts), [document.test.ts](../shared/scene/document.test.ts)).
- **GmState** gains `reached` and `twoPart` (roles, notes by turn, `moved`, `held`), kept as gm_state's `reached` and `twoPart` columns; the bindings are regenerated, the AI service's view carries them, and a scene opens with its start reached and as its stage.
- **The campaign is .kmscene files:** `scenes/<id>.kmscene`, read by the simulation's install and its scene defs, the AI service's campaign, the pack and the in-process host, the page's content (the allowlist takes `.kmscene`), the live record and the client's screen fixtures. The old folders (scene.json and Markdown: `git show 779c6b5:campaigns/caerwyn/scenes/<id>/scene.json`), the old loader and `SceneFile`, `RouteFile`, `CastFile` and `SceneInstall` are gone. The campaign check holds every scene to the format's rules (below), and [campaigns/README.md](../campaigns/README.md) says how to write and check a scene.
- **The scenes:** the hanged clerk as committed with the design; the Drowned Rat, the Heron warehouse and the King's morning copied in byte for byte from kingmaker-scratch/v2/m2/scenes, then their integration items in a commit of their own; the two interludes and the talk template converted from their folders by `tools/scene-folders.mts` (in history at `779c6b5`, deleted with the folders since nothing old is left to convert). The format's checker (`validate_kmscene.py`) finds no problems in the seven files.

### Judgement calls

- **The simulation keeps each scene's file whole** in its `scene_def` row (about 330 KB for the campaign), where it kept scene.json's structure without the Markdown before: the two-part GM's commits read words (a person's attitude and the stage's notes, for the roles it gives anyone new), so the simulation needs them. The install holds each file to the format's shape, then loads and validates it as the simulation reads it; SpacetimeDB took the larger install_campaign call in the e2e.
- **The conversion to the runtime's Scene** (load.ts), so the copied GM code keeps working: the start and the beats are its beats, each with the routes that leave it as its transitions and `anytime` empty, as asked; a route's condition is its `when`, and a route into a discovery with no condition reads "The player has come to understand this: <revelation>"; a cue is a cue, a dice route an attempt with its check (success to its target, failure to `onFailure`, its facts on success), anything else an outcome. The format has no words for how a check goes, so a check's success and failure are the stand-in's ("It comes off: <the node's title>."), and it has no reason (a route's label is a title, not "to …"), so the dice line is the roll's alone. Facts are sentences in transitions (the copied code's) and ids in the flat routes and the beats (the format's), with the scene's `facts` to look them up; essentials are the beats with `carry`, their paths' vias in order, each once, as where they're placed, and the cast among them as carriers.
- **The talk template.** The format has one description for the start, where the old template had an opening and a play note: the start's description is the play note then "As the talk opens: …" (scoped, so later turns don't greet again), and the GM's words for a talk's opening change accordingly (town.test.ts expects them). The conversion has a talk rule so the talk plays as it did: its ways into its ending are anytime attempts (the old goodbye was, judged before the reply from either beat), and its way into told is the talk's own (prepareTalk's confided route, worded for each talk), so the template's route there (which the file needs for told to be reachable) isn't a transition. The template has a truth written for it (the checker asks for one) and its goodbye is a good ending.
- **The interludes,** which the story skips: the downtime beat's play note and the opening are the start's description, its pressure the scene's clock (the format's start has no pressure), the old essential `lead` a discovery the story carries, with two paths written for it (Kell's errand, free; the town's gossip, for asking around) and a way in from the start, and Kell's `whyHere` written; `onward` is an outcome now (it was an attempt; it isn't played).
- **The tests of code that plays a scene have a hanged clerk of their own:** [shared/scene/testdata/hanged-clerk.kmscene](../shared/scene/testdata/hanged-clerk.kmscene), the old one in the format (its five beats stages with their old routes, a route of a later beat with an earlier one's id renamed `<id>-<beat>`, self-loops kept, `make-case` a start route, its essentials discoveries nothing leads to, no truth or whyHere), so they don't follow the campaign's content. What the format couldn't carry of it: a check's reason and words, a check's facts on failure, `given`, `essential` and `towards`, an attempt without dice, `make-case` beyond the yard, the yard's pressure.
- **The GM code's tests keep what the goldens were recorded on:** the old hanged clerk as the runtime's Scene with every word (ai/gm/testdata/scenes/hanged-clerk.json, beside the prototype's other scenes) and the vocabulary as it was (ai/gm/testdata/vocabulary.json). A file of the wrong shape is held to its shape alone, and the runtime's own validation runs only once the format's rules find nothing, so a problem isn't said twice.
- **Play semantics the design left open:** nothing is open once an ending is reached; `takeRoute` takes the turn too (for `closing` and the path), steps the path (source `post`), marks a carried beat reached as landed (so the boundary owes it no more: "a beat reached is landed"), refuses `failure` for a route without dice, and leaves a failed check with no `onFailure` open to try again. `presentCast` counts the nodes reached as visited.
- **The play semantics' tests are on the hanged clerk,** as asked, but its routes have no dice: the dice test adds two to a copy of it (a check at the cart that fails into the mixed ending, and a climb with nowhere to fail to). The Rat, the Heron and the King's morning have dice routes of their own.

### Judgement calls

- **The commands' composition root is `hosts/loop.ts`, not `qa/loop/cli.ts`.** The README's rule: the hosts are the only code that reads the environment or argv. qa/ is handed its config (the loop's folder, the stack's connections, its owner's rows, its campaign, its call log), and `qa/imports.test.ts` holds it to that and to its imports (node:, itself, client/, ai/, shared/).
- **An arm is a stack, not a prompt overlay.** The prototype's variants were prompt folders laid over one harness (`KM_PROMPTS_DIR`); the game's AI service takes no overlay. A round's arm names a dev stack's folder, and a candidate is a stack run from another checkout, so any change, prompts or code, can be an arm.
- **The loop has a stack of its own:** `runs/loop-stack` on ports 3330 and 8817, clear of a dev stack being played on (3300), the e2e (3310) and the arc (3320). It's the dev host run detached with `--no-keys`, ready when its screen is in its log. A round can name another (`--stack runs/dev`).
- **A later scene is reached through `end_scene`, not a new reducer.** The owner-only reducer the QA console's `end` calls already ends a scene through a named exit, and the story moves on through its real boundary. The loop picks the exit: the scene's first good or mixed ending, read from the runtime scene the AI service's loader gives (the M2 format's `result`). Today's scenes don't grade their endings, so until M2 an exit of kind progress or persuaded counts as good, leave as bad, and other and combat as ungraded. If the game refuses an exit, the next is tried. No module change, and it works on the fake and on real models.
- **The character is made from the persona's own sentence**, the GM picking the build, with no backstory, as the prototype's harness had none. That's reliable headless (the e2e and `npm run live -- --creation` do it). If it never takes after the backoffs, the persona's ready-made build is used instead, and the record says why.
- **The suggested lines are off for the loop's players.** The testers write their own lines, as the prototype's harness offered none. The rubric judges the GM's replies, not the lines, and it saves a model call a turn.
- **What a tester and a reviewer see:** the narration, the dice lines, the game's own lines, and each spoken line after its speaker's name as the player knew them then. On a player's screen that's the speaker's colour. The notes on marked words are left out, as the prototype loop left out its ⟨⟩ lines (and M2 drops the notes). A quote is checked against the turn's reply with the names or without.
- **A busy turn is waited on, not only retried.** The module already tries a failed model call again every 15 s for ten minutes. A 402 from the key's in-flight cap reaches it as the AI service's 500 model_failed. The headless verbs wait three minutes. So the loop waits on a busy turn a backoff at a time (20, 40, 80, 160 s), carries a failed turn on with `retry_turn`, and logs each wait on the turn with the 402s the call log shows. The day's ceiling (KM_AI_DAILY_USD) isn't waited on.
- **A fight a line brings on** is fought out by the game's stand-in, as fights aren't built yet; the tester sees its line and the aftermath. A scene that ends in a summons (the Heron's) is recorded as it last stood before the seat moved on.
- **The prep is the runtime scene written out loosely.** prep.ts shows today's fields, the M2 format's (truth, clock, a discovery's revelation and paths, the people's roles, graded endings, a route list), and any prose field it doesn't know, under its own name, so a new format drops nothing. The pack adds what the boundary handed on to a later scene (gm_state's adjustments, rewritten opening, lead, owed and absent, read as the stack's owner as the scene opens) and the player's notebook.
- **A say past the limit, or after the scene's end, exits 0,** as the prototype's did, so a tester agent doesn't take the game's answer for a failure and try again. A turn the game couldn't answer even after the waits says so and exits 1, and the tester is told to say the same line again in a minute: a line said again after its turn failed carries that turn on (retry_turn) and isn't said twice.
