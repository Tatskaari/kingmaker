# Fights in the story

A fight that breaks out in a scene is fought on the board, as the zoo's combat ring fights one (docs/combat-design.md, M6: the map's own tiles, a turn at a time, every move played out), and the scene picks up from how it went. The user (2026-10-18): "The turn based combat we have is really good. Can you get that in the game and give the GM the ability to trigger combat with a `roll_for_initiative()` tool", and "make sure the BBEG fight at the end triggers properly". Then, as it was built: "when you roll for initiative, the GM should make each character pick a side or flee", "each scene should have a set of characters that appear on scene when you start a fight so if you attack Cob or whatever, the guards come after a few turns or a round", "make it so the GM still narrates what happens in the log, and if I click on it, I can still narrate what I do", and "how do I use a bonus action? I think I have second wind".

A first cut fought the story's fights in the conversation instead, the GM narrating every turn through tools (perform_action, move_towards, end_turn, end_combat): it's on branch `claude/combat-in-conversation` (b3c2915), with its four QA rounds below. What it built that the board's fights keep: the GM's roll for initiative, the sides, the prep's fight on an event, the scene's reinforcements, who the story needs, and the fixes its rounds found in the GM.

## How a fight goes

1. **It breaks out.** Three ways:
   - **The GM rolls for initiative in a reply.** The mouthpiece's reply has a `roll_for_initiative` field, decided before its narration as `check` is: `{ foes, allies, flee, why, on }`, everyone here in one of the three by their ids (the cast, a group's members such as the Crakes, and the people and creatures in the world with no card: the re-sacking gang, Duchess). The player is always in it; the companion is beside them unless the GM sets them against them or has them run. It's a field of the reply rather than a function call because the reply is a JSON answer streamed as it's written: the same job as a `roll_for_initiative()` tool, with no extra model call. Its narration takes the fight up to its first blow and ends on "Roll for initiative!". A reply that says so in so many words with no field set starts it too (QA round toll2-1018), and so does one the scene closes on (it reopens: hobo1-1018). `on`, with a check, says which of its outcomes come to blows.
   - **A fight the prep planned.** An event with a `fight` (the Heron's "Blades out": Sallow and both Crakes against the player, Kell beside them) has its line-up told to the GM on every route into it; a reply's fight while one is open is that one (the scene goes there by its route), and the co-GM's move into it starts it after the settle. This is the BBEG fight.
   - **A combat exit** (the format still has the kind; no scene of the story uses one now): the companion beside the player, everyone else here against them.
2. **Everyone takes a side or runs.** Whoever the reply placed is placed; whoever reacted to the player's violence this turn keeps the side their reaction took (who went for the player is against them, who took their side is beside them, who fled or ran for help runs); the rest are the GM's to place before the fight is set out, in one call (the sides step, POST /v1/turns/sides: `pick_sides({ sides: [{ id, side }], narration })`, with_player, against_player or flee, held to the list; its narration is under the reply). Whoever the story needs never leaves: the companion, and whoever holds a discovery the scene can't end well without that the player hasn't made yet (shared/scene/beats.ts `storyNeeds`) run as the GM has them run, but hide from it on the board rather than leave the place (`SceneFight.stays`; smoke6-1018: the GM had Cob bolt behind the water butt, and the board had him out of the gate and gone), and cower where they hid once it's over. The sides' narration never names who's coming later (a sentence that does is dropped: ai/gm/sides.ts `withoutComers`). The scene's transcript says who's where: "Roll for initiative! Against you: … Beside you: … Running from it: …", and the scene is at its fight (`fighting`): no line is taken until it's fought.
3. **On to the fight.** The card under the reply ("Roll for initiative!", To the fight: fight_it_out) sets it out on the board (simulation/src/fight/scene.ts):
   - **the map** is the one that lays the scene's place (the region with its sceneId and worldOrigin), the region the player is in (the hanged clerk has the yard and the tally loft); a place that's a region of a bigger map (the yard on the town's) is fought in its own tiles (the board's `area`): nobody goes beyond them but out by a way out, and whoever comes in when the way in is taken comes in beside it (smoke7-1018: Old Wat in the lane, out of everyone's reach);
   - **everyone** where the place's world has them now, on a tile of their own, as the player knows them (by what they call them until they've heard the name), the scene's people as the rules' own characters when the rules have made them already (hurt as they are), anyone else from their card's statblock;
   - **how each is played** by the ring's AI: beside the player an ally (Kell puts the irons on the beaten); against them the leader of a group whose people fight with them the villain (when he yields, his people yield with him: Sallow and the Crakes), a bow an archer, a commoner a brawler (who gives up hurt), anyone else a bruiser; running from it a coward, who runs for the way out on their turn and is gone from the scene once off the board (one the story needs hides as far from it as they can get, in the place); keeping out of it a bystander, who stays out of it until the player turns on them;
   - **the place's** lit lanterns and braziers as what a fight can knock over, its dusty floors (the Heron's grain dust) and what burns (sacks, straw), and its ways out: the map's own exits, or a region of a bigger map's doorways (the yard's gate);
   - **the player's** hit points, slots and conditions kept: what the fight does to them stays.
4. **It's fought on the board**, a turn at a time, as the ring is. The player can turn on anyone on it, the running and those keeping out of it too (the board's blows are at the other side, and at them: "Attack Cob (Greataxe +5; keeping out of it, 5 ft)"); one keeping out of it they strike is in it now, against them (smoke4-1018: Krug swung at Cob, who kept clear, and the board had no blow at him). The player's turns from the action menu (or headless: `attack@x,y`, `cast_<spell>@x,y`, `walk@x,y`, `feature_<id>`, `dash`, `dodge`, `disengage`, `end_turn`, `yield`, `leave`, `auto`), everyone else's by the AI, each move played out. Class features are the engine's own options (the user: "how do I use a bonus action? I think I have second wind"): Second Wind, Cunning Action's dash and disengage, Rage and the rest are the menu's Bonus action lines, those that take the action (Lay on Hands, Turn Undead) its Action lines, and Action Surge (another action, for nothing) a Your turn line; each uses the engine's planner (its uses, the turn's action or bonus action) and is a moment of its own ("You use Second Wind: 8 hit points back.").
5. **The GM's voice on the board** (the user: "make it so the GM still narrates what happens in the log, and if I click on it, I can still narrate what I do"):
   - **Each turn is told.** As the board plays a turn (anyone's, the player's from the menu too), the GM tells it in the fight's log in the scene's voice: what they did and what came of it, as the dice decided (POST /v1/fights/narrate, a call a turn on the mouthpiece's model, its words after the turn's last moment, however late they come). The next of the other side's turns waits for it, six seconds at most, so the log reads in order; hurrying (F) doesn't wait.
   - **The player's turn in their own words.** On their turn, a click on the log (or t) opens a line: what they type is the GM's to play on the board (fight_say, POST /v1/fights/turn), a call a step through three tools: `act` with one of the board's options by its id (attack@x,y, cast_<spell>@x,y, feature_second-wind, dash, yield…), `move_towards` someone or something (or away), and `end_turn`. The board does each as the menu would (its refusal in the rules' words, for the GM to tell and go on from), and the GM is asked again with what came of it. The log has their words, the GM's attempt before what the board made of it, and the GM's telling of how the turn went; the menu waits meanwhile ("The GM is playing what you said…"). Headless, `say` in a fight is a typed turn.
6. **The scene picks up from how it went.** Out (Enter on the end, headless `out`) goes back to the scene (simulation/src/story/fight.ts): who went down is down in the place's world, who's hurt hurt, who gave up has, who's in irons is, who ran off the board is gone from the scene (bar those the story needs), everyone where they ended up, the lights the fight broke out; "The fight: <how it went>" in the transcript and the notebook; "The fight is over: <how it went>" settled; then the GM's aftermath (the opening's think with how it went, gm.aftermath) on a turn of its own, and the scene is live, the co-GM taking it on from there (the Heron to "Caught in the act" when Sallow's taken).

A fight that breaks out on the town's map (hurting a palace guard in the street: town-design §3.3) is set out on the board the same way, on the town's map where everyone stood; out goes back to the map, the town's people as the fight left them until the clock moves on.

**Who comes** (the user: "each scene should have a set of characters that appear on scene when you start a fight so if you attack Cob or whatever, the guards come after a few turns or a round"): the scene's `reinforcements` (docs/scene-format.md: the granary porters and Old Wat after two rounds in the yard, three dockers off the night shift after two at the Rat, two palace guards at the Marshal's shout after one in the King's dressing room). The GM places them with everyone in the sides step, told when they'll come and why, as they'd take it then (one who'd run doesn't come); the line under the reply doesn't name them (they're the board's surprise). On the board they're in the initiative order from the start but not on it ("coming": no turns, not drawn, not in the bar), and at the top of the round after their `after` rounds they come in at the way in (the place's ways out: the yard's gate), a moment of their own with why they came ("The granary porters and the old porter come into the yard behind the King's Granary."), and fight from their place in the order. Until then nothing names them: not the line under the reply, not the sides' narration, not the fight's opening or its initiative line, and nobody on their side counts them as fallen (a bruiser alone tests their nerve only once their friends on the board are down, not while the rest are still coming). A group fought as one (the porters) is plural in its captions. A fight that's over before they come is over without them; whoever came is in the scene's world after it (the cast by their cards, entered; someone new with their statblock and look).

## The zoo's exhibit

The Crakes' toll (`?zoo=toll`, `npm run play -- new --session <you> --zoo toll`, the title's zoo menu): the Crake brothers have the ring's gate and want a shilling each to let you and Kell out. Pay, talk them down, or fight: a fight is set out on the ring's own sand, and the scene's endings follow how it went (talked out of it, paid, the Crakes beaten, beaten back). The GM loop plays it as `--scene zoo/toll`.

## The GM loop's fights

The GM loop's testers fight on the board as a player would (qa/loop/play.ts): once a reply sets a fight out, `loop say` is their turn in their own words (the GM's to play), `loop act <id>` one of the board's ACTIONS (attack@x,y, walk@x,y, feature_second-wind, auto…), and `loop act out` goes back to the scene once it's over; fight moves don't spend their turns (40 moves at most a fight, then the dice see it through). The record keeps each fight's log, which the review pack and the report show under the turn it broke out on (the GM's tellings, the player's own words, the board's lines and dice), and a quote from it counts as that turn's. The rubric's Fights checks: `fight-start` (a fight when there shouldn't be one, or none when there should), `fight-telling` (the GM's telling isn't what the board did), `fight-turn` (the player's typed turn isn't what they said).

What still holds for the board's fights, since it's about what starts them:
- **Fights broke out when someone fought back, and not before**: a threat, or a blow that felled someone who couldn't answer it, started none.
- **The BBEG fight triggered in all six Heron games**, each with Sallow and both Crakes, from the player going for him or from Sallow drawing as he was exposed, from the reply or the settle.
- Fixed then and kept: threats read as blows by the act step; the closing reply that couldn't start a fight or resolve an act (it reopens the scene); the stage manager's copies of the cast (two Sallows; people who'd run come back as themselves); the initiative line giving a name away; reactions and fights sending away the people the story needs; the player's own words echoed as someone else's line.
- Still open from them: talk around a fight gives secrets up without a check (Hobb names Sallow at axe-point); the Rat's last resort ends the scene on "Hobb hurt" even with the lead in hand (a scene-authoring call); picks that go against the cards' limits.
