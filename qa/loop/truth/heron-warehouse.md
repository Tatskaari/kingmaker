# The fact sheet for this scene

A summary of what's public and what's held in this scene. The scene's prep below is the authority: where this sheet and a person's card, or a discovery's paths, differ, the card and the paths are right. What this sheet adds is what the prep doesn't hold: what the campaign's public lore lets anyone say, so that a person or the narration saying it is no leak. What the player knows coming in is what the scenes before left them, which the player's notebook and what the story handed on (both in the pack) say.

## What the player was told before the scene began (the hook)
Night, on the eve of the Assembly. The big man with the bandaged hand, Hobb Mallory, works nights for a gentleman at the Heron warehouse, at the downriver end of the wharves, and tonight a barge is loading there. If the King's 'lost' grain goes anywhere, it goes through those doors. Sergeant Kell is with you on the dark wharf.

The scene: night on the eve of the Centennial Assembly, an hour or so after the Drowned Rat, at the downriver end of Caerwyn's wharves where the lamps give out. The player is the King's investigator, with Sergeant Agnes Kell (unless she took Hobb to the cells at the Rat, and then she's coming along the wharf to meet them). The clock: at the midnight bell the Master comes in at the land door; at the second bell after it the Saltmere barge Patience casts off on the ebb with the King's grain, whoever has said what.

## What the player may know already (the scenes before)
If the granary and the Rat were ended for the test (the pack says so), each was ended with its discoveries made, so the story so far can have: Prout murdered, the "lost" wagons sold off under the countersignature "J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor", and Big Hobb Mallory at the granary that night; then Hobb as the big man from the loft (he held Prout's arms while his master struck and strangled him, and hauled the rope), **his master as Master Sallow of the Royal Purveyor's office**, his night work at the Heron (due on the hoist at the eleventh bell, in at the river door with a brass heron token), what would turn him (his mum kept safe, swearing for the Crown), and **Prout's page found in Cob's boot**. The Rat ended for the test on "What Big Hobb knows", so Hobb went to his night's work: he's on the Heron's hoist. The player or Kell saying any of that here is no leak, and Kell's notebook has whatever was owned to in her hearing. A scene ended for the test moves nothing in the world, though (an ending only records what it gives): the page itself stayed in the granary yard with Prout's boots, so the player doesn't carry it here, and a GM who goes by the world when they try to show it to Scrope isn't wrong. Knowing that the grain goes through the Heron isn't seeing it: the King's grain re-sacked, the Master's visit and the true book are this scene's.

## What anyone can see from the start (fair game for narration)
- From the dark wharf: the Heron, a tall tarred timber warehouse standing half on the wharf and half on piles over the river, under a painted heron on its signboard; lantern light through the cracks in its boards, with the grain dust swimming in it; a lamp in a high window (the counting-box).
- The land door: big double doors with a wicket in one leaf, a sentry box and a brazier beside it, an old watchman in a dozen coats with a red muffler over his left ear, a lantern and a rattle (Saul), and **a vast elderly brindle mastiff snoring across the threshold** (Duchess). One lantern at the door on an ordinary night; two, and men walking the wharf with a third, if the Heron is expecting trouble.
- The river side: a slippery plank walk along the piles to **the river door**, a low loading door at water level with a grille, where the gang come and go, showing a brass heron token at the grille for whoever's nearest inside to draw the bolt; above it the hoist beam swinging sacks down into the barge moored alongside, **the Saltmere barge Patience**, broad and low, hatches open, her bargees waiting for the ebb. The river high, fast and black, and the tide on the turn.
- A winch of a voice through the river door, shouting the count and calling for hands (Hep), before she's seen.
- Inside, for anyone who gets in (and glimpsed from the plank walk whenever the river door opens): one long floor lit by lanterns and thick with grain dust; **palace sacks stamped with the wheat-ear stacked down one side, new sacks stamped with a heron down the other**, the gang in grain-white slitting, pouring and sewing between them; a chute down through the river door to the barge; a heap of empty wheat-ear sacks by the river door; a brazier; a painted sign, NO NAKED LIGHTS, which everyone ignores; the hoist worked from just inside the river door; and up a short stair a glass-sided counting-box over the floor, where a narrow clerk in rusty black with a green eyeshade sits under a lamp, his left hand flat on a fat green-calf ledger (Scrope).
- Nobody gets a name until someone says it: the watchman, the dog, the forewoman and the clerk go by their look.

## Public knowledge (anyone in Caerwyn may know it: the lore marked public)
What any local knows, in the prep, is public, and so is what its setting shows of the place (its names wait until someone says them): the river door and its brass heron token, double pay for night loading in heron pennies, one lantern or two at the land door, the ebb at the second bell after midnight, NO NAKED LIGHTS (dust goes up like gunpowder), the Crakes' price list, that receiving the King's stolen goods is a hanging matter and so is murder, and that every Assembly somebody falls in the river. Beyond the prep, the lore marked public lets anyone say:
- **The Heron** (Places/Heron Warehouse): the heron is one Saltmere grain factor's house mark, new to Caerwyn this season (Saltmere's own banner is the salt-gull); his sacks go out stamped with it, and his people are paid in **new silver pennies with a heron where the King's face should be**, which spend in Saltmere and not in town; the factor never comes to Caerwyn; on a loading night the watchman's orders are to let nobody in at the land door, and the night gang use the river door; it needs strong hands on loading nights and doesn't care for questions; dock talk has it busy at night all this past month.
- **The people, as the wharf knows them** (Cast/…/public): **Saul Tench**, the Heron's night watchman, forty years in the river watch before that, who ends everything with "and all's well", talks to his dog and answers for her, walks the wharf at every bell calling the hour, and never goes anywhere without Duchess. **Hep Grist**, forewoman of the night gang, who shouts in numbers ("Forty to the hatch!"), never asks questions or answers them, takes on any strong pair of hands on a short night, and stands up for her gang against anyone. **Mister Lemuel Scrope**, clerk to the Heron, keeping its Saltmere factor's books in the glass counting-box (he has kept books on the wharves for one Saltmere factor or another these thirty-odd years), who counts everything twice, apologises to his ledger when he blots it, and always has his left hand on a ledger. **The Crake brothers**, two river-bruisers alike as bollards (Ezra talks, Enoch never has), half their price before and half after, barred from the Drowned Rat, who don't do children, dogs or anything on a Sunday before noon, and are lately in work most nights for someone who pays full rate. **Big Hobb Mallory**, the Rat's arm-wrestling champion, the size of a door, polite to a fault, lately keeping his right hand out of sight. **Kell**, the sergeant who writes everything down, and the constable Nan Quill put out through the Rat's door twenty years ago.
- **Master Sallow, as the palace knows him** (Cast/Master Josiah Sallow/public): the under-purveyor for grain and flour, fifty, pink, in a fur collar, with a silver-topped cane, a wheat-ear signet ring and so much lavender water you smell him a minute before you see him; he signs "J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor", has a key to the King's Granary, and walks fourth in tomorrow's procession beside a garlanded grain cart, his idea. What he does at the Heron isn't public.
- **The wharves** (Places/River Stairs, World/Caerwyn now, the night of the eve): warehouses with hoists over the water and river doors at water level; the grain warehouses at the downriver end where the lamps give out, the Heron the last of them; the Wharf Road winding down behind them, the only way wagons reach the wharves; after dark the wharves black and quiet but for the odd barge loading by lantern; the river watch's patch, walked in fours or not at all, and tonight up on the Assembly route with the whole watch; fog off the river after the eleventh bell; the Heron's hoist working over the Patience, which sails at the second bell after midnight; the Heron's gang grumbling that they're on double pay and still can't buy bread.
- **The grain crisis and the Purveyor's office** (World/Grain Crisis, World/Royal Purveyor's Office): a dozen King's Road wagons "lost to goblins" this month, each written off in the Royal Granary Roll and countersigned by the Purveyor's office; Saltmere barges going downriver full while bread is dear. **The wheat-ear is the office's mark**, on its sacks, wagon canvas, rings and purse silver, so a wheat-ear sack is the palace's; its officers answer to the palace, not the watch.
- **The watch's law** (World/City Watch): a sergeant who sees a crime done may arrest whoever does it, gentleman or not, out loud and with the proper words; otherwise an officer of the royal household can't be touched without a warrant under the royal seal.

## What's held (none of it narrated or said until it's legitimately uncovered)
The scene's truth, in the prep, says what happened. What can come out of it is the scene's three discoveries, each by its paths (who or what holds it, and what earns it, are in the prep):
1. **The King's grain, re-sacked** [kings-grain]: the gang slit the palace's wheat-ear sacks, pour the grain into the factor's heron sacks and send it down the chute to the Patience, and the empty wheat-ear sacks go in the river at the end of the night, weighted with stones; everyone who works here knows it, and nobody says it. It's in plain sight for anyone inside (narrating the floor to them is fair) and glimpsed through the river door; what the people keep is whose it is. Held by: a sack that splits on the hoist and spills wheat-ear sacking on the wharf (the wharf's pressure, free); the re-sacking floor (free to anyone inside who looks; seen with Kell beside them, something she can swear to); Hep (the count in the gang's cant, "wheat" in and "birds" out; "I don't know whose it is. I know how many there are."); Saul (only to a sergeant who treats him as an old colleague and tells him straight what he'd be answering for); Hobb (if he's here and on the player's side, away from the gang, never with the Master in the room).
2. **The Master comes at midnight** [master-due]: in at the land door with his lantern-boy and the Crakes, to see the last sacks down, check the count, initial tonight's page in the clerk's book and take his share in heron pennies. Held by: Hep ("Forty to go before the Master!", shouted at the gang, free to anyone in earshot); Saul (who's allowed in at his door, once Duchess has taken to them, or to anyone who asks why it's barred); Scrope (free to a question about the count that doesn't frighten him); Hobb, as the Rat left him; a bag of heron pennies counted twice and set aside on the clerk's desk.
3. **The proof: the true book** [true-book], required: Scrope's green-calf ledger with the brass clasp, every King's Road wagon the Granary Roll calls "lost to goblins" by Roll number and date, re-sacked and sold downriver, the price and the Master's share, **"J.S." in the Master's own hand on every page**, and at the foot of each month "Hedge-folk, the usual: forty heron pennies"; short of the book, a sergeant's own eyes on him initialling it, or the sworn word of those who'd hang beside him. The Heron's day book (anyone may see it), the barge's bill of lading and Sallow's order of sale all say "condemned stock, lawfully sold" and prove nothing on their own. Held by: Scrope (why the book exists is free to anyone who understands bookkeeping; the book itself only when Wendell's account balances: Prout's page shown, which he knows at sight, or what happened to Wendell made plain with a way through a frightened clerk can believe in); the counting-box (a look over his shoulder, up a stair where a hand has no business; lifting it unseen is a roll); Kell (her own eyes on the Master at the book, if the player puts her where she can see); Hep and her gang (swearing for the Crown, once they believe the Master will fall and those who swear won't hang); Hobb ("I held his arms, Master. I held his arms.", to the Master's face); Sallow himself (his pride correcting the record, in a sergeant's hearing; or, bluffed, showing off his arrangements).

The events: **On the floor** (in as one of Hep's hands, a player is crew: carrying, slitting and sewing with their head down need no roll, and only going where a hand wouldn't gets them looked at); **The Master comes** (the midnight bell: how he comes follows from the day, unhurried and bored, wary with two lanterns at the land door and the loading hurried, or warned, sharp, with the book to go aboard first); **Into the fire** (the true book burned or drowned, cornered).

Held too, on the cards: Sallow killed Prout (the cane head, then a cord, while Hobb held his arms) and never admits it, except to someone he believes is about to die; his cane hides a sword (he fenced for prizes thirty years ago); he pays the hedge-goblins forty heron pennies a month to keep the goblin story true; Prout wrote to the Royal Purveyor, whose letters Sallow opens ("I am the proper channels"); cornered with the book, he'd sooner put it in the brazier or the river. Scrope was Wendell Prout's oldest friend (apprentices together under the old Keeper of the Granary Roll, draughts every Sunday for forty years), doesn't believe he hanged himself, and knows why he died, not who did it or how; at their last game Wendell asked him whether the Heron was busy at nights and whose grain it took in. Hep knows whose grain it is and that receiving it hangs, skims a sack a night for the gang's families (a notch on the tally stick in her apron pocket), and her son Kit is in the gang. Saul is deaf on his left side and hides it, knows whose grain it is, can be bought, dearly, if it's a present for Duchess, and has never forgiven the Master for kicking her. Kell's secret, Prout's visit and her sending him to the palace, if it hasn't come out.

## Who knows what (each card is the authority)
- **Kell** knows the law of arrest (what a sergeant sees done, she may arrest anyone for; otherwise an officer of the royal household needs a warrant), that those who'd hang with the guilty can sometimes swear for the Crown (the King's to grant; hers to write down and speak for), that her whistle brings nobody but her tonight, and whatever was said at the granary and the Rat, in her notebook. She's no sneak and no climber.
- **Saul** knows his door's orders (nobody but the Master on a loading night, at the midnight bell), his rounds at every bell, that a token is for the river door (he sends anyone who shows one round by the planks), that Duchess knows the gang by smell and growls at the Master, whose lavender she smells a minute before his lantern-boy, and that the Patience sails at the second bell.
- **Hep** knows the count (two hundred sacks tonight, forty to go before the Master comes to see the last down and initial the clerk's count), the re-sacking and the empties going in the river, and that she'll put any strong pair of hands to work and ask who they are afterwards. She never says whose grain it is, or the Master's name, on the floor.
- **Scrope** knows the count and the day book, the true book and why it exists (both thieves insisted on it: "There is no honour among thieves. There is bookkeeping."), and that the Master comes at the midnight bell to check the count, initial the page and take his share. He never brings up Wendell himself.
- **Hobb**, where the Rat left him (on the hoist; in the cells and not here; or at the Master's heels at the bell), knows his own part of the night: the hoist, the river door and its token, the Master in at the land door at the midnight bell with the Crakes, and that the clerk writes every sack and the Master initials it. He can't read.
- **Sallow**, from the midnight bell, has his order of sale in his own hand, the Captain who files what the office asks him to file, the Crakes and their price; he doesn't see porters, clerks or anyone with a sack on their shoulder, and talks like an official letter.

## How the GM is supposed to disclose
- People know only what they've seen, been there for, or been told in their hearing. Nobody asks about, names or guesses at something they haven't heard of.
- Narration shows only what the player character can see or hear; it never reveals what's hidden, not even to say it is still hidden.
- Reveals land in steps, one new thing per reply, only as far as the player's question reaches.
- People steer the player through what they want and fear (Hep's count, Scrope's look at the land door, Saul's rounds, the bells, Duchess's nose): that's how the scene hints. Nobody coaches the player out of character, or hands over what they hold before it's earned. Kell says where she'll be, once, backs the investigator's play and never chooses their next move.
- The GM never decides what the player character says, does, wants or intends, and never treats the player as having asked for or mentioned something they didn't.
- Coin from a stranger buys Hep nothing, Saul only dearly and as a present for Duchess, and Scrope nothing beside a page that balances; it's Sallow who offers money. He never admits the murder, but get his arrangements wrong in front of him and his pride corrects the record ("Nothing has ever been lost on my roads through carelessness. Only through goblins.").
