# The fact sheet for this scene

A summary of what's public and what's held in this scene. The scene's prep below is the authority: where this sheet and a person's card, or a discovery's paths, differ, the card and the paths are right. What this sheet adds is what the prep doesn't hold: what the campaign's public lore, which the GM had, lets anyone say.

## What the player was told before the scene began (the hook)
This morning a palace clerk, Wendell Prout, was found hanged behind the Caerwyn granary, and the Watch Captain has already called it suicide. King Aldren, who remembers how Prout took his tea, asked you quietly to 'see he's done right by' and lent you a sergeant of the watch. She's waiting for you in the granary yard. So, by the sound of it, are some children.

The scene: mid-morning in the cobbled yard behind the King's Granary, Caerwyn, on the eve of the Centennial Assembly. The player is the King's investigator, lent Sergeant Agnes Kell of the watch. The dead-cart (Corporal Tull and two constables) comes at the noon bell to take the body away as a suicide.

## What anyone can see from the start (fair game for narration)
- Wendell Prout, a small grey-coated clerk with ink-stained fingers and **no boots** (darned stockings), hanging from the hoist beam's hook above the shut loft door, his feet at about head height. Nothing stands beneath him: the yard was swept bare for the Assembly. The hoist rope runs over the beam's pulley and in through a slot above the loft door, so it's tied off inside.
- His satchel open and empty by the gate; his spectacles bent in the gutter. The big yard doors padlocked. A water butt in a corner. The yard wall, shoulder-high on the lane side.
- A scrawny boy of about eleven (Cob) in a man's waistcoat and **good black clerk's boots three sizes too big**, laced with string, throwing gutter stones at the body; a bruise on one cheek. The left boot **crackles** when he stamps (his card's tell: fine to show).
- A sharp-faced girl of about thirteen (Moll) on top of the wall, one fist kept tightly closed, her eyes going to the gate.
- Sergeant Kell with her notebook. The Watch Captain has called it suicide, and the dead-cart comes at noon.

## Public knowledge (anyone in Caerwyn may know it: the lore marked public, which the GM had)
- **The yard** (Places/Granary Yard): the hoist beam and loft door; **the tally loft behind it, where the under-clerk of the Royal Granary Roll keeps the palace's grain accounts, the Roll a great book chained to the tally desk**; the padlocked yard doors, whose keys the warden and the Purveyor's under-purveyors have; the water butt, wall, gate, lane, cart shed and old elm. Who's about: the porters (one is **Nat Pike**, a porter with a thirst and heavy hands), the granary warden **Mistress Ida Garner** (at the palace this week counting Assembly provisions), the Purveyor's carters under wheat-ear canvas, and the granary rats (children who run errands, pinch things and sleep rough; the watch takes them up for the Mending House). Fewer wagons this month, empty bays, and porters who resent it.
- **The town this morning** (World/Caerwyn now): bunting going up, the whole watch lining the procession route, and "After the Assembly" the answer to everything; bread at tuppence, a penny dearer than at harvest; the porters' talk that a palace clerk hanged himself off the sack hoist and the dead-cart comes at noon; the carters' that nobody takes the King's Road after dark.
- **The grain crisis** (World/Grain Crisis): a dozen King's Road wagons this month **"lost to goblins"**, each written off in the Royal Granary Roll and countersigned by the Purveyor's office; Saltmere barges going downriver full; dear bread. That the wagons go missing and are written off is public. **That they were sold downriver, that the countersignature on them is "J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor", and that Prout found out are not.**

## What's held (none of it narrated or said until it's legitimately uncovered)
The scene's truth, in the prep, says what happened. What can come out of it is the scene's three discoveries, each by its paths (who or what holds it, and what earns it, are in the prep):
1. **He was murdered** [murder]: struck on the back of the head, strangled with a cord, then hoisted up on the sack hoist to look like a suicide. Held by: the body (the torn, dust-grey heels of his stockings, to a careful look from the yard; the level cord mark under the rope's slanting burn, the blow to the back of the skull and his clean palms, only up close); the tally loft (a smashed lantern, spilled ink, heel scuffs to the hoist door, the rope tied off with a docker's knot); Cob (what he heard from the loft); Moll's story, which doesn't hold up (it drizzled all night, the loft door is barred inside, Prout had no debts).
2. **The motive** [motive]: the wagons were written off as lost and sold off, each countersigned "J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor", and Prout had found out. Held by: **the page razored from the Roll, sewn into the lining of the left boot Cob is wearing** (nobody alive knows it's there); Kell's secret (Prout's visit three days ago); Cob (he watched Prout cut a page from the big book and sew something into his boot); the tally loft (the Roll with a page cut out, the pages either side countersigned "J. Sallow"); Tull's written order, once the dead-cart has come ("at the request of Master J. Sallow of the Royal Purveyor's office").
3. **Hobb** [hobb], the scene's required discovery: Big Hobb Mallory, the Drowned Rat's arm-wrestling champion, his right hand bandaged, was at the granary with the gentleman when Prout died. Held by: Cob (a big man in the loft who said "Yes, Master": he describes him, never names him); Moll (a big man with a bandaged hand, who called the gentleman "Master Sallow"; only once she's admitted the payment); the porters at the far gate (Old Wat saw Hobb come out of the yard gate with a gentleman before first light); Kell, who knows the name from the river stairs' talk and says it only once a description with his size or the Rat's arm-wrestling reaches her.

Held too, on the cards: the gentleman's name and look (**"Silvertop"** to the kids: a silver-topped cane, a flower smell, a wheat-ear signet ring; **Master Sallow**), who never appears in this scene, nor does Hobb; **Moll's crown**, a silver crown stamped with a little wheat-ear, in her fist, paid at first light for her story; **the boots are Prout's**, pulled off the body by Cob and Moll at first light; **Cob sleeps in the loft**, in from a loose board behind the water butt, and heard it all.

## Who knows what (each card is the authority)
- **Kell** knows what her card's Knows list says: Prout (under-clerk of the Granary Roll; fussy, honest, no debts), the porters found him at first light, the palace reported him missing the evening before, the Captain's suicide and the noon cart, why nobody got up to him, who the kids are (Cob's father a porter with a thirst and heavy hands), that wheat-ear silver is Purveyor's purse money, and that "J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor" is a signature on the Assembly delivery notices (a name, not a face). Her secret, told once and only when earned: three days ago Prout asked her how a clerk reports "false entries in the King's rolls", and she sent him to the palace ("I should have listened"). She does **not** know Sallow by sight, anything about a page or what's in the boots, or what the kids saw; she knows Hobb's name, not the man, and names him only when a description reaches her.
- **Cob** tells only when it's earned (his card says what opens each thing, in pieces, over several questions): that he sleeps in the loft; what he heard (Silvertop wanting "the page", Prout saying "proper channels", a thump, choking, the hoist creaking); who (Silvertop by sight and smell, and a big man with a bandaged hand who said "Yes, Master": he never says the big man's name); Prout cutting a page from the big book and sewing something into his boot; the loft as it is now. He does **not** know Silvertop's real name, or that the page is in his own left boot.
- **Moll** knows the gentleman who paid her (his cane, ring and lavender; the big man called him "Master Sallow"), that her story is a paid lie, that Cob was in the loft and heard it, and that they took the boots. She breaks for Cob, never for herself, and doesn't know the big man's name.
- **Tull**, who arrives with the dead-cart, carries the Captain's written order: "The death of W. Prout, clerk, to be closed as self-murder, without fuss, at the request of Master J. Sallow of the Royal Purveyor's office." He shows it, won't hand it over, and anyone may copy it while he eats.

## How the GM is supposed to disclose
- People know only what they've seen, been there for, or been told in their hearing. Nobody asks about, names or guesses at something they haven't heard of.
- Narration shows only what the player character can see or hear; it never reveals what's hidden, not even to say it is still hidden ("the page stays in the boot").
- Reveals land in steps, one new thing per reply, only as far as the player's question reaches.
- People steer the player through what they want and fear (a warning, a demand, the noon deadline, a slip, a glance at what they guard): that's how the scene hints. Nobody coaches the player out of character, or hands over what they hold before it's earned.
- The GM never decides what the player character says, does, wants or intends, and never treats the player as having asked for or mentioned something they didn't.
- Coin or food offered to Cob as a price buys boasts and haggling, not trust, and never the look in his boot (his card): a kindness with nothing asked back, a polite ask with the boots staying his, or a promise that answers his fear is what gets the left boot held out.
