# The fact sheet for this scene

A summary of what's public and what's held in this scene: the King's thanks, the prologue's last scene, supper with King Aldren in his parlour. The scene's prep below is the authority: where this sheet and a person's card, or a discovery's paths, differ, the card and the paths are right. What this sheet adds is what the campaign's public lore lets anyone say, so that a person or the narration saying it is no leak.

## What the player was told before the scene began (the hook)
Guardsman Wren has brought you in at the city gate, up through streets hung with bunting and into the palace by a side door, to tell the King himself what happened on his road. A footman has taken your coat. The King, you're told, is at supper, and said to bring you straight in.

The scene: the same evening, in the King's parlour in the palace. It comes to the player (it opens as the toll bridge ends).

## What the player may know already (the scenes before)
The road's, as it went: Fitch, Tansy and the cushions; the goblins at the crossroads and the bridge, "Be seen!", and, if they found them, the heron pennies, Longlegs and Garrow's ledger; Corporal Haskett and Guardsman Wren by name. The player saying any of it here is no leak; the King and Holt know only what Wren and the player tell them.

## What anyone can see from the start (fair game for narration)
- A small, warm, over-furnished parlour off the long gallery: a coal fire, two armchairs (on the King's, a green Assembly cushion), a writing table buried in Assembly papers with, at its corner, a squared-off pile of letters under a card that says "After the Assembly"; a tea tray and a supper tray; the city's lamps through the windows.
- King Aldren: sixty, round and kindly, crumbs on his waistcoat, spectacles on his forehead. Marshal Garran Holt by the window with his pocket watch. Wren at the door. The King and the Marshal are public figures: their names and looks are fair game.

## Public knowledge (anyone may know it: the lore marked public)
- **The King** (Cast/King Aldren/public): he remembers how everyone takes their tea, takes his guests' comfort seriously down to the cushions, can settle any question that doesn't frighten him, puts the big ones off until after the Assembly, and signs nothing that might upset anyone.
- **The Marshal** (Cast/Marshal Garran Holt/public): speaks in times and quantities, never late, runs the Assembly's arrangements by pocket watch.
- **The palace and the Assembly** (World/Royal Palace, World/Centennial Assembly, World/Caerwyn now for the evening two days before): the delegations dining in the Great Hall, the King going down for the toasts at the ninth bell; the palace guardroom's tale of the traveller who saved the cushion patrol; "after the Assembly" as the palace's answer to everything.

## What's held (none of it narrated or said until it's legitimately uncovered)
1. **After the Assembly** [after-the-assembly]: the King puts off whatever frightens him until after the Assembly, the goblins and the "lost" wagons among them, and a pile of letters. Held by: the King (when the goblins or the wagons come up), Holt ("After the Assembly, Majesty"), the card on the pile (free to a look).
2. **The King's grain clerk** [wendell]: his grain clerk of twenty years, Wendell Prout (milk first, one sugar, the cup warmed), wrote to him a week ago asking for an audience about "irregularities in the Royal Granary Roll", and the King has put him off until after the Assembly. Held by: the King (fondly, once the grain wagons or "lost to goblins" come up, or asked kindly about the pile), the letter near the top of the pile (shown by the King if asked kindly; read unseen, a roll).

Held too: the King moves Wendell's letter to the bottom of the pile and back again; it frightens him a little, because irregularities in the Roll would mean deciding something. **Nobody here knows that Wendell will be dead by morning**, and nothing in this scene should hint at it: a GM foreshadowing the murder is a fault. The King signs nothing and gives no commission tonight; he gives thanks, a purse of five silver crowns and a room in the palace.

## If it goes wrong
Steel drawn or a blow struck in the parlour, the King threatened, his letters snatched and read aloud to mock him: two palace guards come at Holt's shout, and the last resort is "Shown the door", which ends the story. Rudeness, a raised voice or an awkward question the King forgives.
