# The fact sheet for this scene

A summary of what's public and what's held in this scene: the King's Road, the first scene of the game and of the prologue. The scene's prep below is the authority: where this sheet and a person's card, or a discovery's paths, differ, the card and the paths are right. What this sheet adds is what the campaign's public lore lets anyone say, so that a person or the narration saying it is no leak.

## What the player was told before the scene began (the hook)
You're riding the last miles of the King's Road into Caerwyn for the Centennial Assembly, on the back of a farm cart piled with three hundred cushions for the Assembly's seats, beside the carter, old Barnaby Fitch, and his granddaughter Tansy, who has never seen a city, or a king. The sun's getting low. Ahead is the crossroads, and its blackthorn hedges.

The scene: late afternoon two days before the Assembly, at the crossroads nine miles out of Caerwyn. There's no story before it: the game opens on this scene, on the cart, and the player's character has never met anyone in it (their backstory says why they're going to the Assembly).

## What the player may know already
Nothing of the story: this is its first scene. Their own backstory is theirs.

## What anyone can see from the start (fair game for narration)
- The crossroads: frozen ruts, a milestone (CAERWYN IX), an empty gibbet with its chain creaking, blackthorn hedges taller than a man on both sides of the road, with gaps in them; the drovers' track going off north; the light going amber.
- Fitch's cart, piled with green cushions in bundles of ten under sacking, Bramble the old bay mare in the shafts; Fitch up front with the reins and a cudgel across his knees; Tansy on top of the cushions with a wooden horse.
- Barnaby Fitch and Tansy by name: they're the hook's, and they say their own names.
- The cushions are for the Assembly's seats, and the King ordered them himself (Fitch has the letter in his hat).

## Public knowledge (anyone may know it: the lore marked public)
- **The King's Road** (World/King's Road): Garrow's toll bridge a mile on toward the city, a humpbacked stone bridge with a toll house, the only crossing for miles; the burnt mill half a day north, where goblins live, people say; no patrols on the road this week (the Marshal has called them in to line the Assembly route); carters won't drive after dark; a dozen palace grain wagons "lost to goblins" this month, and farmers who've never seen a goblin carry off a sack of wheat; the hedge-goblins call people "longlegs" and love anything shiny.
- The scene's `known`: no patrols this week; "lost to goblins" and the carters' doubt; Garrow's penny and his book.
- **The King, as anyone knows him** (Cast/King Aldren/public): round, kind, remembers how everyone takes their tea, takes his guests' comfort seriously down to the cushions, and puts the big things off until after the Assembly.
- **Fitch and Tansy, as they're seen** (their public profiles): an old Nine Furrows carter who carried a pike for King Edric; his granddaughter, nine, out of the Nine Furrows for the first time.

## What's held (none of it narrated or said until it's legitimately uncovered)
The scene's two discoveries, each only once the goblins' fight has begun (their routes open from the ambush):
1. **Paid to be seen** [be-seen]: the goblins weren't after the cart or the cushions; they were paid to make a show on the road ("Be seen!"), in new silver heron pennies, by someone they call Longlegs (a tall one in a grey hood who smells like flowers). Held by: the pouches round the fallen goblins' necks (heron pennies, free to a search); a goblin held down and offered its life (Nabber, or one of his); Fitch, once the fight's over (forty years and never a goblin carrying grain); Tansy (she heard them shout it). Nobody here knows who Longlegs is or what heron pennies are: a GM saying either is a leak.
2. **Smoke at the bridge** [the-bridge], required: the goblins came from Garrow's toll bridge a mile on, where their war-band and a war-boss have the King's patrol pinned; smoke is going up over the river. Held by: Tansy (she sees the smoke first), Fitch (the palace said the one patrol would meet the cushions at the bridge), a begging goblin, the smoke itself (free to anyone who looks east after the fight).

Held too, on the cards: Fitch's hands shake once the light goes (he'd own it only to someone who fought beside him); Nabber kept back some of the band's pennies in his pot-helmet. The goblins never take grain.

## The fight
- The ambush is the prep's planned fight (the event "Goblins in the hedge"): within the first two or three exchanges, an arrow hits the cushions and Nabber's hedge-gang (three goblins, one with a bow) come out of the hedge. Fitch fights beside the player; Tansy hides and never fights. Two more goblins may come up the road behind after a couple of rounds. It's fought on the board, the GM telling each turn.
- Goblins hurt, or with two of their own down, run east toward the bridge. A goblin held down begs and tells.
- If the player loses, the story goes on: Fitch gets them back on the cart, and the scene ends "Beaten up", on to the bridge anyway.
