# The fact sheet for this scene

A summary of what's public and what's held in this scene: Garrow's toll bridge, the prologue's second scene. The scene's prep below is the authority: where this sheet and a person's card, or a discovery's paths, differ, the card and the paths are right. What this sheet adds is what the campaign's public lore lets anyone say, so that a person or the narration saying it is no leak.

## What the player was told before the scene began (the hook)
A mile on from the crossroads, the King's Road crosses the river by Garrow's toll bridge, and that's where the smoke is coming from. Somewhere behind you Fitch is getting his cart moving. Ahead: shouting, a drum, a horn, and goblins.

The scene: dusk, the same evening as the crossroads. The player comes up the road alone; Fitch and Tansy follow with the cart and arrive once the bridge is clear.

## What the player may know already (the scene before)
The crossroads', as it went: the goblins' ambush, Fitch and Tansy by name, and, if they found it, the heron pennies on the goblins, that someone called Longlegs pays them to be seen on the road, and that the goblins' war-band is at the bridge. The player or the GM saying any of that here is no leak. If the crossroads was ended for the test (the pack says so), the player may know none of it but the hook.

## What anyone can see from the start (fair game for narration)
- The bridge: humpbacked stone over a river in winter spate, its parapet long gone; Garrow's whitewashed toll house at the near end, shuttered and barred; the red-and-white toll pole across the road, burning; a hay wain dragged across the road at the bridge foot, goblin arrows in its boards.
- Behind the wain, two palace guards in steel caps and the King's green, one with a snapped-off arrow in her shoulder; round the toll-house corner, three goblins with their backs to the road, a drum and a bow; a voice through the shutters about the King's penny.
- Across the river, unseen beyond the willows, a horn and something that howls.

## Public knowledge (anyone may know it: the lore marked public)
- **The King's Road and Garrow** (World/King's Road, Cast/Garrow/public): Garrow takes the King's penny (a silver penny a head), the Assembly levy and the Marshal's two pennies a weapon, and writes everyone in his book; carters call him the most disagreeable man in three counties; no patrols on the road this week but this one; the palace's grain wagons "lost to goblins".
- **The patrol, as they're seen** (Cast/Corporal Maud Haskett/public, Cast/Guardsman Tobias Wren/public): a veteran palace guard corporal and her nephew on his first patrol. Their names are theirs to say.
- The scene's `known`: the gates shut at the ninth bell this week; the King's green goes through any gate; the King takes his guests' comfort seriously, down to the cushions.

## What's held (none of it narrated or said until it's legitimately uncovered)
The scene's two discoveries, each only once the rearguard's fight has begun (their routes open from it):
1. **The King's patrol** [the-patrol]: Haskett and Wren are the only patrol on the road this week, sent at the King's own wish to bring the Assembly's cushions in; the goblins shot their horses an hour ago, and the war-boss is waiting across the river. Held by: Haskett and Wren (free once the rearguard is beaten), Garrow through his shutters (all written down).
2. **Garrow's book** [garrows-ledger]: his ledger has twelve palace grain wagons crossing his bridge inward to the city this month, laden, every one paid: whatever "lost to goblins" means, it didn't happen on his bridge. Held by: Garrow (asked about the goblins, the road, the wagons or his book), his ledger (read with his leave; over his shoulder, a roll). Nobody here knows what it means, and the GM saying where the wagons went is a leak.

Held too, on the cards: Haskett's arrow went deeper than she lets on; Garrow's own tally of the goblins and their war-boss's name, Grisk (which the goblins shout); what Grisk knows of a lady rider Chief Gubbin took at a ford a week ago for the longlegs (he'd trade it for his life). Nobody here knows the longlegs' name.

## The fights
- Two of the prep's planned fights, fought on the board, the GM telling each turn. First the rearguard (Wartle and two goblins, one with a bow) against the player, with Haskett and Wren beside them, as soon as the player acts. Then, within about three exchanges of it, Grisk on his worg with a spearman over the bridge, against the player and whoever's standing. Two Kläggenheim axemen from a delegation's baggage train may come up the road after a few rounds, on the player's side.
- The bridge has no parapet: a shove can put someone in the river, gone. Grisk quits or bargains once his worg is down or he's badly hurt.
- If Haskett goes down she's badly hurt, not dead; what the player does for her matters.
- However it goes, Wren takes the player to the King: "Taken to the King" if they won, "Carried in" if they went down.
