# The fact sheet for this scene

A summary of what's public and what's held in this scene: the zoo's Crakes' toll (`zoo/toll`), the game's exhibit of a fight in the conversation (docs/combat-gm.md). The scene's prep below is the authority: where this sheet and a person's card differ, the card is right.

## What the player was told before the scene began (the hook)
The ring's gate, and the two very large men standing in it.

The scene: the zoo's combat ring after hours. The player and Sergeant Agnes Kell want to leave by the only gate, and the Crake brothers are leaning on it, demanding a shilling a head. There's no story before it: the visit opens on this scene, as one of the zoo's ready-made characters (the fighter, the wizard or the rogue).

## What anyone can see from the start (fair game for narration)
- Raked sand inside a stone wall twice a man's height, two squat stone pillars, an iron brazier cooling on three legs, and the timber gate in the south wall: the only way out. The wall is too high to climb out.
- Two enormous identical men leaning on the gate, each with a cudgel. One jingles a dented tin cup with a few coins in it and does the talking; the other cracks his knuckles and says nothing.
- Sergeant Kell beside the player, coat off, truncheon in her belt.
- The toll: a shilling a head, "uniforms is extra, but we'll waive it".

## Public knowledge
- **Their surname is public:** the scene's title, which the player sees, is "The Crakes' toll", and the prep's `known` says the Crake brothers are hired muscle off the river, big, slow to start and very hard to stop. "The Crakes" or "the Crake brothers" in the narration is no leak.
- The ring's wall is twice a man's height: the gate is the only way out (the prep's `known`).
- Kell's view, which she may say: demanding money at a gate is extortion.

## What's held (none of it narrated or said until it's legitimately uncovered)
- **Their first names**, Bram (the talking one) and Tam (the silent one): the player calls them the big man with the tin cup and the other big man until a name is said in their hearing.
- **That nobody hired them for this:** it's their own idea. The talking one may boast of it, if asked.
- **What they'll settle for:** a shilling each, a shilling for the pair if haggled, nothing if they're convincingly threatened (a blade out, a spell ready, a sergeant's warrant) and can back down without losing face. They fight only if someone swings first, or the talking one is mocked in front of his brother. Once one is down, the other gives up.
- The scene has no discoveries: its endings are how the player gets out (talked down, paid, the Crakes beaten, or beaten back).

## The fight, if it comes to one
- The GM rolls for initiative in its reply (the reply ends on "Roll for initiative!"), and the game rolls initiative for everyone in it: the player, Kell (on the player's side unless set against them) and the Crakes. Each turn after that is narrated by the GM, with the rules' dice lines (attacks, damage, saves) shown as the player sees them. The dice decide: a hit lands, a miss misses, and someone is down only when the dice line says "down". "Hurt" and "badly hurt" are still standing.
- Each Crake has 11 hit points, AC 12 and a club (1d4 + 1). Kell is a watch guard (AC 16, 11 hit points, a club). A narration that has someone fall, flee or surrender without the rules having said so is a real fault, as is one that says a hit landed that the dice missed, or plays someone's turn out of order.
- How it ends: the rules end it when one side can't fight on; the GM may end it sooner if someone surrenders or runs. Then the scene goes back to conversation, and the co-GM takes it to the ending the fight earned: "The Crakes beaten" (won, or they gave up) or "Beaten back" (the player down or fled).

## Who knows what (each card is the authority)
- **Kell** knows tolls at a gate are extortion and would arrest for it; she won't pay and won't start the fight, and fights beside the player if it comes to blows, arresting whoever gives up.
- **The talking Crake** knows it's their own idea, his rates, and that his brother follows him.
- **The silent Crake** says nothing at all.

## How the GM is supposed to disclose
- People know only what they've seen, been there for, or been told in their hearing.
- Narration shows only what the player character can see or hear, and calls the Crakes what the player would until their names are said.
