# The fact sheet for this scene

A summary of what's public and what's held in this scene. The scene's prep below is the authority: where this sheet and a person's card, or a discovery's paths, differ, the card and the paths are right. What this sheet adds is what the prep doesn't hold: what the campaign's public lore lets anyone say, so that a person or the narration saying it is no leak. What the player knows coming in is what the scenes before left them, which the player's notebook and what the story handed on (both in the pack) say.

## What the player was told before the scene began (the hook)
This morning a palace clerk, Wendell Prout, was found hanged from the sack hoist behind the King's Granary, and you learned there that a huge man with a bandaged right hand was at the granary with a gentleman the night he died: Big Hobb Mallory, champion of the arm-wrestling at the Drowned Rat on the river stairs. At the noon break Sergeant Kell, the watch sergeant the King lent you, saw a gentleman with a silver-topped cane walking beside the Purveyor's wagons, and at his elbow a big dock-man nursing a bandaged hand, who called him 'Master'. Prout went up on a hoist rope, and Kell reckons a rope takes the skin off the palm of whoever hauls on it. She followed the big man down the river stairs to the Drowned Rat. It's evening on the eve of the Assembly, and she has come back down with you, in a borrowed coat over her uniform that fools nobody.

The scene: evening on the eve of the Centennial Assembly, in the Drowned Rat, a dockside dive halfway down the river stairs, packed because the docks shut tomorrow for the procession. The player is the King's investigator, with Sergeant Agnes Kell, off her patch. The clock is the eleventh bell, when the night work on the wharves starts and Hobb must go, whatever is happening.

## What the player may know already (the scenes before)
The granary's: if it was ended for the test (the pack says so), it was ended with its discoveries made, so the story so far can have Prout murdered (struck, strangled, hoisted up as a suicide), the King's Road wagons written off as "lost to goblins" and sold off, each countersigned **"J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor"**, with Prout having found out, and Big Hobb Mallory at the granary with the gentleman when Prout died. The player or Kell saying any of that here is no leak. Knowing the countersignature isn't knowing whose man Hobb is: putting Sallow's name to Hobb's master is this scene's discovery. A scene ended for the test moves nothing in the world, though, and only what the story owes goes on with whoever holds it: after a granary ended so, Prout's boots, with the page still sewn into the left one, stayed in the yard, and Cob comes down the stairs without them.

## What anyone can see from the start (fair game for narration)
- The river stairs at night: bunting on the quay above, the steps wet and green, the river slapping at the foot of them; the Rat dug into the bank on a landing halfway down, three steps down to its one door.
- Inside, one long, low, smoky room under eel traps and old oars, with a scullery at the back; a driftwood fire burning blue with salt; a bar made from an upturned skiff, with the original drowned rat in a jar of gin behind it; **the rules board** just inside the door (no credit, no blades out, no fighting indoors, no watch on duty, no singing before the ninth bell, what's said in the Rat stays in the Rat, gossip sold by the measure; by order, N. Quill; enforced by Manners); **Manners**, a blackthorn cudgel on two nails behind the bar, and the pewter measures beside it from a gill to a quart; the champion's chair by the fire.
- A full house of porters, lightermen and bargees, a great many of them big men and a good few bandaged somewhere (it's the docks). The din drops by half when Kell comes down the steps: the regulars make her for watch (it's the boots).
- By look, until someone says a name: a small square landlady of sixty behind the bar (Nan); an ancient bargewoman in three shawls dealing cards by the fire, with a clay pipe and a pile of coppers and new silver (Dilly); a young lighterman built like a water butt by the door, elbow on a table, a fresh black eye (Arlo); and in the fireside corner, back to the wall, **the biggest man in the room, his right hand out of sight under the table**, flinching when the door opens (Hobb; spoken to, he says "Master" and stops himself: his tell, fine to show). The player knows the name Big Hobb Mallory, not his face.
- Cob comes out of the scullery with his tray only once the newcomers have been in a while: a scrawny boy in a man's waistcoat and a sacking apron to his ankles, a yellowing bruise on one cheek; on his feet Prout's good black boots, three sizes too big and laced with string, or nothing much, as the granary left it. He keeps the bar between him and the fireside corner.
- The bells carry across the water; the night work on the wharves starts at the eleventh.

## Public knowledge (anyone in Caerwyn may know it: the lore marked public)
What any local knows, in the prep, is public, and so is what its setting shows of the place (its names wait until someone says them): the house rules and Nan's measures (a gill, a half, a pint, never a quart), the custom of the chair (three refusals in a night and it goes), Kell as the constable Nan put through the door, Hobb's wages to his mum in the Shambles, Cob's da barred, the heron pennies and the Heron's night loading, the eleventh bell and the ebb. Beyond the prep, the lore marked public lets anyone say:
- **The Rat** (Places/The Drowned Rat): the scullery's slop hatch over the river, just big enough for a boy; the jar marked FOREIGN under the bar, where Nan keeps the heron pennies; nobody else sits in the champion's chair, not even Nan; a watchman off duty may drink like anyone, if he pays; the four shillings and sixpence the watch still owes for the door.
- **The people, as the stairs know them** (Cast/…/public): **Hobb**, the size of a door, polite to a fault ("sir", "missus", "Mrs Quill"), never broke one of Nan's rules, plays at Dilly's table every night, and **lately drinks for two and keeps his right hand out of sight** (Arlo says he's gone soft; Dilly says he's sickening for something, and feeds him). **Nan**, "my duck" to everyone, no credit ever, who barred Nat Pike for hitting his boy. **Kell**, twenty-odd years a sergeant because her reports say what happened ("Kell's written it down"). **Arlo**, who has challenged Hobb more than anyone and lost every time, wagers on everything, calls everyone "champ", and says three Saltmere bargees gave him the eye. **Dilly**, "my lamb", whose Dabchick smells of onions, with two husbands lost to the river. **Cob**, a granary rat who throws things accurately, swears inventively and goes about with Moll; his father, Nat Pike, is a granary porter with a thirst. **The Crake brothers**, Ezra who talks and Enoch who doesn't, barred from the Rat (rule three, once each), with a price list Ezra recites to anyone, lately in work most nights for someone who pays full rate (where, they don't say).
- **The river stairs and the Heron** (Places/River Stairs, Places/Heron Warehouse): the Heron is the last warehouse at the downriver end of the wharves, a Saltmere grain factor's, new this season, under a painted heron; it pays double for night loading in heron pennies (they spend in Saltmere, not in town) and doesn't care for questions; its night gang come and go by the river door off the plank walk, and an old watchman with a mastiff keeps the land door; Hep Grist, its forewoman, takes on any strong pair of hands on a short night. The stairs are the river watch's patch. Tonight a Saltmere barge, **the Patience**, lies alongside the Heron and sails on the ebb at the second bell after midnight.
- **The town tonight** (World/Caerwyn now, evening and night on the eve): every dive on the stairs full by dusk; the whole watch, the river watch with it, lining the Assembly route through the night; the palace shut but for the delegations' dinner and the rehearsal (its gate takes written messages for the Marshal, and the King sees no one before morning); bread chalked up again and the stew thinner; fog off the river after the eleventh bell; the Heron's gang grumbling that they're on double pay and still can't buy bread. Whether the clerk went out on the dead-cart or was signed in as a murder is talked of as the granary left it.
- **The grain crisis and the Purveyor's office** (World/Grain Crisis, World/Royal Purveyor's Office): a dozen King's Road wagons "lost to goblins" this month, each written off in the Royal Granary Roll and countersigned by the Purveyor's office; Saltmere barges going downriver full. The wheat-ear is the office's mark (its sacks, wagon canvas, rings and purse silver), and its officers answer to the palace, not the watch.
- **Master Sallow, as the palace knows him** (Cast/Master Josiah Sallow/public): the under-purveyor for grain and flour, fifty, pink, in a fur collar, with **a silver-topped cane, a wheat-ear signet ring and a cloud of lavender water**; he signs "J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor", comes to the King's Granary with the Purveyor's wagons and has his own key to it. That he's the gentleman Hobb calls Master, or anything to do with Prout, isn't public.
- **The watch's law** (World/City Watch): anyone can be taken up on evidence (his own admission, a witness who'll swear, a wound that matches); a sergeant who sees a crime done may arrest whoever does it; Nan and the stairs say that in thirty years the watch has never once come down them for a gentleman.

## What's held (none of it narrated or said until it's legitimately uncovered)
The scene's truth, in the prep, says what happened. What can come out of it is the scene's five discoveries, each by its paths (who or what holds it, and what earns it, are in the prep):
1. **The big man from the loft** [the-loft]: Hobb held Prout's arms while his master struck and strangled him, then hauled him up on the sack hoist, and the rope took the skin off his right palm (that's what's under the rag). Held by: Hobb (his own words, the loft first, "I only held the rope", once something answers his fear, or on a roll; never where the whole Rat can hear); Cob (the big cove from the loft, by his size and his "Yes, Master", only to someone he trusts and out of Hobb's sight; he never says the name); Arlo (he saw the palm skinned raw in a stripe at the pump this evening: early for a wager, flattery or a sympathetic ear, otherwise at Hobb's third refusal, in front of everyone); Hobb's right hand (a glimpse when he forgets, or the rag coming loose at the arm-wrestling); Nan's pint (last night, with no loading on, he went out before the midnight bells, and he came in tonight with his right hand in a rag he won't let her look at).
2. **Who he calls Master** [the-master], required: Master Sallow of the Royal Purveyor's office, the J. Sallow who countersigned the "lost" wagons. Held by: Hobb (in a whisper, asked straight once he's talking; it comes last); his face at the name or the cane; Nan, who won't sell it ("That's a quart, my duck. I don't sell quarts.": a quart she won't sell is a name she has); Kell's noon sighting with the name on the delivery notices; Cob's Silvertop; Prout's page, if it's in hand.
3. **Where the grain goes: the Heron** [the-heron], required: Hobb's night work is at the Heron; tonight's a loading night with a Saltmere barge in, he's due on its hoist at the eleventh bell, and the gang get in at its river door; if the King's "lost" grain goes anywhere, it goes through there. The Heron's silver and its night loading are public; what's held is that it's Hobb's night work, tonight's bell and the way in. Held by: Nan's pint (fourpence, no dice); Dilly (he told her "at the Heron", the eleventh bell, and he'd sooner be dead: for someone who sits in and plays fair, never with Kell at their shoulder); the regulars' dock talk (Hep Grist, the Heron's forewoman, takes on any strong pair of hands on a short night); Hobb's own part of the night, and the brass heron token on the string round his neck, which he gives up if asked; Hobb at the door when he's stopped at the bell.
4. **What would turn him** [way-out]: he's more frightened for his mum than of the noose (the Master knows where she lives, over a tripe-dresser's in the Shambles); his mum kept out of the Master's reach tonight (Kell's spare chair for one night, the cabin aft on Dilly's Dabchick, a widow on the landing who owes Nan a kindness) would turn him, and so would a way out that isn't the rope: a man who'd hang alongside the guilty can sometimes swear for the Crown. Held by: Hobb ("He knows where my mum lives"); Nan (the bolt-holes, for a reason good for Hobb, not for money); Dilly (the cabin aft, as long as nobody in a uniform sets foot aboard); Kell (the law, and her chair, talked into).
5. **Prout's page, in Cob's boot** [prouts-page]: only while it's still sewn into the lining of Cob's left boot (the granary didn't turn it up, and the world has it there): razored from the Royal Granary Roll, a dozen "lost" wagons each countersigned "J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor", Prout's pencilled river-warehouse weights, and at the foot "Not goblins. Who pays the hedge-folk?" Cob doesn't know it's there; nobody alive does. Held by: Cob (in the scullery, out of Hobb's sight, for a kindness with nothing asked back and a polite ask, or a promise that answers his fear); Nan ("Cob. Here, my duck. Show them your boot.", for a reason plainly good for the boy); the left boot, which crackles when he stamps. If the page isn't there (after a granary ended for the test, it stayed in the yard with the boots), or the player already has it, there's nothing to find, and the GM not steering to it is right.

Held too, on the cards: the Master struck the clerk with the head of his cane and used the cord; at first light he paid a granary girl a silver crown to say she'd seen the clerk jump (Hobb, only if asked); the Master comes at the midnight bell to see the barge off (Hobb); **the porters say the Heron's grain is the King's own** (Nan's quart, never sold); Nan has put the hanged clerk and Hobb's hand together, and would sooner see him taken quietly up the stairs; Cob pinched one of Hobb's heron pennies off the pot tray; Dilly's six casks of Saltmere brandy under the onions on the Dabchick (she's sure the sergeant has come for them); Kell's secret, Prout's visit three days ago and her sending him to the palace, if the granary didn't bring it out.

## Who knows what (each card is the authority)
- **Hobb** owns to being Hobb Mallory, the champion who won't wrestle tonight, and to a night job for a gentleman that pays double in new silver (not what it is); he's due at work at the eleventh bell. The rest is his secrets, one thing per question, the Master's name last. He knows his own part of the night (the hoist, the river door, the token, the bell), not how the Heron works or what's in the clerk's book. He doesn't know Cob was in the loft, that Arlo saw his palm, that Nan has put it together, that Kell followed him, or that a man can swear for the Crown; he believes nobody else was in the loft.
- **Nan** sells by the measure what her card says: the gill (Hobb's the big lad in the corner, champion eleven years, drinking for two since last night); the half (he pays in heron pennies, and works nights for a gentleman); the pint (the Heron, the eleventh bell, tonight's loading, and last night's going out with no loading on). His mum in the Shambles is free, the bolt-holes for a good reason. She noticed Cob hiding in the scullery since Hobb came in, and the good boots; she doesn't know what's in them. The quart, the gentleman's name, she never sells or says in her house.
- **Kell** knows her noon sighting (the gentleman with the silver-topped cane, the wheat-ear ring and the lavender beside the Purveyor's flour wagons; the big dock-man nursing a bandaged right hand at his elbow, who called him "Master"; she followed him here); "J. Sallow, for the Royal Purveyor" as a signature on the Assembly delivery notices in the watch house, a name, not a face; the law (an arrest on real evidence, outside Nan's door; swearing for the Crown is the King's to grant); that the palace gate takes messages for the Marshal; and her room with a spare chair. She can't swear the gentleman at the wagons was Sallow.
- **Cob** knows the Rat is the one place his da can't come, and everybody knows Big Hobb (who once gave him the end of his pie). He knows Hobb is the big cove from the loft and tells it in pieces, to someone he trusts, out of Hobb's sight. He doesn't know Silvertop's real name, or that the page is in his boot.
- **Arlo** knows Hobb has never refused a match until tonight and keeps his right hand under the table; that Hobb's been flush with new silver, working nights for a gent somewhere downriver; the house custom. His secret is the palm at the pump; told what a rope burn means, he's horrified and loud.
- **Dilly** knows Hobb has been playing to lose since last night, every penny a heron; that he told her he's due "at the Heron" at the eleventh bell and would sooner be dead; that the cabin aft has hidden strays. She guesses smuggling, and says it as a guess. She doesn't know what he did.

## How the GM is supposed to disclose
- People know only what they've seen, been there for, or been told in their hearing. Nobody asks about, names or guesses at something they haven't heard of.
- Narration shows only what the player character can see or hear; it never reveals what's hidden, not even to say it is still hidden ("the page stays in the boot").
- Reveals land in steps, one new thing per reply, only as far as the player's question reaches: Hobb's story comes a thing per question, never in one breath, and not where the whole Rat can hear.
- People steer the player through what they want and fear (Nan's rule four, Arlo's challenge, the bell, Hobb's look at the door, Cob's jerk of the head from behind something): that's how the scene hints. Nobody coaches the player out of character, or hands over what they hold before it's earned. Kell lets the investigator lead: she points at things in a word and writes them down, and never says what to do next.
- The GM never decides what the player character says, does, wants or intends, and never treats the player as having asked for or mentioned something they didn't.
- Coin: Nan's gossip is sold for it, by the measure and never short (a pint about Hobb's night work is fourpence, no dice), but never the quart, and coin doesn't buy a look in a child's boot; with Hobb it buys nothing (he's trying to lose his); coin or stew offered to Cob as a price buys boasts and haggling, never the boot or what he saw.
